# Particularly Significant

The membership lists of the [[Propaganda Due]] lodge seized at Castiglion Fibocchi in March 1981 were published by the Italian government on May 21, 1981. Among the names is "Dott. Alberto Ferrari."[^1] The parliamentary commission of inquiry on P2, chaired by [[Tina Anselmi]], counted sixty-seven members drawn from the Treasury, the banks and finance, and singled out a few banks where the presences were "particolarmente significative per qualità e rappresentatività." The first it named was the [[Banca Nazionale del Lavoro]]: four members of the board of directors, the director general, and three central directors, one of them secretary of the board.[^2]

[[Luigi Sardelli]], who ran BNL's North American operations from 1987, later answered written questions from [[Henry B. Gonzalez]]. He wrote that Professor A. Ferrari, then BNL's managing director, "had to resign in shame in 1981 because of his affiliation with Lodge P2." He wrote that on December 25, 1975 Ferrari had asked him to go to the Gulf to explore a BNL merchant bank "with a group of BNL friends," and that the people he met there were Pakistani nationals connected to [[Bank of Credit and Commerce International|BCCI]]. He wrote that Ferrari was a close friend of [[Roberto Calvi]] of [[Banco Ambrosiano]], and that the bank's relationship with BCCI, [[Ghaith Pharaon]] and [[Marc Rich]] was the exclusive domain of Ferrari, A. Florio and A. Conzara. Ferrari had handpicked Florio to head the International Division.[^3]

The FBI searched BNL's [[Atlanta]] branch on August 4, 1989. The indictment returned on February 28, 1991 charged the branch manager [[Christopher Drogoul]], two other branch officers, a Turkish trading company and its executive, the Iraqi state [[Rafidain Bank]] and four Iraqi officials.[^4] Angelo Florio was one of seven Italian nationals who in 1993 declared they would not come to the United States to testify. Drogoul named him, with [[Giacomo Pedde]] and Teodoro Monaco, as the officials who gave the authorizations he relied on.[^5]

The Italian Senate commission of inquiry under Senator [[Gianuario Carta]] approved its preliminary report on December 21, 1991. It recorded that no official of BNL in New York or Rome was among the defendants. It found that the thesis of a fraud by a group of unfaithful officials had proved inconsistent, and that the Atlanta operations could not have been unknown in Rome and New York and were instead "tollerata se non incoraggiata." It also recorded that the [[Department of Justice]] had denied the commission access to documents and witnesses, citing grand jury secrecy and national security.[^6] On October 5, 1992 Judge [[Marvin Shoob]] wrote: "The Court concludes that officials at BNL-Rome were aware of and approved Mr. Drogoul's activities."[^7]
