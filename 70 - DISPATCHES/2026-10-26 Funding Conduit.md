# Funding Conduit

On February 25, 1967 the San Francisco Chronicle reported that "a charitable trust established by John Hay Whitney received $325,000 in 1964 and 1965 from the Granary Fund of Boston, used by the Central Intelligence Agency to channel money from front organizations to various organizations here and abroad." The schedule the [[Granary Fund]] filed with the Massachusetts attorney general showed $225,000 paid to the John Hay Whitney Trust for Charitable Purposes in 1964 and $100,000 in 1965. Walter N. Thayer, head of the Whitney communications corporation, issued a statement in Whitney's name: "The trust which I established Dec. 29, 1958, for charitable purposes has operated since that date."[^1] David Wise and Thomas B. Ross wrote the same year that the trust was "financed in part by the Granary Fund, the CIA conduit."[^2]

In November 1968 [[Charles Katek]], Chief of the CIA's Central Cover Staff, sent the Deputy Director for Plans a memorandum on the "Scope and Possible Damage of New Allegations by Ramparts Magazine." [[Ramparts]] had sent the Internal Revenue Service a list of 145 tax-exempt organizations it suspected of CIA funding. The memorandum's annex of terminated covert funding conduits lists the Granary Fund as a Central Cover Staff proprietary (LPSIPHON/7). It lists "Whitney Trust for Charitable Purposes, John Hay" twice, as Ramparts items 141 and 142, each time as a "Funding conduit under Project QKOPERA (LPCORSET/68)." The same memorandum gives QKOPERA as the project designation for the [[Congress for Cultural Freedom]], and lists the [[Farfield Foundation]] as a "Funding conduit for CCF."[^3] In 1985 the CIA answered a 1980 request for records on "the John Hay Whitney Trust for Charitable Purposes and the Granary Fund." It reported two documents, dated December 10, 1968 and January 23, 1969, and withheld both in full.[^4]

When [[Forum World Features]] began selling articles from [[London]] in 1966, its owner of record was Whitney, according to officials quoted by the Washington Post in 1975. An internal CIA memorandum of about 1968, which the Post quoted from Time Out, says the service was begun in January 1966 from the residue of Forum Service, an activity of the Congress for Cultural Freedom. [[Richard Mellon Scaife]] replaced Whitney early in 1973, and a Whitney spokesman said he had "no comment on the subject."[^5] [[Brian Crozier]], the service's president, later wrote that in the spring of 1965 [[John Hunt]] told him Whitney had agreed to take over the service, and printed a letter of June 10, 1965 from Hunt saying that Whitney "tentatively plans to put about $100,000 a year into Forum Features."[^6]

[[William Harding Jackson]] was managing director of J. H. Whitney & Co. when he wrote to Director of Central Intelligence [[Roscoe H. Hillenkoetter]] on the firm's letterhead on May 29, 1947: "You need only call upon me and I shall do anything within my power to be of assistance."[^7] He became Deputy Director of Central Intelligence in October 1950.[^8] The CIA's history of the [[Walter Bedell Smith]] years names [[Alexander Standish]], "a partner in J. H. Whitney & Company," among the original Princeton Consultants of the [[Board of National Estimates]].[^9] On March 26, 1969 [[Henry A. Kissinger]] wrote to Peter Flanigan about Whitney's interest in the [[Foreign Intelligence Advisory Board]]: "You should know that this has twice been raised with the President and rejected both times."[^10]
