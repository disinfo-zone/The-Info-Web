# The Transmittal

In March 1992 the House task force on the October Surprise hired [[E. Lawrence Barcella, Jr.]] as its chief counsel; its report of January 1993 found "no credible evidence" of an October Surprise deal.[^6] On May 29, 1992 the *Hartford Courant* reported that in the summer of 1985, while an assistant United States attorney, Barcella had given a legal opinion clearing a private $5 million shipment of Soviet-bloc arms to the Nicaraguan contras, arranged by [[John Singlaub]] and [[Barbara Studley]] of [[GeoMiliTech Consultants Corp.]] The Courant quoted Studley's 1991 deposition: asked who in the Justice Department approved the shipment, she answered, "Larry Barcella signed it." Barcella told the paper that "somebody in the government came to me," that he checked with the State Department's Office of Munitions Control and the Justice Department's internal security office, and that he "orally passed that back to the person that had made the request of me." The United States attorney, [[Joseph diGenova]], first said, "Larry would never have done that," and a week later said, "Larry was sought out by a lot of people in intelligence."[^1]

The congressional Iran-Contra record holds one letter from Studley's lawyer. On April 28, 1987 [[Frank Koszorus Jr.]], on the letterhead of Finley, Kumble in Washington, wrote to the House committee's assistant counsel [[Kenneth Ballen]]: "During your telephone conference with Paul L. Perito this afternoon, you requested a copy of the memorandum from Barbara F. Studley's file regarding Oliver North. Please find enclosed a copy of that memorandum." The letter says nothing of a shipment or an opinion.[^2] In 1992 Koszorus told the Courant he was "not at all certain that she was ever issued a written opinion."[^1]

The Courant reported that Barcella had gone into private practice in June 1986 "initially at the same firm where Koszorus, Studley's lawyer, worked," without naming it.[^1] Thirty-one partners of Finley, Kumble's Washington office formed [[Laxalt, Washington, Perito & Dubuc]] in January 1988. Koszorus was described as a partner there in April 1989, and Barcella was described as a partner there when he left in July 1991, the month a Paul Perito, a name partner, moved to [[Paul Hastings|Paul, Hastings, Janofsky & Walker]].[^3][^4] Whether that Paul Perito is the Paul L. Perito of the 1987 letter is not established in the records found.

On March 1, 1993 Paul, Hastings registered under the Foreign Agents Registration Act for the Embassy of the Republic of [[Hungary]], at $8,334 a month, with Koszorus signing as attorney. The firm's supplemental statement, sworn by Koszorus, lists Barcella as having become a partner on July 18, 1994.[^5]
