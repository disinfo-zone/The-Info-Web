# Neither Cleared Nor Witting

[[Klara G. Roman]], a [[Budapest]]-born graphologist and clinical psychologist, taught graphology at the [[New School for Social Research]] in [[New York City]] from 1948. From 1959 she worked on a lexicon of handwriting.[^1][^2]

A [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]] memorandum of August 24, 1961 opened [[Project MKUltra|MKULTRA]] Subproject 137 "to support the work of Dr." [redacted] "in completing a psycholexicon on handwriting." It states that she "has an international reputation for her work in graphology and speech," that "she is advanced in years and her health is failing," and that the manuscript was "now eighty percent complete." The memorandum cites the Technical Services Division's "general requirement to further the Agency's capability for the indirect assessment of personality," states that the project "will be funded through the" [redacted] "for security and cover purposes," and records that the investigator was "neither cleared nor witting of Agency sponsorship." The curriculum vitae attached to the proposal lists a 1928 diploma, the invention of the Graphodyne for measuring writing speed and pressure, a 1948 research appointment in conjunction with an "Institute for Psychoanalysis," and a faculty post from 1949; each entry matches Roman's record.[^3][^1]

A treasurer's check for $2,000 was issued on September 11, 1961. Roman died on August 9, 1962.[^3][^2] An accounting memorandum of February 1963 states that she "died in 1962 and did not send in an accounting," that the file held "a letter from her brother stating that the manuscript is almost finished," and that "The Fund will receive its usual acknowledgement in the publication of the manuscript." The closing certification states that the recipient "died in 1962 without rendering an accounting" and that "a manuscript has been published and value received." The accounting form was approved on March 10, 1964 over the signature of [[Sidney Gottlieb]], "DC/TSD."[^3]

The Encyclopedia of the Written Word was published by [[F. Ungar Publishing Company]] in 1968. Its acknowledgments thank "The Handwriting Institute and The Human Ecology Fund," and Dr. Walter P. Pasternak "of the Human Ecology Fund."[^4][^2] Pasternak appears as technical writer in the 1957 annual report of the [[Society for the Investigation of Human Ecology]], renamed the Human Ecology Fund in 1961.[^5] The Fund's 1963 report, as tabulated by the anthropologist [[David H. Price]], lists $2,000 to "Roman, Klara G." for "Book: The Psychology of Writing."[^6]

The records of New School president [[John R. Everett]] hold a file labeled "Central Intelligence Agency (CIA), 1977-1978." Its description refers to "two projects conducted at the New School in 1962 that received funding through MKUltra," the second of which "concerned graphology and psychopathologies."[^7]
