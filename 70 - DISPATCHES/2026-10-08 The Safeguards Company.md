# The Safeguards Company

The Nuclear Surveillance and Auditing Corporation, established in 1968 to audit fuel fabrication and reprocessing, was headed by [[Ralph F. Lumb]], who had chaired the [[Atomic Energy Commission]]'s ad hoc committee on safeguarding special nuclear materials. Renamed NUSAC in 1975, it hired a former head of security at the commission's Savannah River operations office, and in 1979 a computer security manager from the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]]. By 1980 its security divisions listed the [[Nuclear Regulatory Commission]], the [[Department of Energy]] and the [[United States Navy]] as clients.[^1]

On December 28, 1978 the [[Wackenhut Corporation]] bought NUSAC's assets, and a Florida subsidiary was filed the next day at Wackenhut's Coral Gables headquarters. "NUSAC is and will remain an independent firm," [[George R. Wackenhut]] was quoted as saying. Its board in late 1979 was George Wackenhut as chairman, Lumb, [[Robert E. Miller]] and Dr. [[Myron S. Malkin]], then director of the [[National Aeronautics and Space Administration|NASA]] Space Shuttle program.[^1][^2]

In April 1981 [[Robert Frye]], president of Wackenhut Services, Inc., and Tribal Chairman [[Arthur Welmas]] signed the joint venture between Wackenhut and the [[Cabazon Indian Reservation|Cabazon]] band.[^3] A letter over Welmas's name, headed "CABAZON INDIAN SECURITY /WSI JOINT VENTURE," Indio, describes a planned "new assault weapon, which would have unique characteristics, including an adjustable cycle rate of fire and a laser sighting system." The names that follow the signature are Peter Zokosky, Bob Frye, Dr. Myron Malkin, Colonel Glade Flake, John James, Bruce James, John Paul Nichols and Wayne Reeder. Whether the Dr. Myron Malkin of the letter is the NUSAC director is not established in the records.[^4]

On May 8, 1984 NUSAC, Incorporated became Wackenhut Advanced Technologies Corporation, at [[Reston, Virginia]], absorbing three other Wackenhut security divisions. Its president was [[Ralph E. Caudle]], a retired Air Force colonel who since 1981 had headed the Department of Energy Office of Safeguards and Security, responsible for the protection of nuclear weapons materials and facilities and all of the department's classified information.[^2][^5] In 1985 the new company was selected to draw up the conceptual design of the security system for the nuclear device assembly facility at the [[Nevada Test Site]], and in 1986 to engineer it.[^6]

From September 1987 its vice president for national security studies was [[James B. Motley]], a retired Army colonel, whose resume appears in the June 1990 packet of [[Counter Terrorism Consultants, Inc.]][^7] Renamed the Wackenhut Applied Technologies Center in January 1990 and moved to Fairfax, it had by then a Defense Systems Analysis Group whose clients included the Army Survivability Management Office, and whose staff, the parent's annual report states, "apply their skills and background in military operations, requirements and intelligence to support the Department of Defense."[^2][^8] Wackenhut's annual reports describing this work are among the papers of [[Danny Casolaro]].[^6][^8]

In March 1993 the report of Special Counsel [[Nicholas J. Bua]] on the [[INSLAW]] matter stated that the Wackenhut-Cabazon joint venture had no employees and that "the only individual employed by Wackenhut was Frye."[^3] Wackenhut's annual report for 1994 states that the Applied Technologies Center was sold or discontinued in 1993. The State of Florida dissolved the corporation on August 26, 1994.[^2][^9]
