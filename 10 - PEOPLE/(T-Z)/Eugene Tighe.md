Eugene F. Tighe Jr. was born in 1921 in New Raymer, [[Colorado]], grew up in [[Los Angeles]], enlisted in the Army in September 1942, was commissioned from the artillery officer candidate school at Camp Columbia, Australia, in 1944 and served as an anti-aircraft adviser with the 43rd Bombardment Group in New Guinea, the Netherlands East Indies, the Philippines and the Ryukyus. He graduated from Loyola University, Los Angeles, in 1949 and entered the [[United States Air Force|Air Force]] in August 1950 as an intelligence officer, serving in Korea, at Carswell, with the 497th Reconnaissance Technical Squadron in Germany, and from 1960 to 1965 as chief of the research center of the 544th Aerospace Reconnaissance Technical Wing at Offutt. After the Air War College he was director of targets at Seventh Air Force in Vietnam, then at Air Force headquarters director of estimates and of intelligence applications, and from 1972 director for intelligence at [[United States Pacific Command|Pacific Command]]. He was deputy director of the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]] from September 1974 to August 1976, acting director from December 1975 to May 1976, assistant chief of staff for intelligence of the Air Force from January 1977, and, appointed by the president in June 1977, director of DIA from September 1977 to August 1981, promoted lieutenant general on September 1, 1977. He retired on September 1, 1981, and died on January 29, 1994.[^1][^2]

### Grill Flame

Under Tighe DIA carried out the 1978-79 reorganization that "established five major directorates," and his deputy for scientific and technical intelligence, [[Jack Vorona]], chaired the Defense Department's committee on the Army's remote-viewing program. An Army memorandum of July 10, 1978, records "the 23 June meeting with VORONA and LTG TIGHE," "at which time VORONA said that Congressman [[Charlie Rose|ROSE]] has agreed to sign some sort of letter that DOD could pull out and use in a contigency situation," and that "LTG TIGHE is not fully supportive, or at least enthusiastic, about the military jumping in on Parapsychology R&D"; Vorona sat on the Grill Flame steering committee at its first meeting in February 1979 "standing in for LTG Tighe." When the [[Grill Flame Scientific Evaluation Committee|Gale Committee]] briefed the working group on January 10, 1980, "it was suggested that General Tighe contact Dr. Laberge to obtain copies of the report and to stop any further distribution," and Major [[William Stoner]] recorded that "Dr Vorona sent a memo to LTG Tighe (11 Jan). It urged the Director to intercede with Dr LaBerge on the report"; Lieutenant Colonel [[Murray Watt]] wrote his comments the next month for "Dr Vorona's letter to LTG Tighe," whose "tone... will be rather general." An undated note from Major General [[Edmund Thompson]] asked him to meet as one of the Grill Flame steering committee's "principals."[^3]

### After DIA

The Senate Select Committee on POW/MIA Affairs reported in 1993 that DIA's handling of live-sighting reports had been reviewed in "Task Force reports conducted in 1986 by Gen. Eugene Tighe (USAF-Ret.) and Col. [[Kimball Gaines]] (USAF-Ret.)," among reviews that found the agency's POW/MIA office "defensive toward criticism" and "slow to follow-up on live-sighting and other reports," with "a 'mindset to debunk.'"[^4]
