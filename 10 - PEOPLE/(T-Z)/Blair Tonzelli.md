Blair Tonzelli is a [[Philadelphia]] woman who was last heard from in August 2022 and was reported missing in February 2023, at 35. On June 19, 2026, a woman riding with [[Eugene Horsch]] in a car stopped by a federal park ranger beside [[Independence National Historical Park]] was carrying an identification card in Tonzelli's name; police said the woman was not Tonzelli. The stop led to the search of the Horsch house on West Chew Avenue in [[Olney, Philadelphia|Olney]] and to the [[Olney House Investigation]].[^1][^2]

### Life

She attended [[Archbishop Ryan High School]], according to her Facebook page. She had a son at 18, who was adopted by other relatives and last saw her when he was five. A friend, David McCarty, who lived with her on Wensley Street in [[Kensington, Philadelphia|Kensington]], said that her last Facebook post was on August 17, 2022, and that he heard nothing from her after that month. "Even in the throes of her addiction," the *[[The Philadelphia Inquirer|Philadelphia Inquirer]]* reported, she was "fiercely loyal," and she once threw herself in front of a tow truck to stop it taking McCarty's car. Friends reported her missing in February 2023, telling police she had last been seen at the Horsch house the previous summer.[^1][^3]

### The Horsch House

[[R.C. Horsch]] met her in Kensington when he was in his late seventies and she was living at a drug house; he later said she had worked as a home health aide before she went missing in August 2022. A witness told police that she had been an aide in the house and believed that she and Eugene Horsch had a disagreement over money, according to law enforcement sources cited by the *Inquirer*. From about 2020 until at least September 2023, seven months after the missing-person report, a nude photograph of her with what appeared to be a black eye and blood on her lip was among the lead images on Horsch's website. NBC10 reported that during the investigation detectives learned that Eugene Horsch "allegedly made a statement suggesting Tonzelli would 'never be seen again,' according to court documents."[^1][^2][^4]

Her son, Nicholas Nagle, said in September 2026 that she had been entered in the [[National Missing and Unidentified Persons System]] only after her identification card was found: "Imagine if it was a completely other woman that he made the fake ID in the name of. There could be 30 more women missing." "She had a family, she had loved ones and so did all these other women," he said. The *Inquirer* reported that her DNA was not found on the items tested at the house.[^3][^5]
