Leonid Vasiliev was a [[Soviet Union|Soviet]] parapsychologist and chairman of the physiology department at the University of Leningrad, a past winner of the Lenin Prize (as Ostrander and Schroeder and Schnabel give it; the 1975 DIA study gives the Order of Lenin) and a key figure in the resurgence of Soviet [[Parapsychology|psi]] research following the publication of [[The Nautilus (Telepathy Project)|"The Secret of the Nautilus"]] article in 1960. He argued that "the discovery of the energy underlying ESP will be equivalent to the discovery of atomic energy."[^1]

He stated in April 1960 that Soviet scientists had conducted extensive, unreported investigations into telepathy under the Stalin regime, predating American efforts, and that they had successfully performed telepathy tests over a quarter-century before the alleged U.S. Nautilus tests. He held that the publication of articles like [[Jacques Bergier]]'s "Thought Transfer, Weapon of War" and "The Secret of the Nautilus" in French journals stimulated Soviet research, and used them to advocate for increased Soviet investment in the field: "Today the American Navy is testing telepathy on their atomic submarines. Soviet scientists conducted a great many successful telepathy tests over a quarter of a century ago. It's urgent that we throw off our prejudices. We must plunge into the exploration of this vital field."[^1][^2]

Ostrander and Schroeder date that address to April 1960, at a gathering to commemorate the discovery of radio; [[I. V. Tsykunov]] suggests May, when Soviet Radio Day fell, and the translated Znanie-Sila report of [[Gleb Anfilov]] places a Vasiliev lecture at the Leningrad Scientists Club on June 15, 1960 in which he mentioned the American attempts at telepathic communication with the Nautilus.[^3][^4][^5]

Tsykunov reports a seven-page memorandum of March 21, 1960 drawn up by Vasiliev, [[P. I. Gulyaev]] and [[Igor Poletaev|I. A. Poletaev]], attached to a report that Poletaev sent to Defense Minister Malinovsky on March 26, 1960. As Tsykunov quotes it, the report says Vasiliev had told Poletaev in March 1960 that the American armed forces had adopted telepathy for communication with submarines, that two French magazines had published brief reports, and that a test with the Nautilus submerged under polar ice about 2,000 kilometers from its base had produced 70 percent correct symbols; it says that without discussing the reliability of the reports the danger of a new psychological weapon was too great to leave them without attention, and it proposes that Vasiliev and Gulyaev continue telepathy research.[^4]

Raphael L. Khérumian of the Institut Métapsychique International sent Vasiliev clippings of the Science et Vie and Constellation articles in late 1959 and early 1960. In his 1962 book Vasiliev acknowledged receiving them and, in a footnote, said such reports must be treated with caution, having been denied in Washington and by other American sources; [[Martin Ebon]] quotes the book as calling the experiment a "totally unexpected foreign confirmation" of tests Vasiliev had conducted twenty-five years earlier.[^6][^7]

By the early 1960s Vasiliev was heading a well-funded parapsychology laboratory, and his work soon concentrated on "remote-influencing" experiments, such as attempting to telepathically induce sleep in a distant receiver. In a 1963 Kremlin edict, the Soviet minister of defense, [[Rodion Malinovsky]], declared [[Telepathy]] to be science- and technology-based, leading to the creation of the Special Laboratory for Biocommunications Phenomena at the University of Leningrad, with Vasiliev in charge; its goals included establishing "scientific proof of telepathic communications" and identifying the nature of the brain energy that produces it. His efforts, along with those of other Soviet researchers like [[I. M. Kogan]], contributed to the establishment of numerous other psi research centers in the Soviet Union, and to the Soviet nomenclature around ESP being rewritten to sound technical, severing ties with its occult past: mental telepathy became "long-distance biological signal transmission," and [[Psychokinesis|psychokinesis]] "non-ionizing, in particular electromagnetic, emissions from humans."[^1][^2]

Tsykunov writes that the laboratory was created at the physiology institute of Leningrad University in 1960 and that a "biotelecommunication" group in Vasiliev's laboratory of electromagnetic fields and aeroions included P. I. Gulyaev, V. K. Pavlenko, A. I. Pudovkin, V. P. Leutin, V. A. Doroshenko and A. S. Efremov.[^4]

A [[Defense Intelligence Agency|DIA]] briefing on "Soviet Activities in Psychoenergetics," given to the Air Force in July 1987, opened its timeline with [[Vladimir Bekhterev|V. M. Bekhterev]] and "L. L. Vasil'yev (1919-)," under the headings "hypnosis," "automatic responses" and "long distance (1700 km)," and dated his "open publications" and "new lab, Leningrad Univ." to 1959-1965, with [[Genady Sergeyev|G. A. Sergeyev]] working in "Vasil'yev's lab" from 1965 to 1970.[^8]
