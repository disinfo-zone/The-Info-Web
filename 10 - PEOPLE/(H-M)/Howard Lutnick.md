Howard Lutnick is an American financier who has led the brokerage and investment bank [[Cantor Fitzgerald]] since 1991 and who became [[United States Department of Commerce|Secretary of Commerce]] in the second administration of [[Donald Trump]] in February 2025.[^1][^2] For more than a decade he lived next door to [[Jeffrey Epstein]] on East 71st Street in [[Manhattan]], in a house that Epstein had sold in 1996 to an entity that resold it to Lutnick two years later. In October 2025 Lutnick said he had cut ties with Epstein in 2005; documents released by the [[Department of Justice|Justice Department]] in early 2026 showed correspondence through 2018, a family visit to [[Little St. James]] in December 2012, and a joint investment in an advertising-technology company under a purchase agreement dated five days after that visit.[^1][^3]

### Cantor Fitzgerald and the Commerce Department

Lutnick became president and chief executive of Cantor Fitzgerald in 1991 and chairman in 1996.[^1] Prospectuses of Cantor's special-purpose acquisition companies state that he has been its president and chief executive officer since 1992 and chairman since 1996.[^4] The firm's venture arm, Cantor Ventures, is its venture-capital subsidiary.[^1] Trump nominated Lutnick as Secretary of Commerce after the 2024 election, and he took office in February 2025, with responsibility for trade policy, export controls, and the department's technology-standards bodies, including the [[National Institute of Standards and Technology]] and the [[U.S. AI Safety Institute]].[^2]

### The House on East 71st Street

In 1996 Epstein sold the property at 11 East 71st Street to an entity called Comet Trust. In 1998 Comet Trust sold it to Lutnick, and it became his primary residence, adjoining Epstein's own mansion on the same block.[^1] The houses stand near the [[Frick Collection]]. In 2018 Lutnick wrote to Epstein about the museum's planned expansion, warning that it might "block your sunlight and views," and pressed him: "You should put in a letter. I'm sending a lawyer. Don't ignore this."[^1]

### The 2005 Account

In October 2025 Lutnick told the [[New York Post]] that he and his wife, [[Allison Lutnick|Allison]], had cut ties with Epstein in 2005 after touring Epstein's townhouse, and said, "I will never be in the room with that disgusting person ever again."[^1]

In the October 2025 interview on "Pod Force One with Miranda Devine," quoted by NBC News on February 10, 2026, Lutnick said, "I was never in the room with him socially, for business, or even philanthropy."[^5]

### The Correspondence After 2005

The released emails show Lutnick and Epstein arranging calls and planning drinks in 2011.[^1] On April 4, 2011, Epstein's assistant [[Lesley Groff]] told him, "Howard Lutnick returned your call." On April 27 she wrote that Lutnick "will come see you at 5pm on Sunday May 1st," and Epstein's calendar for May 1, 2011 reads "5:00 Drinks w/Howard Lutnick."[^6] On November 20, 2012, Groff wrote to Lutnick: "Jeffrey Epstein understands you will be down in [[St. Thomas]] some over the holidays," passing on Epstein's telephone numbers and naming as contacts the staff of his St. Thomas office, "FTC," the [[Financial Trust Company]]. Calendar alerts of December 20 and 21, 2012 read "can Howard Lutnick do lunch Sat or Sun on JE's island?" and "can Howard Lutnick do lunch on Sat. 22nd or Sun. 23rd?"[^7] In December 2012 Lutnick, his wife, and their four children planned a visit to Little St. James, and Lutnick was invited to lunch there on December 23, 2012;[^8] afterward Epstein's assistant wrote to Lutnick on Epstein's behalf, "it was nice seeing you."[^1]

The earliest dated item in the released files that concerns a request to reach Epstein on Lutnick's behalf is an email of Monday, October 19, 2009, 6:50:26 p.m., sent to Epstein under the subject line "HOward Lutnick," with the sender's name redacted. It opens "The following is from" followed by an individual's name, and the quoted message reads: "HOward Lutnick would like to speak to you...one of his employees asked me if Howard could have your number so he may call you. Would you like me to give him your number? They did not give me Howard's number...." The email does not say who the employee was. At the House interview the email was Majority Exhibit 5; the questioner described it as "a message passed along to Mr. Epstein from" the named individual "about you wanting to speak to Mr. Epstein," and Lutnick said of that individual: "He was someone who worked for me at Cantor Fitzgerald. So on my staff." Asked whether he recalled why he would have tried to contact Epstein, he said: "I don't have any recollection," and added: "I shouldn't speculate. I don't know. And I did not speak to him" (pp. 31-32).[^9][^10]

In 2011 the sequence continued past the April 4 entries. On Tuesday, April 5, 2011, at 1:58 p.m., Lesley Groff wrote to Epstein under the subject "reminder Howard Lutnick": "Reminder you want to speak to Howard Lutnick today." On Tuesday, April 26, 2011, at 10:11 a.m., Epstein wrote to Groff: "[...] also on sunday if posible. . try to schedule, also howard lutnik." The next day, April 27, Groff wrote "Howard Lutnick will come see you at 5pm on Sunday May 1st," cited above. On Friday, October 28, 2011, at 2:52 p.m., Groff wrote to Epstein under the subject "Howard Lutnick": "FYI- Howard Lutnick's assistant called asking if you would be available to speak with Howard today..." The documents do not record whether a call took place on October 28.[^11]

In the opening statement of the House Oversight interview of May 6, 2026, Lutnick said: "My second interaction occurred 6 years later, in 2011, when his staff initiated contact with my office, suggesting he had a reason to get in touch with me. Our offices attempted to connect us by phone over the course of several weeks but were unable to do so." He continued that his staff "arranged for me to ring his bell, during my and my wife's typical Sunday walk around the block with our dogs, to hear what he had to say" (pp. 9-10). Asked whether he ever had telephone calls with Epstein, he answered: "I do not recall ever having a phone call with Mr. Epstein" (p. 18). Shown the April 4, 2011 email reading "Howard Lutnick returned your call," he said: "my office must have suggested I call him back to hear what he had to say, and we were unable to connect by phone" (p. 32).[^10]

Adfin was an advertising-technology company since shut down, whose Series A purchase agreement is dated December 28, 2012, five days after the lunch. Epstein signed for [[Southern Trust Company]], Inc., the [[U.S. Virgin Islands]] entity through which he conducted much of his business; Lutnick signed for an LLC named CVAFH I. Their signatures appear on neighboring pages, and the document lists nine shareholders.[^1][^12] A person close to Lutnick told CBS News that "Cantor was a small minority investor in Adfin" and that Lutnick "would not have any knowledge of who the other investors were."[^1] Adfin correspondence continued into 2014, when the shareholder David Mitchell wrote to Epstein about further fundraising involving Cantor Ventures.[^1]

On January 8, 2013, eleven days after the date of the Adfin purchase agreement, Epstein had his assistant forward Lutnick a document concerning casino legislation in the U.S. Virgin Islands, where Epstein kept his island and a range of business interests. A Lutnick spokesperson said he "ignored the document sent to him."[^1]

### Adfin: The July 2013 Documents

The Series A Preferred Stock Purchase Agreement of [[AdFin Solutions]], Inc. is dated December 28, 2012. Its initial closing that day involved two purchasers, David Mitchell and Jonathan Leitersdorf. The Schedule of Purchasers lists Southern Trust Company, Inc. for 1,142,857 shares on April 10, 2013, and, on July 23, 2013, "CVAFH I LLC" for 800,000 shares at $350,000 and Southern Trust Company, Inc. for a further 285,714 shares. The counterpart signature page for Southern Trust Company, Inc. reads "Name: Jeffrey Epstein," "Title: President." The counterpart signature page for CVAFH I LLC reads "Name: Howard W. Lutnick," "Title: President," with a handwritten marginal line, "[Signature Page to $350,000 Series A Preferred Stock Investment in AdFin Solutions, Inc.]." Counterpart pages of the same form for both entities appear in the Investors' Rights Agreement, the Right of Refusal and Co-Sale Agreement and the Voting Agreement, and a July 2013 letter agreement on [[Cantor Ventures]], L.P. letterhead, with a CVAFH I LLC signature block titled "President," states: "Cantor has been formed for the specific purpose of acquiring the Shares and the ultimate holding company of Cantor is Cantor Fitzgerald, L.P., a Delaware limited partnership."[^13][^14]

At the House Oversight interview of May 6, 2026, a questioner described a stock purchase agreement "that you signed on behalf of CVAFH and Mr. Epstein signed." Lutnick was asked whether he knew Epstein was an investor: "No." He said he learned it "when these documents were released." Lutnick's counsel stated that the documents show that "when Cantor Fitzgerald Venture Capital signed these documents, it was ... much later ... in 2013 in the spring," and Lutnick said: "I don't think I heard about this investment till 2013, May. I think the pitch was made to me in May of 2013, from the document. So it wasn't in December." Asked whether it is common for another investor to hold a signature page from a different investor, he answered: "No. I found it inexplicable and unsettling that he had ... that it was released, that my company's signature page would be in someone else's files."[^15]

### The 2020 FBI Complaint

### Senate and House Questioning

At a Senate Appropriations subcommittee hearing on February 10, 2026, Lutnick acknowledged the 2012 island visit, saying, "My wife was with me, as were my four children and nannies," and that after first meeting Epstein as a neighbor, "Over the next 14 years, I met him two other times that I can recall."[^3] Senator [[Chris Van Hollen]], the subcommittee's ranking member, told him that "the issue is not that you engaged in any wrongdoing in connection with Jeffrey Epstein, but that you totally misrepresented the extent of your relationship with him to the Congress, to the American people, and to the survivors of his despicable criminal and predatory acts."[^3] Senator [[Jeff Merkley]] listed eight interactions in the files after 2005, including a possible telephone call and an effort to schedule drinks, and said the planned family trip "sounds like somebody you know well enough to call up."[^3] Senator [[Jacky Rosen]] called the same day for his resignation, saying he had "clearly misled the American people."[^52] Representative [[Robert Garcia]], ranking member of the [[House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform|House Oversight Committee]], said: "He said he had no interactions with Epstein after 2005, yet we now know they were in business together."[^1]

On October 3, 2013 Richard Kahn sent Epstein an email with a PDF attachment; the body text begins "Attached is resume of Lutnick nanny." At the House interview, a questioner said that "a nanny that was in your employ had sent her resume to Mr. Epstein. Do you know anything about that?" Lutnick answered: "I have no knowledge about it at all." Asked to confirm that he "never encouraged any of the nannies that were under your employment to reach out to Mr. Epstein in any respect," he answered: "That's correct. None" (p. 71). Later a questioner asked whether he knew the name of the person in question ("No"), then showed Minority Exhibit G, read from the body "Attached is a resume of Lutnick nanny," and asked whether the person named in the subject line was the nanny who accompanied the family to Epstein's island; Lutnick answered "I don't know" (pp. 93-94).[^53]

The Commerce Department's statement called the reporting "nothing more than a failing attempt by the legacy media to distract from the administration's accomplishments" and said that "Secretary Lutnick had limited interactions with Mr. Epstein in the presence of his wife and has never been accused of wrongdoing."[^1] In a closed-door House Oversight interview whose transcript was released in May 2026, Lutnick said he had "no recollection of anything being discussed" on the island, adding, "If you said chitchat, I'd go with that," and "'Discussed' sounds like there was like a discussion, which I don't think there was."[^54] He remained in office.
