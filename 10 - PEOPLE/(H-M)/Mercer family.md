The Mercer family is the family of Robert Mercer, who was a co-chief executive of the hedge fund [[Renaissance Technologies]] until he announced in November 2017 that he would step down, and his daughters Rebekah Mercer and Jennifer Mercer; a Heather Mercer appears with them as a director of the family's private foundation and as a beneficiary of a trust holding shares in [[Emerdata]].[^1][^2] The family's records include a private foundation, a series of federal super PAC and party contributions, an investment in the data company [[Cambridge Analytica]], a role in the social network [[Parler]], and a private equity vehicle, [[1789 Capital]], of which Rebekah Mercer is a managing member of the general partner.[^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]

### Renaissance Technologies and the basket options hearing

On July 22, 2014 the [[Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations]] of the [[United States Senate]] held a hearing, "Abuse of Structured Financial Products: Misusing Basket Options to Avoid Taxes and Leverage Limits," chaired by [[Carl Levin]]. In his opening statement Levin said that Renaissance's Medallion fund had used basket options to produce profits from 1999 to 2013 totaling more than $30 billion and that [[Deutsche Bank]] and [[Barclays]] had earned about $570 million and $655 million in revenue from the structures. The tax expert Steven Rosenthal testified that the structures reduced taxes paid to the Treasury by approximately $6.8 billion by converting short-term trading profits taxed at 35 percent into long-term gains taxed at 15 percent.[^7] The Renaissance witness was Peter Brown, identified in the hearing record as co-chief executive officer and co-president of Renaissance Technologies. The name Mercer does not appear in the text of the printed hearing record, which lists among the exhibits an October 1, 2004 indemnity agreement among Barclays and the Medallion entities including Medallion Capital Investments Ltd.[^7]

[[Federal Election Commission]] contribution records from the 2010 through 2022 cycles give Robert Mercer's employer as Renaissance Technologies and his occupation in some entries as "co-CEO" and in others as "CEO."[^8] The Form 990-PF of the [[Mercer Family Foundation]] for the period beginning June 18, 2004 lists an investment in "Medallion Capital Investments" with a book value of $550,358; the return for 2010 lists "Medallion investments" valued at $8,456,921 at the start of the year and $23,361,133 at its end.[^9]

On November 2, 2017 Robert Mercer wrote to Renaissance investors that he was stepping down as co-chief executive and selling his stake in [[Breitbart News]] to his daughters; in the same letter he wrote that he had been mistaken to support [[Milo Yiannopoulos]]. [[National Public Radio]] wrote that the Mercers reportedly introduced [[Donald Trump]] to [[Steve Bannon]] and to [[Kellyanne Conway]] and "also own part of Cambridge Analytica."[^10]

In September 2021 the [[Associated Press]] reported, citing reports by the [[Wall Street Journal]] and the [[New York Times]], that Renaissance insiders including its founder [[James Simons]] and Robert Mercer had agreed to pay as much as $7 billion to settle a dispute with the [[Internal Revenue Service]] covering transactions between 2005 and 2015.[^11]

### Mercer Family Foundation

The Mercer Family Foundation (EIN 20-1982204) is a private foundation with a recognition date of June 2005 whose recent returns carry the care-of address of the accounting firm BerryDunn in Waltham, Massachusetts. Its Form 990-PF for 2004 covers a tax year that began on June 18, 2004; the foundation's disbursements for charitable purposes were $69,000 in 2005, $292,000 in 2007, $1,565,000 in 2008, $1,770,000 in 2009 and $4,039,000 in 2010.[^9][^12] The returns for 2005, 2011, 2012, 2014, 2015, 2016 and 2017 list Rebekah Mercer as the only director; the return for 2023 lists Rebekah, Robert, Jennifer and Heather Mercer, each at one hour a week, and gives Rebekah Mercer as the person in whose care the books are kept.[^2][^13]

The foundation's grants paid, as reported to the Internal Revenue Service, were $7,105,215 in 2011, $11,677,197 in 2012, $13,492,358 in 2013, $18,300,979 in 2014 and $24,544,570 in 2015, and its total assets at the end of the year fell from $37,887,151 in 2011 to $22,729,081 in 2015.[^12] The return for 2016 reports grants of $19,025,582.[^14]

The itemized recipients in the returns for 2011 to 2017 include the [[Government Accountability Institute]] ($1,000,000 in 2013 and 2014, $1,735,000 in 2015, $2,000,000 in 2016 and $1,725,000 in 2017, a total of $7,460,000); the [[Media Research Center]] ($1,800,000, $3,000,000, $2,972,420, $3,000,000, $3,000,000, $2,000,000 and $2,000,000 in 2011 through 2017, a total of $17,772,420); the [[Heartland Institute]] ($444,000, $912,000, $877,000, $885,000, $100,000, $800,000 and $800,000 in 2011 through 2017, a total of $4,818,000, after a grant of $100,000 in 2010); the [[Federalist Society]] ($1,600,000 in 2013, $2,050,000 in 2014, $2,300,000 in 2015 and $500,000 in 2017); the [[Citizens United Foundation]] ($2,000,000, $1,000,000, $550,000 and $250,000 in 2012 through 2015); the [[Heritage Foundation]] ($500,000 in each of 2013 through 2016); and [[Reclaim New York]] ($75,000 in 2014, $1,250,000 in 2015, $1,600,000 in 2016 and $1,760,000 in 2017).[^14] The return for 2016 also lists $500,000 to the [[Cleveland 2016 Host Committee]] and $500,000 to the [[Rev the Vote Foundation]], and the return for 2017 lists $500,000 to the [[Susan B. Anthony List]] Education Fund.[^14] The itemized grant lists in the returns for 2011 through 2017 and 2019 through 2024 contain no grant to Breitbart News, Parler or Cambridge Analytica.[^14][^15]

From 2016 the lists change in form. [[DonorsTrust]] received $2,250,000 in 2016 and $500,000 in 2017, and the returns for 2019 to 2024 list DonorsTrust as the principal recipient: $1,400,000 in 2019, $19,250,000 in 2020, $5,800,000 in 2021, $31,000,000 in 2022, $3,500,000 in 2023 and $3,750,000 in 2024, which with the amounts for 2016 and 2017 total $67,450,000 (the return for 2018 is not included in these totals).[^14][^15] The only other recipients in the returns for 2019 to 2024 are the [[American Association for Aerosol Research]] ($9,000 in each of 2019 to 2021), the [[Marine Corps University Foundation]] ($20,000 in 2019), the [[Hospital for Special Surgery]] ($6,772,269 in 2020), the [[Federation of Tamil Sangams of North America]] and the [[Explorer's Club]] ($100,000 each in 2021), the [[Oregon Institute of Science and Medicine]] ($250,000 in 2021) and [[Ohio State University]] ($149,778 in 2022).[^15] The Oregon Institute's address on the return for 2021, 2251 Dick George Road, Cave Junction, Oregon, is also the registered address of the committee Art Robinson for Congress, which paid Cambridge Analytica $20,000 in 2014.[^15][^16] The foundation's return for 2011 lists a grant of $965,000 to an organization at Cave Junction, Oregon.[^14] The return for 2014 lists $100,000 to the Job Creators Alliance of Addison, Texas.[^17]

A letter dated May 5, 2014 on the stationery of the Mercer Family Foundation, 240 Riverside Boulevard, New York, addressed to Mary Ann Coleman of Deutsche Bank Trust Company Americas, asks that $100,000 be wired from the foundation to the account of Job Creators Network Foundation; a callback verification block on the page names Rebekah Mercer. The document is in the [[Department of Justice]] [[Epstein Files|Epstein Library]] data set 10 among Deutsche Bank trust department records that also include client lists naming a Mercer family trust, and the page itself contains no reference to [[Jeffrey Epstein]].[^18]

### Government Accountability Institute

The Government Accountability Institute (EIN 45-4681912), a [[Tallahassee]], Florida charity with a recognition date of January 1, 2013, states its mission as "to investigate and expose crony capitalism, misuse of taxpayer monies, and other governmental corruption or malfeasance."[^19] Its Form 990 for 2012 lists Bannon as chairman (30 hours a week, compensation $80,769), [[Peter Schweizer]] as president and secretary-treasurer, and Rebekah Mercer as a director at one hour a week, with [[Hunter Lewis]], Owen Smith and Ronald Robinson as the other directors, and Wynton C. Hall as communications strategist. Schedule O to that return states that "Stephen Bannon, Chairman, and Rebekah Mercer, Board Director, have a business relationship."[^19]

Schedule L to the returns for 2013, 2014 and 2015 reports purchases of advertising on the website of Breitbart News Network, LLC, with the explanation that "Stephen Bannon is chairman of the board" of that company, in the amounts of $102,500, $7,500 and $98,000; the return for 2015 adds that a committee of disinterested board members reviewed the proposed purchase and collected comparison data from other online advertising venues.[^20] Bannon's reportable compensation from the institute was $100,000 for 2013, $100,733 for 2014 and $96,703 for 2015.[^20] The return for 2016 lists Rebekah Mercer as "Chairman of the Board" at 30 hours a week without compensation, Bannon as a board director at one hour a week with compensation of $61,539, Schweizer at $210,000 and Hall as a key employee at $175,000; the returns for 2017, 2020 and 2022 also list her as chairman.[^21]

The institute's total contributions were $2,200,000 in 2012, $2,602,500 in 2013, $1,701,000 in 2014, $2,587,000 in 2015, $2,601,000 in 2016 and $2,435,250 in 2017; for 2013 through 2017 the foundation's itemized grants to the institute were $7,460,000 of $11,926,750 in contributions.[^14][^22]

### Cambridge Analytica and Emerdata

According to [[Christopher Wylie]], Robert Mercer invested $15 million in Cambridge Analytica for 90 percent of the company and served as its president, with Jennifer Mercer as treasurer; the company's statement of financial affairs lists Rebekah Mercer as a member of the board of managers and vice president and Jennifer Mercer as a member of the board of managers from March 6, 2014 through January 23, 2018.[^4] [[Brittany Kaiser]] told the [[Senate Select Committee on Intelligence]] that almost every client meeting with a political figure was preceded by an introduction from Bannon, Rebekah Mercer or Conway, and [[Jared Kushner]] told the committee that the Mercers aggressively advocated the campaign's use of the company.[^27]

Rebekah and Jennifer Mercer became directors of Emerdata Limited on March 16, 2018 and are recorded as active directors on the Companies House register of October 1, 2026; the company has been in administration since December 9, 2024.[^28] Jennifer Mercer was appointed on March 12, 2021 a director of [[Dynamo Recoveries Limited]], a non-trading company incorporated on March 4, 2021 with Terence William Donal Blaney as its first person with significant control; since March 12, 2021 Rebekah Mercer has been recorded as its only person with significant control, holding 75 percent or more of the shares and votes. [[Jacquelyn James-Varga]], who is also a director of Emerdata, is a director of the company.[^29] Companies House gives the correspondence address of the Mercers, James-Varga and Dynamo Recoveries as 60 Churchill Square, Kings Hill, West Malling, England.[^28][^29]

### Parler and 1789 Capital

In November 2020 [[Newsweek]] reported, citing people familiar with the matter who spoke to the [[The Wall Street Journal|Wall Street Journal]], that the Mercers had ties to Parler; Newsweek wrote that the chief executive John Matze had confirmed Rebekah Mercer as a lead investor in the early stages of the company and that her funding was conditional on the application relinquishing control over what users can see, and that she had written on the platform that she launched Parler with Matze to "provide a neutral platform for free speech, as our founders intended."[^30] A Form D filed with the [[Securities and Exchange Commission]] on January 6, 2022 by Parler Inc., a Delaware corporation at 901 Woodland Street, Nashville, lists Rebekah Mercer as an executive officer, director and promoter alongside the chief executive George Farmer; it reports a first sale of equity on December 15, 2021, $20,250,000 offered and sold to 10 investors at a minimum of $50,000, and finders' fees of $500,000, with William C.T. Gaynor II of Washington as the sales compensation recipient.[^5]

A Form D filed on February 9, 2023 by 1789 Capital Fund I, LP of Palm Beach, Florida lists [[Omeed Malik]], Rebekah Mercer and [[Christopher Buskirk]] each as executive officer, director, promoter and managing member of the general partner, with a first sale on January 31, 2023, $100,000,000 offered and $10,000,000 sold to one investor at a minimum of $1,000,000.[^6] Two further Forms D filed on June 10, 2024 by 1789 Capital Co-Invest I, LP and 1789 Capital BH, LP list her as manager of the general partner; the first reports $2,162,000 sold to seven investors and the second $9,700,000 of a $10,000,000 offering sold to 28 investors.[^31]
