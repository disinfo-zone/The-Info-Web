John E. Mack was born in [[New York City]], took an undergraduate degree at [[Oberlin College]] in 1951 and a medical degree at [[Harvard Medical School]] in 1955, served in the [[United States Air Force]] from 1959 to 1961, became chief of psychiatry at [[Cambridge Hospital]] in 1969 and professor at [[Harvard University|Harvard]] in 1972, and won the 1977 [[Pulitzer Prize]] for the biography *[[A Prince of Our Disorder]]: The Life of [[T. E. Lawrence]]*. He was killed by a car in [[London]] on September 27, 2004.[^1]

### The Center

The [[John E. Mack Institute]]'s history states that the organization began in 1982 in [[Cambridge, Massachusetts|Cambridge]], founded by Mack and [[Robert J. Lifton]] as an affiliate of Harvard Medical School studying "the psychological, historical and political complexities that maintain and perpetuate nuclear weapons"; it became an independent nonprofit in 1986 as the [[Center for Psychological Studies in the Nuclear Age]], took consultative status with the [[United Nations]] Economic and Social Council in 1988, and in 1992 became the [[Center for Psychology and Social Change]].[^2] Its newsletters of 1993 to 1995 carry the line "An affiliate of Harvard Medical School at The Cambridge Hospital."[^3]

### Abduction Research and Rockefeller

In 1993 the Center formed the [[Program for Extraordinary Experience Research]]; its newsletter of September 1993 states that the project was "Funded by a grant from the Human Potential Foundation," and the Institute states that it was formed "with funding from Laurence Rockefeller."[^2][^4] [[Laurance Rockefeller]] invited Mack to the roundtable he hosted at the [[JY Ranch]] in September 1993, and the photograph published by [[Paradigm Research Group]] names Mack beside Rockefeller.[^5] Mack's book *[[Abduction: Human Encounters with Aliens]]* was published in 1994; the Rockefeller correspondence of April 26, 1994 mentions it.[^6] *PEER Perspectives* of Winter 1998/99 reports a panel of scientists "funded by the Laurance S. Rockefeller Fund."[^7] [[Ben MacIntyre]] of the London *[[The Times|Times]]* wrote in May 1995, while Harvard's review was under way, that "His Center for Psychology and Social Change is supported by an annual grant of $250,000 from Laurance S. Rockefeller," and quoted Mack's lawyer Roderick McLeish in the same article.[^8] The [[Valley News]] of May 20, 1995 stated that Mack's work "has received generous subsidies from Laurance Rockefeller, patriarch of [[Woodstock, Vermont|Woodstock]], who reportedly donates a quarter million dollars a year" to the Center, and that a spokesman for Rockefeller "said his policy is not to comment publicly on Mack's projects."[^9] Neither article names a source for the figure.

### Harvard Review

Dean [[Daniel C. Tosteson]] convened a "Special Faculty Committee" in the summer of 1994 under the chairmanship of [[Arnold Relman]]. In the [[Baltimore Sun]]'s account of August 4, 1995, Tosteson had told Mack the previous Friday that the review had ended; Harvard counsel [[Anne Taylor]], a member of the committee, said that Mack remained a full member of the faculty "with no adverse action taken," and Relman said the committee never considered revoking his tenure. The same report states that the committee's preliminary report, drafted earlier in 1995, "chastised Dr. Mack for 'affirming the delusions' of his many patients" and found him "in violation of the standards of conduct expected of a member of the faculty of Harvard University," and that Mack's lawyer, [[Eric MacLeish]], contested the findings.[^10] [[Alan Dershowitz]] criticized the inquiry in the [[The Harvard Crimson|Harvard Crimson]] on June 30, 1995; Relman replied there on September 13, 1995 that the committee's work was confidential, that Mack's right to research abduction was never challenged, and that the committee was critical because Mack "wasn't doing any scientific research on the problem."[^11] Whether Rockefeller or the [[Human Potential Foundation]] intervened with Harvard is not documented in any record.
