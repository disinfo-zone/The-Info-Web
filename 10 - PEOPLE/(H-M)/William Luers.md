William H. Luers was a deputy to [[Harry Shlaudeman]], assistant secretary of state for inter-American affairs, in 1976.[^1]

### The Cable

Shlaudeman's memorandum to [[Henry A. Kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] of August 5, 1976, warned that the [[Southern Cone]] governments "have established Operation Condor to find and kill terrorists" in their own countries and in Europe. Luers and Shlaudeman's other deputy, [[Hewson Ryan]], drafted the response over three weeks. Cable 209192 of August 23, drafted by R. Zimmerman and Luers, cleared by Shlaudeman, R. Kirk, and the under secretary [[Philip Habib]], and approved by Kissinger, went by the Roger Channel to the embassies in [[Buenos Aires]], [[Montevideo]], and [[Santiago]]. "Government planned and directed assassinations within and outside the territory of Condor members has most serious implications which we must face squarely and rapidly." There were "rumors that this cooperation may extend beyond information exchange to include plans for the assassination of subversives, politicians and prominent figures both within the national borders of certain Southern Cone countries and abroad." The ambassadors were to "seek appointment as soon as possible with highest appropriate official, preferably the chief of state." The embassy at [[La Paz]] was told: "We are not repeat not instructing you to make the specific demarche on Condor."[^1][^2]

Popper replied on August 24: "I seriously doubt that an approach to President Pinochet is the best way to achieve it. It is quite possible, even probable, that Pinochet has no knowledge whatever of Operation Condor." He proposed that the CIA station chief tell Contreras instead, "in the context of having heard a rumor which he cannot believe."[^3]

### No Further Action

Ambassador [[Ernest Siracusa]] in Montevideo declined to deliver it and asked for instructions; the ambassadors in Chile and Argentina raised concerns. Luers spent the following weeks pressing Ambassador [[David Popper]] to deliver the message to [[Augusto Pinochet]] or through the CIA to [[Manuel Contreras]]. On September 16 Kissinger cabled from [[Lusaka]] that he "declined to approve message to Montevideo" and "instructed that no further action be taken on this matter." On September 19 Luers cabled Shlaudeman in [[Costa Rica]] on how to proceed; the cable remains classified. On September 20 Shlaudeman directed Luers "to instruct the ambassadors to take no further action, noting that there have been no reports in some weeks indicating an intention to activate the Condor scheme." [[Orlando Letelier]] and [[Ronni Moffitt]] were killed in Washington the next morning.[^1]
