Cecil B. "Scott" Jones (February 19, 1928, at [[Emporia, Kansas]], to January 16, 2023) was a Navy fighter pilot and intelligence officer who worked from 1985 to 1991 on the staff of Senator [[Claiborne Pell]] of [[Rhode Island]], then as president of the [[Human Potential Foundation]].[^1] His birth year is also recorded as 1924, in the register of his papers at the [[University of Colorado Boulder]].[^2]

### Navy and Intelligence Career

Jones joined the [[United States Navy]] about 1946, was commissioned in 1950 after the [[Aviation Midshipman Program]], flew two combat tours in [[VF-191]] in the [[Korean War]] in the [[F9F-2 Panther]] from the [[USS Princeton]], and served about fifteen years in naval intelligence, including with [[Carrier Division 14]] and as assistant naval attache in [[New Delhi]] and [[Kathmandu]] in the 1960s. He held an A.B. in government from [[George Washington University]] (1961), an M.A. from the [[University of Maryland]] (1963) and a Ph.D. in international studies from [[American University]] (1975), with a dissertation on how the Indian [[Lok Sabha]] handles defense matters, and taught at [[Casper College]] and the [[University of Wyoming]].[^3] By another account he spent twenty years in naval intelligence, then taught at Army colleges from 1970 to 1972, at the [[University of Kansas]] for the Navy in 1973 to 1975 and as a professor at the University of Wyoming from 1975 to 1978, then two years at the contractor [[CACI]] managing intelligence contact with the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]], "during which spent 10% of his time on applied anomalous phenomena," then work for [[Kaman Tempo]] on reporting on oceanic nuclear tests for the [[Defense Nuclear Agency]]; he met Pell during this period and organized two symposia on applied anomalous phenomena and on consciousness and survival.[^2] [[Grant Cameron]] gives a contractual consultancy to the Defense Nuclear Agency from 1981 to 1985.[^4] His papers include correspondence with [[Laurance Rockefeller]], [[Hans-Adam II, Prince of Liechtenstein]], [[Rima Laibow]], [[Uri Geller]] and [[Dick Cheney]], and drafts of letters to Presidents [[George W. Bush]], [[Bill Clinton]] and [[Barack Obama]].[^2]

In the 1992 white paper's account, Kaman Tempo organized a seminar on parapsychological applications in 1983 for federal employees, with Jones as editor of the proceedings, and Jones's MUFON biographical sketch says he worked on government-sponsored projects for the Defense Nuclear Agency, the DIA and the [[United States Army Intelligence and Security Command]].[^3]

### Pell's Office, 1985 to 1991

Jones served as Special Assistant to Pell from January 1985 until March 1991 "with the purpose of investigating consciousness research being conducted in other countries, primarily the [[Soviet Union|USSR]]."[^2] The [[Washington Times]] reported in 1989 that Pell "has a full-time staff member, C.B. Scott Jones, whose exclusive job is to monitor reports of psychic activities."[^5] The [[United Press International]], summarizing the [[Providence Journal|Providence Sunday Journal]] of July 17, 1988, reported that Jones "retains a top secret security clearance and earns nearly $50,000 a year on the public payroll," and that Jones said the senator wanted scientists to have a fair chance at funding for the research.[^6] The two reports give the figures differently. The UPI summary says "nearly $50,000 a year"; the version of [[C. Eugene Emery Jr.]]'s Providence Journal story printed in *The [[San Francisco Examiner]]* of August 13, 1988 states the amount as $48,853 a year, describes Jones as "Pell's point man" in the effort to make the government more receptive to paranormal research, and says that in his spare time he was a UFO enthusiast who ran a psychic-research center out of his home. Both give the clearance as top secret (the Examiner prints "top-secret security clearance").[^7] The *[[Winston-Salem Journal]]* of July 18, 1988, also summarizing the Providence Journal, described Jones as a former naval intelligence officer and one of Pell's "government-paid researchers" who researched telepathy, unconventional healing and near-death experiences.[^8] Jones used a Senate office and stationery; [[U.S. News and World Report]] of December 5, 1988 mentioned Pell and Jones, and in 1991 a personal letter from Jones to "Dear Dick" Dick Cheney was published in [[Harper's Magazine]] (January 1991, p. 25).[^3] The [[University of Rhode Island]]'s register of the [[Pell Senatorial Papers]] lists three files titled "Human Potential Foundation: Scott Jones File" (1987; 1987 to 1994; 1988 to 1991) in the subject file series.[^9]

### The White Paper of 1992

"Will the Real Scott Jones Please Stand Up?," dated February 20, 1992 and credited to [[Robert J. Durant]], appears in the [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]] reading room as CIA-RDP96-00792R000400300004-7; a note at its end states that [[George P. Hansen]] co-authored it without his name appearing.[^3] It opens by describing Jones as "the Renaissance Man of the Paranormal." Its claims, attributed to Durant, include that Jones chaired a panel and acted as liaison to "the Prince of Liechtenstein who was the major benefactor of the conference," at the [[TREAT II]] abduction conference at [[Virginia Tech]] in January 1990 organized by Rima Laibow; that [[MUFON]] appointed him Special Consultant in International Relations in 1989; that in 1991 he told the Chinese officials he visited about UFOs, in a story he later told at the Omega conference, that his questions were taken for pretense, and that metal given to him there proved to be debris from an electrical transmission tower; that he delivered "Government UFO Connections" at the 1991 MUFON symposium and there said he "honestly did not know of any activity of the [[U.S. government]]" on UFOs; and that he spoke approvingly of [[William L. Moore]] and met Moore and [[Richard Doty]] while on Pell's payroll.[^3]

The paper also relates a story that [[Lee Graham]], an [[Aerojet]] technician, told [[Don Ecker]] and Durant: after an [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]] agent and an unidentified man visited Graham in 1987 and discussed the [[Majestic-12|MJ-12]] papers, Jones telephoned Graham, said they had "a mutual acquaintance," and sent on Senate letterhead the Air Force biography of Major General [[Michael Kerby]], the Air Force legislative liaison director, whom Graham recognized as the visitor. In the paper's account Jones explained that Kerby had called on Pell, noticed Jones, and told him of the visit because "Kerby knew of my interest in these matters"; Jones declined to elaborate. Durant also wrote that an unnamed informant learned that [[Jaime Shandera]] and Moore were in contact with Jones, and listed rumors, which the paper attributes to unnamed "Scott watchers," that Jones had asked parapsychologists about recruiting psychics for military intelligence, had been involved for years in classified research on electronic mind control, and had approached media representatives in 1986 about a UFO program offering secret government information. The paper also describes dolphin telepathy experiments by Jones with Colonel [[John B. Alexander]] and [[Theodore Rockwell]]. The paper states of Jones's organizations that the Human Potential Foundation was established in 1989, was housed in the [[Vienna, Virginia]], offices of the law firm [[Sandground Barondess and West]] with [[Mark Sandground]] and Pell on its board, and was "reportedly funded by Laurance Rockefeller."[^3]

### Human Potential Foundation, 1989 to 1998

Jones left Pell's staff in March 1991 and ran the Foundation until its dissolution in 1998.[^2] The Foundation's primary donor in its first two and a half years was Laurance Rockefeller, "whose support was conditional with a program designed to get the [[United States]] government to acknowledge the existence of alien life."[^2] Jones wrote as its president to [[John Gibbons]] at the [[Office of Science and Technology Policy]] from 1993 to 1995, to Representative [[Steven Schiff]], and in August 1995 to the congressional leadership; the letters are described under the [[Rockefeller Initiative]].[^10] [[Dinah Eng]]'s wire story of May 1995 on the Foundation's conference "[[When Cosmic Cultures Meet]]" (May 27 to 29, Washington) identified Jones as the Foundation's president and as a former naval intelligence officer who served on Pell's staff; it reported that the program would examine contact with extraterrestrials rather than debate whether intelligent life exists elsewhere, and quoted Jones as saying he had "no doubt that intelligent life exists elsewhere" and that there was "an extraordinary amount of fear associated with this phenomena."[^11] In a letter of February 17, 1994 he wrote to Gibbons: "My mention of mind-control technology at the February 4 meeting was quite deliberate. Please be careful about this. There are reasons to believe that some government group has interwoven research about this technology with alleged UFO phenomena."[^12] In his letter of August 11, 1994, after describing a conference of the [[Institute of Noetic Sciences]] in [[Chicago]] and Pell's seat on its board, he advised Gibbons to speak with [[James Woolsey]]; the passage that follows (the scan is torn) reports that Woolsey had a document search under way in his agency that had found nothing of significance, and quotes the statement: "If I had the responsibility to run a super safe black program on this subject, I would take it completely out of the government's [...] and run it from the private sector, probably somewhere within [the] aerospace community, or perhaps [[General Motors]]."[^13] A handwritten note signed "JHG" and dated 3/29/94 on the same letter reads: "Scott Jones used to be associated with Senator Pell. He was formerly a Navy pilot. The Human Potential Foundation is funded in part, I believe, by L.R."[^12]

Jones presented a talk on exopolitics in [[Hawaii]] in June 2006.[^14] He founded the [[Peace and Emergency Action Coalition for Earth]] in 1998, which received its [[Internal Revenue Service]] ruling in October 1998.[^2][^15]

### Series 4 and 6 container titles

The finding aid lists Series 4 (Human Potential Foundation, with subseries for Pell, the Foundation Board, Grants and Funding, Projects and Events) and Series 6 ("Special Assistant to Senator Claiborne Pell," with Legislation, Symposium on Consciousness and Survival 1985, Soviet-American Relations, Paranormal Aide, Reports and organizations, Unidentified Flying Objects, Speeches and Subject Files subseries). Among the titles are "Uri Geller Scandal" files, "Soviet Psychotronics correspondence and memos," "National Science Foundation: [[Andrija Puharich]] reports and correspondence," "Howard J. Hoffman Government Liaison Project" and "A Model of Government Preparation for ET Contact and Cooperation" by Jones; the full title lists are on the pages for [[Human Potential Foundation]] and [[Claiborne Pell]].[^16]
