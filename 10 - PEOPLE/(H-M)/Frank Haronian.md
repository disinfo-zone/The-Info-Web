Frank J. Haronian Jr. (1922-1994), who published as Frank Haronian, was a clinical psychologist who practiced in the Princeton area of [[New Jersey]] and the investigator of [[Project MKUltra|MKULTRA]] Subproject 134, a 1961 study of physique and personality financed by the [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]]. He received his Ph.D. from the [[New School for Social Research]] in 1963, co-authored body-type studies with [[A. Arthur Sugerman]] between 1964 and 1967, and spoke and wrote for the [[Psychosynthesis Research Foundation]].

### Family and education

Haronian was born in [[Durham, North Carolina]], in 1922 and lived in [[New York City]] before moving to [[Lawrenceville, New Jersey]], in 1958.[^1] His father, Frank J. Haronian, was born in Constantinople. The records of the [[University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill]] list a Frank J. Haronian of Constantinople among graduate students for 1920 and 1921, and a Frank John Haronian, M.A., as instructor in French for 1922-1923.[^2] His mother, Violet E. Haronian, was born at Mikhalitch, Turkey, on December 23, 1896, and died at [[Ocean Grove, New Jersey]], on May 3, 1981. Her obituary describes her as the widow of Frank J. Haronian, a former Captain in the [[United States Army]] who after his retirement taught French at the University of North Carolina and at Morris High School in New York City, and lists her survivors as two sons, one of them the subject.[^3]

Haronian received a bachelor of science degree in 1943 from the [[City College of New York]], where he was enrolled in the ROTC program. He enlisted in the Army, served as a sergeant in [[Paris]] during [[World War II]], and afterward received a master's degree in education from City College.[^1] He began work in 1950 as a school psychologist in the [[Somerville School District]] of New Jersey and also worked in the school districts of Princeton and neighboring towns.[^1] In 1963 he received a Ph.D. in clinical psychology from the New School for Social Research in New York City.[^1]

### Subproject 134

Haronian was the investigator of MKULTRA Subproject 134.[^4][^5] The subproject's status page gives its purpose as exploratory research on the relation of component patterns of physique to patterns of personality characteristics. It records the subproject as initiated in July 1961, at a cost of $22,000.00, with actual work expected to commence in September 1961.[^6]

A memorandum for the record of May 29, 1961 calls the study "frankly exploratory in nature" and places its relevance to the Agency in its potential contribution to clandestine indirect assessment work carried out without the subject's awareness.[^7] The memorandum refers to the investigator only as "Mr." with the name blacked out, and states that he "is neither cleared nor witting of true Sponsor." It says that the research will provide a redacted doctor with data "useful in advancing his own research program (MKULTRA, Subproject 77)."

A memorandum of June 9, 1961, titled "Recommendation for Funding," recommends the $22,000 "despite the superficiality of the proposal and the questionable products which have issued from previous research." It says the work "fits into our indirect assessment requirement and will contribute to the [[David Wechsler|Wechsler-Bellevue]] work" in which a redacted doctor is engaged.[^8]

The proposal arrived as a revision in the form of a letter. It covered the year from September 1, 1961 to August 31, 1962, with a budget of $22,000 in which the stipend line was $10,800. It planned preparation from September to November 1961, testing and data collection from December 1961 to May 1962, and analysis from June to August 1962, with at least 100 subjects and possibly as many as 150.[^9] The letter says the author would devote his full attention to the project "as soon as my present contract expires at the end of June," and that a redacted consultant "has kindly agreed to act as a consultant to me."

The pilot study used fifty adult male Protestant theological students and ministers, photographed and somatotyped by [[William H. Sheldon|Sheldon]] or his assistants. The participants were given the MMPI, the Myers-Briggs and the [[Saul Rosenzweig|Rosenzweig]] picture-frustration test, and only the MMPI scores had entered the statistical analysis.[^10] A reviewer whose name is redacted, signing as "Ph.D.," called the report "poorly organized," noted "more variables (60 to 70) than subjects," and wrote that "some of the findings are provocative, and more work of this sort should be done."[^11]

An invoice memorandum of July 12, 1961 asks that the check be forwarded by July 24, 1961, and says that additional funds were anticipated for the project.[^12] A February 1963 accounting for the grant period from September 1, 1961 to August 31, 1962 records $22,000.00 received. Its expenditures include $10,800.00 for the "Salary of Principal Investigator" and total $18,827.50, which leaves a balance of $3,172.50 "to be applied to next year's grant." The accounting closes with the statement that it is a true account "as reported to the Fund." A receipt dated August 16, 1961 acknowledges Treasurer's Check No. 184788, dated August 11, 1961, in the amount of $22,000.00.[^13]

[[David H. Price]] tabulated the grants of the [[Society for the Investigation of Human Ecology|Human Ecology Fund]] for 1960 to 1963. His table lists "Physique and psychological functioning," with Frank Haronian as investigator in psychology, at $39,000. It is the only entry in the table whose title concerns physique.[^4] The release redacts the investigator's name in every memorandum. The 1961 memorandum fixes the subproject at $22,000, and the accounting records a balance carried to the next year's grant.

The first-page note of the 1964 paper by Sugerman and Haronian reads: "This study was partly supported by a grant to Frank Haronian from the Human Ecology Fund."[^5]

The copy of the subproject file that the CIA sent to the New School on September 7, 1977 carries the school's name reinserted in red on the May 29 memorandum.[^7] The covering letter, signed by [[A. R. Cinquegrana]] of the agency's Office of General Counsel and addressed to [[John R. Everett]], says that "the name of the School has been reinserted wherever it appears in the original documents." The letter states that the names of all other institutions, all individuals and the CIA employees who are not publicly acknowledged remain deleted.[^14]

### New Jersey Bureau of Research in Neurology and Psychiatry

The 1964 paper with Sugerman gives Haronian's address as [[Trenton, New Jersey|Trenton]], New Jersey, and Sugerman's affiliation as the [[New Jersey Bureau of Research in Neurology and Psychiatry]] in [[Princeton, New Jersey|Princeton]].[^5] The papers of 1965 to 1967 list both men at the Bureau, two of them with a Princeton address.[^15][^16][^17]

With Sugerman he published the 1964 study in the [[Journal of Personality]], a 1965 comparison of two methods for quantifying physique in the [[American Journal of Physical Anthropology]], a 1966 study of field independence in [[Perceptual and Motor Skills]], and a 1967 review of field independence studies in [[Psychological Reports]].[^5][^15][^16][^17] Alone, he published "The ethical relevance of a psychotherapeutic technique" in the [[Journal of Religion and Health]] in April 1967.[^18]

### Psychosynthesis Research Foundation

[[Roberto Assagioli]] wrote that the foundation was created in 1957 with its own seat at Valmy, Greenville, Delaware, and that an international congress was held at Valmy in 1959.[^19] The London institute's own history states that the foundation was created in New York under the patronage of the [[Dupont family]] and names [[Frank Hilton]] as its head in New York.[^20] By 1969 the foundation had an office in New York, with Frank Hilton as director.[^21] By proclamation of January 24, 1966, [[Charles L. Terry Jr.]], Governor of [[Delaware]], declared repealed the charters of corporations that had failed for two years to pay assessed taxes, and the list printed in the Laws of Delaware includes the Psychosynthesis Research Foundation.[^22] The foundation's notice of December 1, 1967 gives its New York office and, as its registered office and treasurer, an address in [[Wilmington, Delaware]].[^23]

Haronian spoke on "The Denial of the Sublime" at the third meeting of the foundation's 1967/68 series of Psychosynthesis Seminars, held at 7:45 p.m. on December 15, 1967 in the Directors Room of the Park Sheraton Hotel in New York. The notice, signed by [[Jack Cooper]], M.D., describes him as of the Bureau.[^23] The paper was issued as "The Repression of the Sublime," "Presented at a seminar of the Psychosynthesis Research Foundation, on December 15, 1967."[^24] Assagioli's 1973 book The Act of Will says that the subject had been "well dealt with by Frank Haronian."[^25] At the second meeting of the 1968/69 series, on November 15, 1968, [[Jean Houston]] spoke on "The relationship between the psychedelic experience and some of the techniques of psychosynthesis." The participants listed include Frank Haronian, Mr. and Mrs. Frank Hilton, Jack Cooper and Houston.[^26] Haronian translated [[Robert Desoille]]'s The Directed Daydream, issued by the foundation as its Issue No. 18.[^27]

Haronian's "A Psychosynthetic Model of Personality and Its Implications for Psychotherapy" appeared in the [[Journal of Humanistic Psychology]] in the fall of 1975, and "Psychosynthesis: A Psychotherapist's Personal Overview" in [[Pastoral Psychology]] in Fall 1976. In the second, he wrote that he first became interested in psychosynthesis because he was looking for "a point of view that could bridge psychology and religion," and that he had been surprised by the readiness with which "my clerical colleagues" accepted the Freudian image of human nature. The article's author note gives a Lawrenceville address, states "Dr. Haronian is in private practice," and directs readers to the foundation's New York office.[^28][^29]

### Practice, family and death

Advertisements in the Princeton weekly Town Topics from 1987 to 1991 list Frank Haronian, Ph.D., among the practitioners of psychotherapy and transpersonal counseling at an office in Princeton.[^30] He was a co-founder and the first clinician of [[Trinity Counseling Service]] in Princeton, where he remained active.[^1]

His wife, Ardell Marashian Haronian, died in 1990. He died at his home in Lawrenceville on October 22, 1994, aged 72, survived by two daughters, a brother and four grandchildren. A memorial service was held at Trinity Church in Princeton.[^1] The New Jersey death indexes list Violet E. Haronian in 1981 and Frank J. Haronian Jr. in 1994.[^31] [[Nancy Newton Verrier]]'s book The Primal Wound is dedicated to Frank Haronian (1922-1994).[^32]
