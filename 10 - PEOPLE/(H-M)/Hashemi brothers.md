The [[Hashemi brothers]] were a trio of [[Iran|Iranian]] brothers living in the [[United States|West]] who claimed to have connections in Iran with [[Ahmed Khomeini]], the son of [[Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini]]. They offered their services to the [[Jimmy Carter|Carter]] administration to help secure the release of the American hostages held in [[Tehran]].[^1]

The brothers, [[Cyrus Hashemi]], [[Jamshid Hashemi]], and [[Reza Hashemi]], also claimed to be cousins of the influential [[Ali Akbar Hashemi Rafsanjani|Hojjat El-Islam Ali Akbar Hashemi Rafsanjani]]. They made a significant amount of money in arms sales, using their influence with the [[White House]] to sell small quantities of military equipment to the Iranians, supposedly to gain goodwill for the release of the hostages.[^1]

The report of the congressional Iran-Contra committees records that in May 1984 federal criminal charges were filed against Cyrus Hashemi and his brothers Reza and Jamshid, among others, for alleged arms export control violations; that Cyrus arranged with the U.S. Customs Service to run a sting operation resulting in the April 22, 1986 indictment of 17 individuals in the Southern District of New York on charges of an illegal scheme to smuggle $2.5 billion in American-made weapons to Iran; and that until Cyrus's death in July 1986 he was to be the primary prosecution witness.[^2] The Second Circuit wrote that the arms in that case were destined for Cyrus, "a government agent pretending to be an Iranian buyer," and that the original indictment was returned in May 1986.[^3] Judge [[Leonard B. Sand]] wrote in 1987 that none of the arms deals charged was consummated and that Cyrus's negotiations with the defendants were tape recorded.[^4]

Their Tehran contact, [[Iran Najd Rankuni]], was connected with the [[Iranian Revolution]] but did not have direct access to the Supreme Council, which made it impossible for them to set up a serious dialogue between the Americans and the Iranians for the hostages' release. The Hashemi brothers could not promise major arms sales through the [[Israel|Israelis]], which limited their effectiveness.[^1]

After the [[Ronald Reagan|Reagan]] administration took office in 1981 and the hostages were released, the [[U.S. Customs|U.S. Customs]] began investigating the Hashemi brothers. In 1984, they were indicted under the [[Arms Export Control Act]] for the illegal sale of weapons to Iran. Cyrus and Jamshid were tipped off and escaped the U.S., but Reza was arrested and became a hostage himself. His elder brothers then negotiated with Customs to become informants against the Iranian exile community in [[Europe]] and the United States.[^1]

Cyrus was later used by [[Oliver North]] and U.S. Customs in a sting operation to entrap individuals connected with Israeli intelligence arms sales to Iran. Cyrus was found dead in his [[London]] apartment, with his death attributed to a sudden case of virulent leukemia, though Israeli intelligence suspected foul play.[^1]
