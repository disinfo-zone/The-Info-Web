Kenneth A. Howery co-founded [[PayPal]] after graduating from [[Stanford University]] and later co-founded a venture capital firm, [[Founders Fund]], with [[Peter Thiel]] and [[Luke Nosek]].[^1][^2] He was the [[United States]] ambassador to [[Sweden]] from 2019, appointed as a non-career appointee with [[Texas]] as his state of residence, and was confirmed as ambassador to [[Denmark]] on October 7, 2025.[^3][^4] He told the [[Senate Foreign Relations Committee]] in 2025 that his grandfathers "eventually raised their own families in Texas, where I grew up."[^5]

### Family and Early Career

In his statement to the Senate Foreign Relations Committee on May 16, 2019, Howery introduced his mother, Karen Howery, his father, Ken Howery, and his sister, and said that both of his grandfathers had served in the Second World War: Fred Howery in the [[United States Navy]] in the Pacific and Hubert Jurek in the [[United States Army]] in Europe and North Africa.[^1] He repeated the family history to the committee in May 2025.[^5]

He told the committee in 2019: "When I graduated from Stanford, I co-founded PayPal. Our idea was simple: to make online transactions easy."[^1] [[Ingrid Lunden]] of TechCrunch wrote in January 2025 that Howery was part of the original "[[PayPal Mafia]]", "the CFO who helped bring it together and sell it to eBay", and "a close pal of Elon Musk's".[^2] A [[Federal Election Commission]] record of a 5,000 dollar contribution to the PayPal Inc. Political Action Committee on January 17, 2002 gives his employer and title as "PayPal Inc./Director of Corporate D[evelopment]", Palo Alto.[^6]

### Founders Fund

In his prepared statement of 2019 Howery wrote: "After PayPal, I co-founded a venture capital firm that has and continues to support hundreds of new companies, helping create tens of thousands of jobs, and fostering innovation in space exploration, quantum computing, biotechnology, virtual reality, alternative energy, and consumer technologies." He named [[Spotify]] as one of those companies.[^7] In 2025 he described the fund's early investments as including companies "working in fields including aerospace, defense, artificial intelligence, and biotechnology", and said: "In 2019, I left the private sector for public service".[^5]

The [[Securities and Exchange Commission]]'s Form D filings for Founders Fund vehicles list Howery with Thiel and Nosek. Founders Fund SpaceX Fund, LP (filed May 15, 2009; 3,481,500 dollars sold to 11 investors, amended on August 17, 2009 to 6,737,500 dollars from 29 investors) lists Howery, Thiel, Nosek and [[Sean Parker]] as directors, at One Letterman Drive, Building C, Suite 420, [[San Francisco]]. Founders Fund III (filed May 5, 2010; 107,272,727 dollars from 21 investors) and Founders Fund IV (filed December 1, 2011; 500,000,000 dollars from 58 investors) list him as a promoter; Founders Fund V (filed March 6, 2014; 1,015,200,000 dollars from 109 investors) and Founders Fund VI (filed March 25, 2016; 1,274,127,000 dollars from 197 investors) list him as a director.[^8] A Federal Election Commission record of October 7, 2016 gives his employer as Founders Fund and his occupation as general partner.[^6] At his 2019 hearing Senator [[Mitt Romney]], presiding, introduced him as "the co-founder of PayPal, as well as an early investor in major [[Silicon Valley]] companies such as Facebook and SpaceX".[^1]

### Ambassador to Sweden

The committee heard the nomination on May 16, 2019, with Romney presiding and Senators [[Ben Cardin]], [[Jeanne Shaheen]], [[Tom Udall]] and [[Tim Kaine]] present. Howery listed participation in the [[World Economic Forum]] and membership of the [[Explorers Club]], said that he advised [[Kiva]], and said he had travelled to 99 countries. In questioning, Romney asked about the security partnership with Sweden, and Howery answered that Sweden was "a [[NATO]] partner, not a NATO member". Shaheen asked about Russian disinformation in Sweden; Howery cited Sweden's national board for psychological defense and agreed to meet the State Department's [[Global Engagement Center]]. Udall asked about climate change and Kaine about antisemitism in Sweden.[^1]

In answers to written questions from Senators [[Robert Menendez]] and Cardin, Howery answered "No" when asked whether anyone had made a complaint of sexual harassment, discrimination or inappropriate conduct against him. Asked whether he would bring to the committee's attention any change in policy that he suspected might be influenced by the business or financial interests of the President or senior [[White House]] staff, he answered: "If confirmed, I commit to comply with all relevant federal ethics laws, regulations, and rules, and to raise concerns that I may have through appropriate channels." Asked whether he or his immediate family had financial interests in Sweden, he answered: "My investment portfolio includes interests in entities that have a presence in Sweden. This includes stock holdings, mutual funds or exchange traded funds, and private investment funds that hold interests in companies with a presence in Sweden." He added that if confirmed he would "divest all investments the State Department Ethics Office deems necessary and recuse myself from matters when required to avoid a conflict of interest".[^1]

The Senate confirmed the nomination, PN127, on September 17, 2019 by a vote of 62 to 32 (roll call 285).[^9] He was appointed on September 20, 2019 and assumed charge on October 12, 2019.[^3] Federal Election Commission records show three contributions by "Howery, Ken Mr." to the [[Republican National Committee]] dated October 13, 2020: 106,500 dollars to its headquarters account, 58,000 dollars to its legal proceedings account and 35,500 dollars to its general account, 200,000 dollars in all. The employer on each entry is "Stockholm" and the occupation "U.S. Ambassador".[^6] In 2025 Howery told the committee that in Sweden he had worked on "countering China's influence" and on "strongly advocating for Sweden's increased defense spending and interoperability, helping pave the wave for its eventual NATO accession".[^5]

### Ambassador to Denmark

Lunden described Howery in January 2025 as "U.S. President-elect Trump's pick to be the next ambassador" to Denmark. She quoted [[Greenland]]'s prime minister, [[Múte Egede]]: "We don't want to be Americans."[^2] The Copenhagen Post wrote that [[Donald Trump]] nominated Howery in December 2024.[^11]

At the committee's hearing on May 8, 2025, Howery's prepared statement named three priorities: shared security, the economic relationship, and "Arctic security, particularly in Greenland". It referred to the 1951 defense agreement with Denmark, to [[Pituffik Space Base]] as "the northernmost U.S. military base", and to the reopening of the American consulate in Nuuk in 2020, and stated that "recent conversations about this reality, and the appropriate U.S. role in Greenland's future, have been spirited". It also said he would "work closely with Danish leaders to strengthen defense, law enforcement, and intelligence cooperation".[^5]

The Senate confirmed him on October 7, 2025 in roll call 547, a single en bloc vote of 51 to 47 on 107 nominations provided for under Senate Resolution 412. The package included his nomination, "Kenneth Howery, of Texas, to be Ambassador to the Kingdom of Denmark", with those of [[Sergio Gor]] (India), [[Herschel Walker]] (Bahamas), [[Jacob Helberg]] (Under Secretary of State for Economic Growth) and [[Paul Atkins]] (Securities and Exchange Commission).[^4] The Copenhagen Post reported that the official residence in Copenhagen was under renovation.[^11] The Local, carrying a Ritzau report of November 10, 2025, wrote that Howery had arrived in Denmark the previous week and had an audience with King [[Frederik X of Denmark|Frederik]] on November 5.[^12]

### Greenland Interference Reports

The Local reported that the Danish broadcaster [[DR (broadcaster)|DR]] had reported in August 2025 "that at least three US officials close to US President Donald Trump have been involved in 'interference' activities in Greenland aimed at creating division with Denmark", including gathering information on past tensions between Greenland and Denmark and identifying Greenlanders sympathetic to an American takeover. The Danish intelligence service [[PET]] had stated that Greenland was the target of such campaigns. Foreign minister [[Lars Løkke Rasmussen]] told AFP in August that "any attempt to interfere in the internal affairs of the kingdom will of course be unacceptable" and summoned the American chargé d'affaires; Denmark had also summoned the chargé in May 2025 after the [[Wall Street Journal]] reported that the United States wanted to increase espionage in Greenland. The State Department said at the time: "The United States respects the right of the people of Greenland to determine their own future." Prime Minister [[Mette Frederiksen]] had noted that the United States had not explicitly denied involvement.[^12]

In an interview with the newspaper [[Børsen]] reported on November 10, 2025, Howery stated that he had no direct knowledge of any interference campaign orchestrated by the United States in Greenland and did not believe the United States government to be behind it, and said: "There are individual Americans who could have an interest in Greenland. But the US government does not direct or control the actions of private individuals."[^12]

On November 25, 2025, with his employer given as the [[State Department|U.S. Department of State]] and his occupation as ambassador, Federal Election Commission records list contributions from Howery of 7,000 dollars to Whatley for Senate (part redesignated), 5,000 dollars to North Carolina First, and corresponding WinRed entries.[^6]
