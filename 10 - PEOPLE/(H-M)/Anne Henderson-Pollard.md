Anne Alicia Henderson was born in 1955 in Montreal, [[Canada]], to a family with Israeli connections. She met [[Jonathan Pollard]] while both were working in Washington, D.C., and they married on August 4, 1985, two months before Jonathan's arrest. The marriage occurred after the espionage operation was already underway, and Anne subsequently became directly involved in it.[^1]

### Role in the Pollard Espionage Operation

Anne Henderson-Pollard's participation in Jonathan's espionage for [[LAKAM]] went beyond knowledge of her husband's activities. According to court documents and the statements of investigators, she:

- Photocopied classified documents that Jonathan brought home from his workplace
- Assisted in preparing intelligence materials for transfer
- Had documents stored in the couple's apartment
- Accompanied Jonathan to meetings with his Israeli handlers on at least one occasion

Her direct participation made her criminally liable under statutes covering possession and transfer of classified materials. Prosecutors described her as an active co-conspirator in certain aspects of the operation, not merely a passive spouse with knowledge.[^1]

### Arrest and Prosecution

Anne Pollard was arrested on November 22, 1985, a day after her husband, as an accessory.[^2][^3] The couple had attempted to seek asylum at the embassy before Jonathan's arrest; Anne was present at the embassy entrance during those events.

The 1992 opinion records that the government refused to enter a plea agreement with her unless her husband pleaded guilty as well.[^2] She pleaded guilty in 1986. The counterintelligence history describes the plea as "being an accessory after the fact to possession of national defense documents and conspiring to receive embezzled government property"; Hersh and Olive describe the two counts as conspiracy to receive stolen government property and possession of stolen government property.[^1][^3] Each of her offenses "carried a maximum penalty of five years imprisonment," and the government agreed to recommend that her sentences run concurrently.[^3] The sentencing judge gave her five years, against Jonathan's life imprisonment.[^2] Hersh and Olive describe the five years as probation to be served in a residential treatment center, and attribute the lighter sentence to her reduced culpability and her cooperation with the government during debriefings.[^1]

### Court Account of Her Role

The 1992 appellate opinion gives a narrower account of her part. It records that when Pollard was questioned after the stop, he used the code word "cactus" in telephone calls to her, and that she then removed a suitcase of classified documents from the apartment and contacted his Israeli handlers to say that he was in trouble. The court wrote: "This was her only active involvement in Pollard's espionage."[^2] The photocopying and document-storage account in the section above is from Hersh and Olive and stands beside the court's account.

### Illness and Later Life

The 1992 opinion records that she had, for several years, suffered from a debilitating gastrointestinal disorder that had not been accurately diagnosed before her arrest. It records that during her stay in the D.C. jail she was seriously ill, losing forty pounds over three months, and that she was released on bail in February 1986.[^2]

In 1988 the Justice Department allowed her to travel to Israel, where she remained under conditions negotiated between U.S. and Israeli authorities.[^1] The counterintelligence history, which discusses events through the 1997 clemency denial, records: "After Anne was released from prison, she went to Israel to reside. She is now divorced from her husband."[^3]

Jonathan Pollard remained incarcerated in the United States. He married Elaine Zeitz in 1994. He was released on parole in November 2015 after serving 30 years, and emigrated to Israel in December 2020.
