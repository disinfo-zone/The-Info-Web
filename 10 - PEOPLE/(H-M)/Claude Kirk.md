Claude R. Kirk Jr. was the governor of [[Florida]] from January 1967 to January 1971, the first Republican to hold the office since Reconstruction.[^1]

### The War on Crime

In his inaugural address of January 3, 1967, Kirk announced a "war on crime" and named [[George R. Wackenhut]] its director at a dollar a year. The detectives and offices were supplied by the [[Wackenhut Corporation]] of [[Coral Gables, Florida|Coral Gables]] under a contract reported at 500,000 dollars, to be paid not by the state but by the donations of citizens. Against what [[Time (magazine)|Time]] called "anguished cries of 'Nazism!' and 'Dictatorship!'" the experiment, the magazine wrote, "succeeded to the extent of pointing up the need for a regular state police force and led to formation of the first statewide law-enforcement bureau." It produced more than eighty indictments, some of them of local officials. Time put the unpaid bill owed to the company at more than 300,000 dollars.[^1]

On January 13, 1967, headquarters of the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]] instructed its Florida offices to give Kirk's men nothing: "The Bureau does not recognize the Wackenhut Corporation as a properly constituted law enforcement agency." The order was repeated on April 3, after Kirk had called on the attorney general. That month the [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]], which had used Wackenhut's company since 1961 to give cover to its officers in [[Miami]], withdrew his covert security approval "due to the notoriety that Mr. Wackenhut had received in connection with his activities in Florida." The crime reporter [[Hank Messick]], hired from the [[Miami Herald]], resigned from the company after six weeks, calling the campaign "a cruel hoax" and objecting that Wackenhut kept as a client a former Bahamian minister with gambling interests. The company told the legislature that the "overlord of the whole state's criminal syndicates" was [[Santos Trafficante, Jr.|Santo Trafficante]].[^2][^3]
