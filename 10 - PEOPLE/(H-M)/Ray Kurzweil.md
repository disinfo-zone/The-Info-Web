Ray Kurzweil is an American inventor, author and futurist. In *The Age of Spiritual Machines* (1999) and *The Singularity Is Near* (2005) he popularized the [[Singularitarianism|technological singularity]], the idea, named by Vernor Vinge in 1993, of a point at which machine intelligence outruns human understanding, and set out a "law of accelerating returns" with forecasts for 2029 and 2045.[^1] On December 14, 2012, he announced that [[Google]] had hired him as a director of engineering focused on machine learning and language processing.[^2]

### The Singularity Summit and Singularity University

In 2006 [[Peter Thiel]], who had begun funding [[Eliezer Yudkowsky]]'s Singularity Institute for Artificial Intelligence the year before, joined Yudkowsky and Kurzweil in founding the [[Singularity Summit]], first held at Stanford; in December 2012 Singularity University acquired the summit and the institute sold it the "Singularity" name, and the institute became the [[Machine Intelligence Research Institute]].[^1] Singularity University was founded on September 20, 2008, at a founders conference hosted by S. Pete Worden, director of the [[NASA]] Ames Research Center, by a group that included Kurzweil, the X Prize chairman [[Peter Diamandis]], and Robert Richards and Michael Simpson of the International Space University; NASA later clarified that its personnel were not founders. It was housed at the NASA Research Park at Ames.[^3]

Thiel later turned on the risk wing of the movement: in 2025 he named Yudkowsky one of the "legionnaires of the Antichrist" in a private lecture series.[^4] [[Émile P. Torres]] and [[Timnit Gebru]] grouped Kurzweil's singularitarianism with transhumanism, extropianism, cosmism, rationalism, effective altruism and longtermism in their [[TESCREAL]] bundle.[^5]
