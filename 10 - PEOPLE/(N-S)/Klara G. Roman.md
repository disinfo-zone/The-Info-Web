Klara G. Roman (1881-1962) was a [[Budapest]]-born graphologist and clinical psychologist who taught graphology at the [[New School for Social Research]] in [[New York City]] from 1948 until shortly before her death. The posthumous Encyclopedia of the Written Word thanks the [[Society for the Investigation of Human Ecology|Human Ecology Fund]] among two foundations that helped her. In 1961 [[Project MKUltra|MKULTRA]] Subproject 137 paid $2,000 through the Fund toward the lexicon, approved under the signature of [[Sidney Gottlieb]]; the CIA's memorandum describes her as "neither cleared nor witting" of the source.

### Budapest and Emigration

Roman was born and raised in Budapest. She graduated in 1928 from the [[Hungarian Royal State Institute for Abnormal Psychology]] and later founded the [[Hungarian Institute of Writing Research]].[^1] The New School Archives state that she suffered severe deprivation and lost her husband to the Nazis during the Second World War, and that she immigrated to the United States in 1947.[^1] The biographical note is drawn from the introduction by Rose Wolfson to her Encyclopedia.[^1]

In the preface to her 1954 book, Roman wrote that she had "the privilege of working for more than two decades on research projects instituted by government agencies and clinical institutions in Europe, notably in Budapest." The preface credits work at [[Michael Reese Hospital]] in [[Chicago]], "to which she was called by Dr. [[Franz Alexander]]," and her students at the New School.[^2]

### Teaching at the New School

Roman was invited to offer a course at the New School in 1948.[^1] The school's archive holds the course "Graphology: A Psychological Method for the Study of Personality," dated 1949 and 1959.[^3] Her 1954 preface credits "her unusually mature students at the New School for Social Research, New York" with "further excellent opportunities for observation and testing."[^2] She continued to teach until shortly before her death and entrusted her classes to a protege, Daniel S. Anthony.[^1] The Encyclopedia's acknowledgments thank Anthony "of The New School."[^4]

Roman served as research consultant to the treatment clinic of the [[Manhattan Criminal Court]] and as clinical psychologist and graphologist for the [[Children's Health Council of the Mid-Peninsula]] in [[Palo Alto]], California.[^1] She invented the "graphodyne," an instrument for measuring writing speed and pressure.[^1]

### Books

*Handwriting: A Key to Personality* appeared in 1952 and was published in London by [[Routledge & Kegan Paul]] in 1954.[^1][^2] Its preface states that the method "rests mainly upon psychometrically and experimentally verified observations."[^2] A further book, *Handwriting, etc.*, is dated 1961.[^1]

*Encyclopedia of the Written Word: A Lexicon for Graphology and Other Aspects of Writing* was "written and compiled by Klara G. Roman" and "edited by Rose Wolfson and Maurice Edwards" (xviii, 550 pages, [[F. Ungar Publishing Company]], New York, 1968).[^5] The New School holds the editors' working files for it, dated 1962 to 1969.[^3] Edwards, a stage actor who had worked for Roman helping to put her lectures and writings into English, edited it after her death.[^1][^6]

### Funding of the Encyclopedia

The Encyclopedia's acknowledgments thank "the two foundations that helped Klara G. Roman during the years she worked on this Encyclopedia: The Handwriting Institute and The Human Ecology Fund," and, among individuals, Huntington Hartford and Dr. Clarence A. Tripp "of The Handwriting Institute," Dr. Walter P. Pasternak "of the Human Ecology Fund," and Daniel S. Anthony "of The New School for Social Research and former assistant to Mrs. Roman."[^4] The editors' account, signed Rose Wolfson, states that "Sometime later, The Human Ecology Fund in New York City contributed research funds for the preparation of this book" and that "From 1959 to the beginning of 1962, Klara Roman labored on it."[^4] A preface in the first person states that The Handwriting Institute subsidized Roman for the work and retained an editor to help her with it.[^4] The preface also states that when she died the Encyclopedia was about eighty-five per cent finished.[^4]

Pasternak is listed as technical writer in the 1957 annual report of the Society for the Investigation of Human Ecology, which was renamed the Human Ecology Fund in 1961.[^7][^8]

### Death

The New School Archives date her death to 1962.[^1] Wolfson's account in the Encyclopedia says she found a letter from Roman dated July 31, in which Roman wrote "I have lost my way," and that Roman "had died on August 9"; the same passage places the year at 1962.[^4] A memorial edition of the periodical [[Handwriting Analysts News]], titled for her, appeared in 1963, and the school holds a copy.[^3]

### MKULTRA Subproject 137

A file in the [[John R. Everett]] records at the New School, labeled "Central Intelligence Agency (CIA), 1977-1978," describes "two projects conducted at the New School in 1962 that received funding through MKUltra," the second of which "concerned graphology and psychopathologies."[^9]

The [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]]'s released file for MKULTRA Subproject 137 names no contractor. Its memorandum of August 24, 1961 states that the purpose of the subproject was "to support the work of Dr." [redacted], whose institution is also redacted, "in completing a psycholexicon on handwriting." It continues: "Dr." [redacted] "has an international reputation for her work in graphology and speech. She is advanced in years and her health is failing. It is important that her manuscript, now eighty percent complete, be finished in order not to waste many tedious years of effort." The memorandum describes the Technical Services Division's "general requirement to further the Agency's capability for the indirect assessment of personality," states that the project "will be funded through the" [redacted] "for security and cover purposes," and states that the investigator was "neither cleared nor witting of Agency sponsorship."[^10]

The attached proposal, "A Proposal for Support to Complete the Manuscript of a Psycholexicon on Writing and the Written Word," requests $2,000, of which $1,750 was for a half-time research assistant-secretary and $250 for photographic services, with no salary for the principal investigator. It states that "no compendium of terms exists for student or layman which deals with the history and evolution of handwriting, its psychology and psychopathology," that the manuscript was "presently 80 per cent complete," and that twelve more months were needed.[^10] The curriculum vitae attached to the proposal has the name redacted. Its entries are set beside her recorded biography below.

The invoice for Subproject 137 shows a $2,000 treasurer's check, number 185479, dated September 11, 1961, payable to a redacted institution, with a receipt dated October 4, 1961. The accounting memorandum of February 1963 states: "Dr." [redacted] "died in 1962 and did not send in an accounting." It records that she had "four editorial assistants during the period," that "We have two letters from Dr." [redacted] "acknowledging receipt of the money and a letter from her brother stating that the manuscript is almost finished," and that "The Fund will receive its usual acknowledgement in the publication of the manuscript." Two men whose names are redacted "concur that no further accounting is possible." A certification states that the recipient "died in 1962 without rendering an accounting" and that "a manuscript has been published and value received." The accounting form for the $2,000 advance of September 11, 1961 was approved on March 10, 1964 over the signature of Sidney Gottlieb, marked "DC/TSD."[^10] The F. Ungar Publishing Company published the Encyclopedia of the Written Word in 1968.[^5]

The Human Ecology Fund's 1961-1963 report, as tabulated by anthropologist David H. Price, lists a grant of $2,000 for "Book: The Psychology of Writing" to "Roman, Klara G." in the field of psychology.[^11]

The investigator of Subproject 137 was Roman. The released file redacts her name, but its curriculum vitae matches her record entry for entry, the Everett file places the graphology project at the New School in 1962, the Human Ecology Fund's report lists the $2,000 under her name, and her Encyclopedia thanks the Fund and its technical writer. The 1961 memorandum states that she was neither cleared nor witting of Agency sponsorship.
