[[Menachem Schneerson]], also known as the Lubavitcher Rebbe, was a prominent orthodox Jewish leader based in Brooklyn, [[New York City]]. According to [[Ari Ben-Menashe]], his financial institutions were enlisted by the [[Likud Party]]-controlled [[Iran-Israel Joint Committee]] to funnel money for housing projects in the [[West Bank]] and [[Gaza Strip]] for Jewish settlers, drawing from the slush fund generated by arms sales to [[Iran]].[^1]

Ben-Menashe wrote that large amounts of money were funneled through his institutions to [[Drexel Burnham]], a brokerage house where [[Michael Milken]] built his junk-bond fortune, and that until 1987 some of the profits from the slush fund were used to finance the Lubavitcher yeshivahs (colleges).[^1]

He wrote that in 1987 the committee and Schneerson parted ways, partly due to questions regarding his allegiance to the Likud Party and the emergence of the orthodox [[Shas Party]], which was at odds with the Rebbe; that funds previously directed to the Lubavitcher yeshivahs were redirected to the Shas yeshivahs; and that the committee also decided to withdraw funds from Drexel and from Schneerson's control due to concerns that the funds might be frozen if relations between [[Israel]] and the [[United States|U.S.]] deteriorated, contributing to Drexel's eventual downfall.[^1]
