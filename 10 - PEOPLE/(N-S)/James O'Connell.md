James Patrick O'Connell was born in [[New York City|New York]] in 1917 and graduated from [[St. John's University]] in 1939. He served with the Navy in the Atlantic, was a special agent of the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]] from 1946 to 1951, and joined the [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]] as a security officer in 1951.[^1]

### Roselli

In September 1960 [[Sheffield Edwards]], the director of security, put O'Connell to the operation against [[Fidel Castro]] arranged through [[Robert Maheu]]. Under the name Jim Olds he was introduced to [[Johnny Roselli]] and was his case officer from September 14, 1960, until May 1962, when [[William Harvey]] took him over. Roselli brought in [[Sam Giancana]] and [[Santos Trafficante, Jr.|Santos Trafficante]]. Poison pills from the agency's technical division went to [[Cuba]] by way of [[Juan Orta]] and later [[Tony Varona]]. Edwards recorded the operation for Attorney General [[Robert F. Kennedy|Robert Kennedy]] on May 14, 1962.[^2][^3]

### Deputy Director of Security

By 1968 O'Connell was deputy director of security. On October 15 of that year he signed the [[CIA Office of Security|Office of Security]]'s memorandum on [[Arthur Hanes]], the former mayor of [[Birmingham, Alabama|Birmingham]] then representing [[James Earl Ray]], recording Hanes's service as a confidential correspondent from 1952 to 1959 and his part in recruiting [[Alabama Air National Guard|Alabama Guard]] pilots for the [[Bay of Pigs Invasion|Bay of Pigs]]. He retired in 1973 with the Intelligence Medal of Merit, worked as a security consultant, and died at [[Arlington, Virginia|Arlington]] on February 15, 1992.[^1][^4]
