John Marlan Poindexter was born August 12, 1936. He graduated first in his class from the [[U.S. Naval Academy]] in 1958 and earned a PhD in nuclear physics from the [[California Institute of Technology]] in 1964. He pursued a naval career that included service in the Pentagon's [[National Security Council]] staff, rising to military assistant to President Reagan's National Security Advisers before assuming the position himself.[^1]

### National Security Adviser

Poindexter succeeded Robert McFarlane as National Security Adviser in December 1985. His tenure coincided precisely with the critical period of the Iran-Contra operations: the arms-for-hostages sales to Iran through Israeli intermediaries in 1985-1986, the diversion of profits to the Nicaraguan Contras by NSC staff member [[Oliver North]], and the collapse of the arrangement in October-November 1986. Poindexter was the official who approved North's diversion memo - the document authorizing the use of Iran arms sale profits to fund the Contras - and the Tower Commission found that he had not informed President Reagan of the diversion.[^1]

Poindexter resigned on November 25, 1986, the same day North was fired, after Attorney General Meese's announcement that the Iran arms sale profits had been diverted to the Contras. His resignation and North's termination were the first public accountability actions in the Iran-Contra affair.[^1]

After the September 1986 discussion between Oliver North and Israeli Defense Minister [[Yitzhak Rabin]] of an Israeli transfer of Soviet bloc weapons to the Contras, Poindexter sanctioned the offer: "It can be a private deal between Dick [Secord] and Rabin that we bless." He later cautioned that "[a]bsolutely nobody else should know about this. Rabin should not say anything to anybody else except you or me." When North's memorandum briefing the President for a visit by Prime Minister [[Shimon Peres]] recommended that the President thank Peres for the Israeli bloc arms, Poindexter penciled next to the sentence: "Rabin. Very tightly held."[^2]

### Congressional Testimony and Criminal Proceedings

Poindexter testified before the joint congressional [[Iran-Contra Affair|Iran-Contra]] committees under a grant of immunity in July 1987. He stated that he had authorized the diversion of funds and had not told [[Ronald Reagan|Reagan]] about it. He said he made the decision himself because he did not want to place the President in the position of having approved an operation that violated the [[Boland Amendment]]'s prohibition on Contra assistance. This testimony was the central account of executive branch knowledge and authorization in the entire Iran-Contra affair.[^1]

Independent Counsel [[Lawrence Walsh]] indicted Poindexter in March 1988 on charges of conspiracy, obstruction of Congress, making false statements, and destroying documents. The case was tried in 1990 after North's conviction. Poindexter was convicted on five counts in April 1990 and sentenced to six months in prison.[^3]

The D.C. Circuit Court of Appeals reversed Poindexter's conviction in November 1991, on the same grounds that had reversed North's: that witnesses against him had been exposed to his immunized congressional testimony, potentially tainting their accounts in ways the government could not demonstrate had not occurred. Walsh declined to retry the case.[^3]

### Total Information Awareness

[[DARPA]] created the [[Information Awareness Office]] in mid-January 2002, under the [[George W. Bush|Bush]] administration, and Poindexter directed it.[^4] The office, a research program focused on mining large-scale databases for intelligence indicators, developed the Total Information Awareness (TIA) system, which proposed aggregating commercial, government, and intelligence databases to identify patterns predictive of terrorist activity.

When TIA became publicly known in late 2002, it generated intense opposition from civil liberties organizations and members of Congress, who argued that the system constituted a mass domestic surveillance infrastructure. Poindexter's own Iran-Contra history (including his conviction and its reversal) was cited repeatedly in the congressional debate over TIA. On August 12, 2003 Poindexter submitted a five-page letter of resignation to DARPA director [[Anthony Tether]], effective August 29.[^4] The conference report on the fiscal 2004 defense appropriations act provided in Section 8131 that none of the funds may be obligated "for the Terrorism Information Awareness Program," excepting processing, analysis and collaboration tools for counterterrorism foreign intelligence funded in the National Foreign Intelligence Program, and its joint explanatory statement states that the conferees "direct that the Office be terminated immediately."[^5]

The joint explanatory statement lists four projects previously under the office that may continue under DARPA: Bio-Event Advanced Leading Indicator Recognition Technology, Rapid Analytic Wargaming, Wargaming the Asymmetric Environment, and Automated Speech and Text Exploitation in Multiple Languages, including Babylon and Symphony.[^5]

DARPA's May 20, 2003 report to Congress on Terrorism Information Awareness lists FutureMAP, the Information Awareness Office's prediction-market program, among TIA's decision-support projects.[^6] On July 28, 2003 Senator [[Ron Wyden]] told reporters that the program's [[Policy Analysis Market]] was under Poindexter's supervision, and Senator [[John Warner]] said the next day that Poindexter and DARPA director Anthony Tether had personally reviewed it.[^7][^8] Nextgov reported that Poindexter approved FutureMAP and that he was not leading the office when the original solicitation was answered; [[Robin Hanson]], who designed the market, wrote that he had never met him.[^9][^10] The Pentagon abandoned the market on July 29, 2003, and Poindexter's resignation letter to Tether is dated August 12, 2003.[^8][^11]
