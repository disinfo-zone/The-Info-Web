Major William L. Stoner, GS, of the Army assistant chief of staff for intelligence's office (DAMI-ISH), was listed as "Coordinator" of the [[Grill Flame Scientific Evaluation Committee]] under [[Manfred Gale]] in 1979 and attended the Defense Department's Grill Flame committee under [[Jack Vorona]] as "Major Bill Stoner" on November 27, 1979. His memorandum of conversation on Gale's briefing of January 14, 1980, ran through Gale's oral claims one by one: that Gale "has, in briefing the report here and elsewhere, done a great deal of verbalizing (shading) issues, personal opinion, 'faction,' and general guesswork not contained anywhere in the report"; that [[Robert Jahn]] at [[Princeton University|Princeton]] "does not have a drop dead time two years out"; that [[Ray Hyman]] "was rejected not because of any action by Gale, but because of a protest memo from MG [[Edmund Thompson|Thompson]] to Dr [[Walter LaBerge|LaBerge]]"; that Gale's "over 5000 experiments" was "an unadulterated W.A.G. (wild guess)" that "commingled" [[United States Army Intelligence and Security Command|INSCOM]]'s "300-odd runs and SRI's 1972 work," "disregarding the fact that INSCOM's operational tests have been producing some amazing things"; that Gale's remarks on the Surgeon General rested on "a cribbed copy of the Surgeon General's comments. Question I have is who gave it to him," and that "because of prior intelligence problems (e.g., LSD case) two members of the Surgeon General's review panel are dead set against this kind of work," one of them the panel chairman, "in a position to drag feet, (two months to get the recommendations out) perform a little sub rosa disinformation on the side, etc."; and, of Gale's "the GRILL FLAME Working Group is in a shambles," that "as you know I am less than thrilled by the Working Group's leadership, but categorically deny that the Group is in shambles." His conclusion: "the Gale report is big, but weak," Gale "has gone far beyond that charter, impinging on the ACSI's responsibility for total program management," and "should desist from brokering it around the Pentagon"; Vorona's memorandum to Lieutenant General [[Eugene Tighe|Tighe]] of January 11 "should stand unless we are willing to watch Gale work it out to his satisfaction, delivering us a fait accompli."[^1]

On January 25, 1980, Gale asked Stoner to distribute the 27 copies of the report, and "in accordance with the security guide for this program, your office is designated as Office of Record for all reports, as well as for the authorization and release of any additional copies."[^2]
