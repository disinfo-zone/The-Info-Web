Candida Royalle (born Candice Marion Vadala in [[Brooklyn]]; died September 7, 2015) was an adult film actress of the 1970s who in 1984 founded [[Femme Productions]] to produce "adult films from a woman's perspective." She died of ovarian cancer at her home in [[Mattituck, New York]], at 64.[^1]

### Career

Royalle performed in the mid-1970s in [[San Francisco]] and appeared in adult films including *Thoroughly Amorous Amy* and *Fascination* before moving to writing, producing and directing.[^1][^2] Femme Productions released 18 films between 1984 and 2007, 13 of them directed by Royalle; its best known included *Three Daughters* (1986) and *Sensual Escape* (1991). Two others, *Lovers* (1993) and *Lovers 2* (1994), were directed by [[R.C. Horsch]] under the name "RC Hürsch."[^2][^3] In the 1990s she arranged distribution of Femme titles through the adult-goods company [[Adam and Eve (company)|Adam and Eve]], with a line of vibrators. Her 1992 film *Revelations* she described as "my response to the right-wing attacks on the adult industry."[^2] According to the performer [[Annie Sprinkle]], she co-founded Club 90, a support group of adult film performers, in about 1985.[^1]

### Free Expression

In 1992 she founded, according to *Reason,* or was a founding member of, according to *The Suffolk Times,* [[Feminists for Free Expression]], a nonprofit that opposed censorship legislation and defended free speech in courts, on campuses and in the press.[^1][^2] Femme was founded, *Reason* noted, at the time [[Andrea Dworkin]] and [[Catharine MacKinnon]] were testifying to legislators that pornography was abusive by nature. Royalle wrote *How to Tell a Naked Man What to Do* (2004) and in 2014 received a doctorate in human sexuality from the Institute for the Advanced Study of Human Sexuality.[^1][^2]
