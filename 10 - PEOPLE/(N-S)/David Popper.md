David H. Popper was the [[United States]] ambassador to [[Chile]] from February 1974 to May 1977, arriving five months after the coup.[^1]

### Human Rights

Between April 1974 and February 1975 Popper made ten approaches to Chilean officials on human rights. On December 13, 1974, he told the foreign minister, Admiral [[Patricio Carvajal]], that "Congressional critics might be impressed and progress be possible if the GOC were to move towards restoration of normality regarding the applicability of traditional Chilean legal requirements and the state of siege, restoration of habeas corpus, a role for civil court review of military sentences and convincing assurances regarding alleged practice of torture." On February 3, 1975, he told the interior minister, General [[César Benavides]], that "the release of a few well known prisoners" was "probably not sufficient to restore the GOC's image." Beside those two entries on the list sent him by [[William D. Rogers]], [[Henry A. Kissinger|Henry Kissinger]] wrote: "What was this about?" On an earlier cable Kissinger had written: "Tell Popper to cut out the political science lectures."[^2][^3]

### Condor

The department's cable of August 23, 1976, told him to see the head of state about "government planned and directed assassinations within and outside the territory of Condor members." Popper answered the next day: "While I appreciate importance of objective sought in reftel, I seriously doubt that an approach to President Pinochet is the best way to achieve it." Given [[Augusto Pinochet]]'s "sensitivity regarding pressures by USG, he might well take as an insult any inference that he was connected with such assassination plots. It is quite possible, even probable, that Pinochet has no knowledge whatever of Operation Condor. Particularly of its more questionable aspects." He proposed instead that the [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]] station chief convey the message to Colonel [[Manuel Contreras]] of [[DINA]] "in the context of having heard a rumor which he cannot believe, but which if substantiated would be disastrous for the perpetrators." On August 27 [[Harry Shlaudeman]] told CIA officers that an approach to Pinochet would be futile. [[William Luers]] pressed Popper through September to deliver the message. On September 20 the ambassadors were told to take no further action. [[Orlando Letelier]] was killed in Washington the next day.[^4][^5]

On September 13 Popper reported that Pinochet's anniversary speech "contains little of encouragement to us," and that the release of 205 prisoners that day "does not indicate that detentions and 'disappearances' have ceased to occur in Chile. They still do occur." In early October, "after consulting with Ambassador Popper," the station chief "confronted the Chilean head of DINA, Colonel Manuel Contreras, with U.S. knowledge of Condor's existence. Colonel Contreras denied that our information of Condor was accurate and refused to admit that one of its objectives was to operate against terrorists in Europe. Recent clandestinely acquired information indicates that plans for Condor are continuing."[^6][^7]
