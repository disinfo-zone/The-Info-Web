Alec Stapp received a bachelor's degree from the [[University of Arizona]] and a master's degree in economics from [[George Mason University]], where he was a graduate research fellow at the [[Mercatus Center]]. He was a technology policy fellow at the [[Niskanen Center]], a research fellow at the [[International Center for Law and Economics]] and director of technology policy at the [[Progressive Policy Institute]], and wrote for *[[The Washington Post]]*, *[[The Atlantic]]*, *[[MIT Technology Review]]* and *[[Politico]]*.[^1]

### Institute for Progress

Stapp and [[Caleb Watney]] co-founded the [[Institute for Progress]] (IFP) in 2021 and announced it on January 20, 2022, with the mission "to accelerate scientific, technological, and industrial progress while safeguarding humanity's future," applying "a framework we like to borrow from the effective altruism community" of choosing issues "that are important, neglected, and tractable."[^2][^3] [[Open Philanthropy]] (later renamed Coefficient Giving)[^4] granted IFP 1.6 million dollars in October 2021, "to conduct research and advocacy on policies relevant to a number of our grantmaking areas," and 9 million dollars over three years in March 2023.[^5] IFP's returns report Stapp's pay as co-chief executive as 54,231 dollars for 2021, 163,125 dollars for 2022, 202,500 dollars for 2023 and 246,008 dollars for 2024.[^6] After Watney left in 2026 to become managing director of public policy at Coefficient Giving, Stapp was listed as IFP's sole chief executive.[^3][^7]

His IFP writing includes "Why [[SpaceX]] Is an Underrated Strategic National Asset for the U.S." (March 2022); "The Case for High-Skilled Immigration Reform (And How to Make It Happen)" (August 2022, with Jeremy Neufeld); papers on permitting reform and environmental review with [[Brian Potter]], Arnab Datta and Aidan Mackenzie (2022 to 2024); responses to the [[Office of Science and Technology Policy]] on research with dangerous pathogens (October 2023) and to the [[Environmental Protection Agency]] on far-UVC systems for pandemic prevention (December 2022); a January 2024 paper urging Congress to strengthen the [[Federal Select Agent Program]]; and "Biosecurity in the Trump Era" (November 2024).[^1]
