Gerald G. Oplinger was an aide to [[Zbigniew Brzezinski]] for global issues. He had retired from the Foreign Service and worked at the [[Nuclear Regulatory Commission|NRC]] before joining Brzezinski's staff. Oplinger was summoned back to an urgent meeting in the [[White House]] situation room after a [[VELA Satellite]] recorded a double flash of light on September 22, 1979, from a region that included the southern Atlantic, the Indian Ocean and the southern tip of Africa.[^1] [[Seymour Hersh]] wrote that Oplinger recalled that Brzezinski was present at the meeting, where the [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]] and [[Defense Intelligence Agency|DIA]] stated that the odds were at least ninety percent that it had been a nuclear explosion, and that Oplinger personally had no doubt that it was a nuclear event.[^2] The mini-Special Coordination Committee meeting of September 22, 1979 in the Situation Room is summarized in the Foreign Relations of the United States, which lists [[Henry Owen]], Odom, Schecter and Funk for the National Security Council.[^3]

In a memorandum of January 23, 1980 to Owen, Oplinger wrote that he had told the State Department to put the press release and congressional consultations on hold, and that the [[Department of Energy]], the [[Department of Defense]] and technical people within the intelligence community had "sufficient doubts about the Press panel report to make it very dangerous to proceed without another look." He wrote that [[John Deutch]] and Dan Murphy felt strongly that unless the questions were given "a further airing, there will be continued internal dissent and an appearance that policy people (including the White House) did a whitewash for political reasons."[^4]
