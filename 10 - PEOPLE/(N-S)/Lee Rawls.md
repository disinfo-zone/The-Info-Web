W. Lee Rawls (Newport, Rhode Island, to December 2010, aged 66) was the [[Department of Justice]]'s assistant attorney general for legislative affairs under [[Richard Thornburgh]], the department's liaison to Congress in the years when the [[House Committee on the Judiciary]] under [[Jack Brooks]] was demanding [[INSLAW]] documents and the [[House Banking Committee]] under Henry Gonzalez was opening its inquiry into the [[Banca Nazionale del Lavoro]] loans to [[Iraq]]. A Princeton graduate of 1966 with a law degree from [[George Washington University]], he began as a legislative specialist at the Environmental Protection Agency, was chief of staff to Senator [[Pete Domenici]] by 1975, and served as staff director of the [[Senate Judiciary Committee]] before joining the department.[^1]

### Thornburgh's Congress Man

In 1990, as Gonzalez prepared a full-scale investigation of the [[Atlanta]] branch of BNL, Rawls wrote Thornburgh a briefing memorandum: "Our best attempt to thwart any further congressional inquiry by the House Banking Committee into this case is to have you contact Chairman Gonzalez directly." Gonzalez declined to take the attorney general's call, and Thornburgh wrote to him of his "profound disappointment." In May and July 1991 Rawls corresponded on the department's behalf with the House Judiciary Committee during its INSLAW investigation.[^2][^3]

### Later Career

Rawls was chief of staff to Senate Majority Leader Bill Frist from 2003 to 2005 and then chief of staff and senior counsel to [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]] Director [[Robert Mueller]] until 2009. In private practice he was a partner at Vinson & Elkins and managing partner of the Washington office of Howard Baker's firm, Baker Donelson; he taught at the National Defense University and the College of William and Mary, was a public policy scholar at the Woodrow Wilson Center, and in 2009 published In Praise of Deadlock, a defense of the Senate filibuster. He died of acute leukemia in December 2010.[^1]
