Colonel Sheffield Edwards was director of the [[CIA Office of Security|Office of Security]] of the [[Central Intelligence Agency]] in the years of the plots against [[Fidel Castro]].[^1]

### 1960

In August 1960 [[Richard Bissell]], the deputy director for plans, asked Edwards "if he had assets that may assist in a sensitive mission requiring gangster-type action." Edwards took the question to the director, who approved. He chose [[Robert Maheu]], "a cleared source of the Office of Security," as the intermediary, and Maheu produced [[Johnny Roselli]]. Edwards assigned [[James O'Connell]] as case officer. "Sam Gold" and "Joe," whom Roselli brought in, were [[Sam Giancana]] and [[Santos Trafficante, Jr.|Santos Trafficante]]. He informed the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]]'s liaison, [[Sam Papich]], of the agency's contacts with the two.[^1][^2]

### 1962

When Maheu's wiretappers were arrested in [[Las Vegas]] in a job done for Giancana, Edwards asked that prosecution be dropped, and it was. On May 7, 1962, he and the general counsel, [[Lawrence Houston]], briefed Attorney General [[Robert F. Kennedy|Robert Kennedy]]. On May 14 Edwards wrote the memorandum for the record, "prepared at the request of the Attorney General of the United States following a complete oral briefing of him relative to a sensitive CIA operation conducted during the period approximately August 1960 to May 1961," which Houston sent to Kennedy the next day. In 1963 [[Richard Helms]] told Director [[John McCone]] that it was the only written record of the relationship with Giancana. The inspector general found in 1967 that Kennedy "was not told that the gambling syndicate operation had already been reactivated." Edwards testified to the Senate committee on May 30, 1975.[^1][^2][^3]
