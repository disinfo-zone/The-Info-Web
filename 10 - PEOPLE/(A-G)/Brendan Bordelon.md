Brendan Bordelon is an American technology policy reporter at *[[Politico]]*. A native of the [[San Francisco Bay Area]] and a graduate of the [[University of California, Riverside]], he worked at *[[The Daily Caller]]*, for two years as a political reporter at *[[National Review]]*, and at [[Morning Consult]], covering technology and cybersecurity policy, before [[National Journal]] hired him as technology correspondent from March 27, 2017.[^1] He also holds a master's degree from [[George Washington University]].[^2]

### Politico

Bordelon joined Politico on January 25, 2022 to write the *Morning Tech* newsletter.[^2] In January 2023 the global technology editor, [[Steve Heuser]], named him "Tech Influence reporter," covering the industry's influence on politics and policy.[^3] In September 2026 he was one of four authors, with [[Calder McHugh]], [[Christine Mui]] and [[Sam Sutton]], of the newsletter of Politico's new technology policy unit, Decoded.[^4]

### Reporting on Open Philanthropy

On October 13, 2023 Bordelon reported on the policy fellows funded by [[Open Philanthropy]], writing that the [[Horizon Institute for Public Service]] was "a nonprofit that Open Philanthropy effectively created in 2022," that Horizon fellows worked in the offices of Senators [[Martin Heinrich]], [[Mike Rounds]] and [[Todd Young]], and that the [[Center for Security and Emerging Technology]] was "funded almost entirely by Open Philanthropy." He described [[Jason Matheny]] of the [[RAND Corporation]] as "a luminary in the effective altruist community."[^5] On December 15, 2023 he reported on RAND's part in [[Joe Biden]]'s executive order on AI.[^6] *[[Semafor]]* reported in December 2025 that Open Philanthropy had been "bothered by media coverage of it, in particular a series of critical stories from Politico."[^7]
