Stefano Delle Chiaie led [[Avanguardia Nazionale]], which he told an Italian parliamentary commission had "surely more than a thousand" members. A warrant in the [[Piazza Fontana bombing]] investigation was issued for him in 1970, and he was a fugitive from Italy for seventeen years, active, as the commission's president put it to him, in "Spain, Chile, Bolivia and Argentina." He entered Italy four times in that period.[^1]

### Rome, 1975

In 1975 Major [[Raúl Iturriaga Neumann|Raúl Iturriaga]] of [[DINA]] ordered [[Michael Townley]] "to enlist Delle Chiaie's organization in an operation to kill" [[Bernardo Leighton]]. Delle Chiaie advised against better-protected exiles such as [[Carlos Altamirano]]: "Leighton was a better, and easier target." On October 6, 1975, [[Pierluigi Concutelli]] shot Leighton and his wife in the street. "Townley and the Italians celebrated with dinner at Delle Chiaie's apartment, thinking that the Leightons were dead." Weeks later [[Augusto Pinochet]] flew to [[Madrid]] for the funeral of [[Francisco Franco]]. "[[Manuel Contreras|Contreras]] arranged for Delle Chiaie to come to Madrid. Delle Chiaie was given a private audience with Pinochet in his hotel room. Contreras also presented Delle Chiaie with the $5,000 payment."[^2]

### Testimony

Before the commission Delle Chiaie declined to describe his years abroad: "Non le sto a raccontare come ho vissuto da latitante perché è un fatto mio personale." He said a NATO-linked structure built against communism had been turned by "central political power" to "stabilization," and that fringes of the "movimento nazional-rivoluzionario" had been infiltrated and used in the strategy of tension. He read from an internal circular of April 1973 warning members of a "vasto piano provocatorio" to blame the movement for "una serie di ignobili fatti delittuosi," and a letter of June 8, 1974, after the [[Piazza della Loggia bombing]], distancing it from the group of the former partisan Fumagalli. He named the intelligence officers [[Antonio Labruna|Labruna]] and [[Gianadelio Maletti|Maletti]] and [[Giorgio Almirante]] as sources of what he called slander linking his movement to the Interior Ministry's Ufficio Affari Riservati. Asked about American intelligence: "Certo, la Bolivia non era stata riconosciuta dagli Stati Uniti quando c'ero io. Il Cile non aveva una ambasciata americana." "Mi porti un documento che dice questo. Io sono pronto a fare il mea culpa se si portano delle prove concrete." The president called much of it "autodifesa" and set against it the commission's findings on [[Propaganda Due|P2]] and the [[Argo 16]] inquiry.[^1]

He was arrested in [[Caracas]] on March 27, 1987, and sent to Italy two days later. He was acquitted in Rome on February 20, 1989, in the [[Bologna railway station bombing]] case and in 1991 in the Piazza Fontana case.[^3]
