Meir Amit was the former chief of the [[Mossad]], [[Israel]]'s national intelligence agency. He was one of the high-profile Israeli figures who met with [[Uri Geller]], with reports indicating that Geller bent a spoon for him[^1].

### Washington visit, May 31 to June 2, 1967

Major General Amit, then head of the Mossad, visited Washington from May 31 to June 2, 1967. On the afternoon of June 1 he met Secretary of Defense [[Robert S. McNamara]]. Vice Admiral [[Rufus Taylor]], the Deputy Director of Central Intelligence, wrote the memorandum of the conversation, which carries the handling caption "Personal and Eyes Only for the Secretary of Defense and the Director of Central Intelligence."[^2] The memorandum records that Amit described the blockade of the [[Strait of Tiran]] as "window dressing" for a Soviet-backed grand design he called the "Domino Effect," said that time was against Israel, that Israel had mobilized 100,000 people and that a pre-emptive air attack by the United Arab Republic was a main worry, said "there were no differences between the U.S. and the Israelis on the military intelligence picture or its interpretation" and that the Russian story of a planned attack on Syria was "a sheer fabrication," and said his remarks were informal and not an official representation of the Israeli government. McNamara told him he would be seeing the President shortly and would convey his views. After the meeting Amit wondered aloud whether he should have tried to see the President and whether he should stay longer; Taylor told him to go back to Israel as soon as possible.[^2]

At a conference on the war in June 1992, Amit said his objectives had included "to tell the Americans, I, Meir Amit, am going to recommend that our government strike," and said that McNamara told him "it was all right, the president knows that you are here and I have a direct line to the president," and asked how long a war would last, to which Amit answered "Seven days."[^3] [[David S. Robarge]], the CIA's chief historian, writes that [[Richard Helms]] met a senior Israeli official on June 1 who hinted that Israel could no longer avoid a decision, that the official left the United States on June 2, and that Helms wrote an "Eyes Only" letter to Johnson that morning forewarning that Israel would probably start a war within days.[^4]

On June 5, 1967, Amit met Ambassador [[Walworth Barbour]] and the presidential special counsel Harry C. McPherson, Jr. in Tel Aviv. [[Walt Rostow]] told Johnson that Amit argued there had been artillery fire on three Israeli villages and air incursions, and that the Israelis had then "punched all the buttons," and added: "At least that's his story."[^5]
