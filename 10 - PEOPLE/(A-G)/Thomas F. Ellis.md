Thomas F. Ellis was a [[Raleigh, North Carolina|Raleigh]] lawyer and the chief organizer of the political operation of Senator [[Jesse Helms]]. He studied at the [[University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill|University of North Carolina]] and the [[University of Virginia School of Law|University of Virginia]] law school, served as a Navy lieutenant in the [[World War II|Second World War]], was an assistant [[United States]] attorney and special counsel to the [[North Carolina]] governor's advisory committee on education, and was a founder of the firm of [[Maupin, Taylor and Ellis]]. He was North Carolina chairman of [[Ronald Reagan]]'s campaign in 1976, national co-chairman of [[Jack Kemp]]'s in 1992, and an adviser to [[Steve Forbes]]'s in 1996.[^1]

### The Helms Organization

Ellis was chairman of the [[National Congressional Club]], the committee that raised 9.8 million dollars in 1982 and helped raise a record 13.99 million for Helms's reelection in 1984. His partner [[Carter Wrenn]] was its executive director and treasurer. Ellis was the principal stockholder of the tax-exempt [[Educational Support Foundation]], which owned the direct mail company [[Jefferson Marketing]], and chairman of the [[Coalition for Freedom]], "a tax-exempt foundation whose goal is to finance conservative-oriented television programs." The coalition produced "KGB: The Lie and the Truth," shown on August 28, 1982, on two and a half hours of air time donated by [[Ted Turner]], with Helms, Senator [[John East]], and Representative [[Philip Crane]]. The [[Federal Election Commission]] sued over Jefferson Marketing's services to campaigns in 1985. The [[Internal Revenue Service]] later revoked the coalition's exemption for political activity and private benefit to insiders. [[Thomas Farr]], who joined Ellis's firm in 1983, represented the Helms committees.[^2][^3]

### The Pioneer Fund

Ellis joined the board of the [[Pioneer Fund]] in 1973, with the Kinston lawyer [[Marion Parrott]], and left it in 1977. The fund's tax returns show 195,000 dollars to the Coalition for Freedom: 10,000 in 1983, 20,000 in 1984, 30,000 in 1985, 100,000 in 1987, and 35,000 in 1989. The fund's president, [[Harry Weyher]], was the lawyer for [[Fairness in Media]], which Ellis, Wrenn, and [[James Palmer Cain]] formed on November 13, 1984, to urge conservatives to buy stock in [[CBS]]. The network's suit against the group cited the officers' ties to "a controversial tax-exempt foundation, The Pioneer Fund."[^1][^2][^4]

### The 1983 Nomination

Reagan nominated Ellis on February 8, 1983, to the [[Board for International Broadcasting]], which supervised [[Radio Free Europe]] and [[Radio Liberty]]. Forbes was nominated to the same board. At the hearing of the [[Senate Foreign Relations Committee]] on July 26, where his chief critics were Senators [[Paul Tsongas]] and [[Joe Biden|Joseph Biden]], he was challenged on his service on the board of "a group financing studies into theories that whites are genetically superior to blacks," his membership in an all-white country club, a letter circulated in 1976 claiming that President [[Gerald Ford|Ford]] was seeking a black running mate, and a visit to [[South Africa]] paid for by its government. He told the committee: "I do not believe in my heart that I'm a racist." A majority of the committee asked the president to withdraw the name. On July 28 Ellis asked to be withdrawn, in a letter on his firm's stationery distributed by a Helms aide, calling the criticism "an obvious partisan political effort to drive a wedge between you and the black community using me as the instrumentality." He hoped the board would continue to "broadcast the tenets of Christian belief" without "interference from some of those in public office who do not recognize the threat of communist world domination." Reagan replied that he had "acted in a manner consistent with the reputation for honor that you have always possessed." The nomination lapsed on August 4.[^5][^6]

Asked by [[New York Times|The New York Times]] in 2001 whether one race was genetically superior to another, Ellis said: "I have no idea."[^3]
