William Francis "Bill" Birch (1934 to July 2026) was a [[New Zealand]] National Party politician who served 27 years in Parliament and held, by one count, 15 portfolios in 12 years of government, earning the nickname "Minister for Everything."[^1]

### Early Career

Born in Hastings in 1934 and educated at Hamilton Technical College, Birch qualified as a surveyor in 1957, practised in [[Pukekohe]], and was twice the town's deputy mayor before winning the Franklin seat for the National Party in the 1972 election. He became a party whip in 1974 and senior whip in 1975.[^1]

### Energy and Think Big

On December 13, 1978, in the ministry of [[Robert Muldoon]] formed after that year's election, he was appointed "Minister of Energy, Minister of National Development and Minister of Science and Technology."[^2] At the reshuffle of February 12, 1981, he resigned Science and Technology to [[Ian Shearer]] and was appointed Minister of Regional Development in addition.[^3] As Minister of National Development he was at the centre of the government's [[Think Big]] programme of state-led industrialization and energy self-sufficiency, "sometimes against Treasury advice," in the words of Radio New Zealand's obituary.[^1] On July 14, 1981, he and the Minister of Customs, [[Hugh Templeton]], announced a licence for the ethanol distillery of [[Chemical Technology Limited]] at [[Temuka]]; Birch said it would save 250,000 dollars a year in overseas funds.[^4] He resigned the offices of Minister of Energy, Minister of National Development and Minister of Regional Development on July 26, 1984, after National's defeat.[^5]

### Later Career

In opposition Birch was third-ranked in the party and an adviser to its leaders Muldoon, [[Jim McLay]] and [[Jim Bolger]]. After National's return to office he became Minister of Finance in 1993, replacing [[Ruth Richardson]]. He died in July 2026, aged 92; Prime Minister [[Christopher Luxon]] called him a "giant" of the National Party.[^1]
