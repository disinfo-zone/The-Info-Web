Abdulrahman Alfozan is a Saudi national and software engineer who, according to [[OpenAI]], worked on the product team at the company's [[San Francisco]] offices from July 2025 until his arrest. The [[United States Attorney's Office for the Northern District of California]] announced on October 13, 2026 that he was arrested on October 9 and charged with using or attempting to use a device to intercept oral communications. He is also a venture partner at [[RAED Ventures]], an early-stage investor focused on the region.[^1]

### Education

A [[LinkedIn]] profile under his name lists the [[Massachusetts Institute of Technology]] and [[University of California, Berkeley]] among his schools.[^2] The [[CNN]] report on the arrest cites the same profile for both institutions.[^3] RAED Ventures' announcement of his venture role says he "studied computer science and engineering at MIT."[^4] The profile lists MIT projects from 2012 and 2013: MAS.863, a digital fabrication course that he attended in the fall of 2012; Lounge, a web-based housing platform for undergraduate dormitories that placed second in the MIT 2013 iCampus competition; and work at the [[MIT Center for Wireless Networks and Mobile Computing]] on protecting RFID tags through channel randomization.[^2] The profile also lists a poster at EECScon, the first annual MIT Course 6 undergraduate research conference, held April 16, 2013. The conference guide lists the poster as "The automated room project: a multidisciplinary modular design experiment," with the supervisor given as "Self." The abstract describes room automation gadgets built with 3D printing, laser cutting, PCB design, microcontroller programming and networking, among them an automated curtain, a keyless door entry system and music-driven lights.[^5] His LinkedIn profile gives the conference date as January 2013 and titles the poster "Low-Cost Software-Defined Radio for 8-bit Microcontrollers."[^2] The profile also lists a [[King Abdullah University of Science and Technology]] (KAUST) Gifted Students Scholarship, described as a university full scholarship under the KAUST Gifted Students Program,[^6] and a finalist entry at the 2009 [[Intel International Science and Engineering Fair]].[^2] Research internships are listed at KAUST and at [[Nanyang Technological University]] in [[Singapore]].[^2]

The [[San Francisco Standard]] reported that the LinkedIn profile gives a master's degree in data science from UC Berkeley.[^7]

### Saudi Competitions, Scholarships and Talks

Two separate Saudi competitions of spring 2020 appear in his record.

The first is the Saudi COVID-19 Challenge, run by the [[Saudi Data Community]] (المجموعة السعودية للبيانات), which describes itself as a community initiative of data specialists and enthusiasts.[^8] A public GitHub README for another participant's entry states that the challenge was held by the community on March 15, 2020, asked entrants for a report using open COVID-19 data, and had more than 150 participants.[^9] Alfozan placed second; the community's account on X congratulated him on April 12, 2020 for second place in the "#تحدي_كوفيد١٩" (COVID-19 challenge) and, the same day, congratulated a different participant for third place.[^10] His public GitHub repository "COVID-19-notebook," described as the "Saudi COVID-19 Notebook," links the challenge announcement and the April 12 post.[^11] His LinkedIn profile lists the entry as "Saudi COVID-19 Challenge, Mar 2020, 2nd place, $50k prize."[^2]

The second is the Homathon, an online hackathon run by the [[Saudi Federation for Cybersecurity, Programming and Drones]] (SAFCSP) with a registration period of April 14 to 18, 2020 and an event period of April 19 to 25, 2020.[^12][^13] The launch coverage gave a total prize pool of $85,000, with $50,000 for first place, $25,000 for second and $15,000 for third.[^13] His LinkedIn profile lists "Homathon COVID-19 Challenge, issued by safcsp.org.sa, May 2020, 1st place," with a link to homathon.com.[^2] A team of two engineering alumnae of [[Alfaisal University]] placed third with a project called "Raqeeb."[^14] The Homathon's English homepage lists [[Abdullah bin Sharaf Alghamdi]], president of the [[Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority]] (SDAIA), among its speakers, and Alfaisal University states that the Homathon was set up by the federation "in collaboration with the Saudi Data and Artificial Intelligence Authority."[^12][^14]

The profile dates an Intel International Science and Engineering Fair finalist entry to May 2009 and a WorldSkills International Medallion for Excellence in IT/Software Applications to September 2009.[^2]

The profile lists talks at [[PyData Riyadh]] in 2020. PyData Riyadh announced on August 12, 2020 a talk on [[MLflow]] by "@alfozan, Systems Engineer at Facebook," and a second MLflow session on October 14, 2020 ("As per your demands we have @alfozan back with us for MLflow part 2").[^15] The profile also lists talks at the [[Spark + AI Summit]] in 2019 and 2020.[^2]

### The Recording Device

The [[Department of Justice|Justice Department]]'s release summarizes the complaint as alleging that OpenAI personnel found a device, called Recording Device 1 in the complaint (OpenAI is "Company 1" in the complaint, according to the Northern District of California's version of the release), on September 30, 2026. It was inserted into a USB port on the back of a television in an executive conference room in one of the company's San Francisco buildings, and it was concealed in toilet paper. OpenAI determined that the device was installed on or about September 28, and that its contents were encrypted.[^1] The [[Associated Press]] and TechCrunch report the same details from the complaint.[^24][^27]

According to the complaint, closed-circuit footage showed Alfozan near the conference room on multiple dates before the discovery, although he had no authority to enter the room or attend meetings held there. The complaint describes those meetings as frequently involving highly confidential technical, commercial and strategic information, including proprietary research, product plans, business-development opportunities, and nonpublic information about OpenAI's capabilities, priorities, "government engagements" and commercial relationships.[^1][^28] AP reported that a camera added to the room after the discovery captured him entering the room and checking behind the television, according to the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]].[^27] A photograph published with the TechCrunch report carries the overlay "2026-10-05 13:20:47" to "13:21:30."[^24]

The complaint alleges that before September 28 Alfozan used a productivity tool to make multiple inquiries about OpenAI's building addresses, meeting rooms, meeting-room capacities and calendars, the locations of "leadership and exec" offices, and external partner meetings.[^1][^28] It further alleges that a chatbot account associated with Alfozan's spouse, which shared multiple identifiers and attributes with his own chatbot account, submitted prompts between September 24 and September 30 about building, customizing, installing, audio recording, testing, obfuscating and remotely accessing a device. The quoted prompts include "build[ing] a continuous audio recorder," "customiz[ing] Wi-Fi . . . connectivity," "improv[ing] audio and live listening," and "encrypt[ing] the SD card." On September 27 and 28 the same account submitted prompts about Recording Device 1, including its custom header and unique MAC address.[^1][^28][^29]

On October 5, the complaint alleges, Alfozan entered the executive conference room and appeared to look behind the television where the device had been inserted. The same day he left a bag unattended in a different room in an OpenAI building. Security personnel who opened it found his wallet and identification, along with a second recording device similar to the first.[^1]

### Arrest and Charge

Alfozan, 35, was arrested on Friday, October 9, 2026.[^1] OpenAI said that it fired him that day and coordinated the timing with the FBI so as not to disrupt the investigation. According to OpenAI, it had gradually restricted his access to systems and spaces from September 30, then suspended his physical and network access, placed him on leave and secured his company-issued equipment.[^7] He made his initial appearance in federal court on October 13 and remains in custody.[^27]

The charge is using or endeavoring to use an electronic, mechanical or other device to intercept oral communications, under 18 U.S.C. § 2511(1)(b).[^28] If convicted, he faces a maximum of five years in prison, a $250,000 fine and three years of supervised release.[^1] Assistant U.S. Attorney [[Nicolas Parker]] of the Northern District of California is prosecuting the case with assistance from Trial Attorney [[Kate Tagert]] of the [[National Security Division]]'s Counterintelligence and Export Control Section.[^1] The release states that the prosecution is in support of National Security Presidential Memorandum 11 ([[NSPM-11]]), "designed to support American superiority in super intelligence technology."[^1] The memorandum, signed June 5, 2026, is titled "Artificial Intelligence in the National Security Enterprise" and uses the terms "artificial intelligence" and "AI" throughout.[^30] Executive Order 14434 of September 29, 2026 directs executive agencies to use "Super Intelligence" and "SI" in place of "Artificial Intelligence" and "AI."[^31] A detention hearing before U.S. Magistrate Judge [[Alex G. Tse]] is set for October 19, 2026.[^7]

### Statements

[[Kash Patel]], the director of the Federal Bureau of Investigation, said: "According to the complaint unsealed today, Abdulrahman Alfozan allegedly concealed a covert recording device inside his employer's executive conference room to secretly capture sensitive communications about the company's super intelligence technology." He added that "the FBI views that mission as one of our highest counterintelligence priorities," and thanked "Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton and our law enforcement partners."[^1][^28]

U.S. Attorney [[Craig Missakian]] said: "For us it is a matter of national security. This arrest highlights our relentless efforts in partnership with the FBI to identify and prosecute those who seek to steal our technology, as alleged in the complaint."[^1] [[Scott Schelble]], special agent in charge of the FBI's San Francisco field office, said the agency is committed to protecting sensitive research and proprietary technology.[^7] [[Jay Clayton]], the Director of National Intelligence, called winning the global super intelligence race a national security prerogative and credited the president's leadership in creating the Super Intelligence Force.[^1]

OpenAI said on X: "At this time, we have no indications of this individual inappropriately accessing or exfiltrating model weights, source code, or customer data."[^24] The company described the device as "sophisticated" and said it contained "advanced encryption, audio monitoring capabilities, and a mechanism for transferring data designed to evade monitoring."[^24] Its spokesperson said that employees used the company's models to analyze the device and identified "that a specific employee had planted it a few days earlier."[^27] OpenAI also said it has evidence that Alfozan intended to place other recording devices in sensitive spaces to secretly record meetings.[^7] The company said it treats protecting its technology, data and people as "a matter of national importance."[^7]
