Michael Baden is an American forensic pathologist and former chief medical examiner of [[New York City]] who, retained by [[Mark Epstein]], observed the August 11, 2019 autopsy of his brother [[Jeffrey Epstein]] and later said that the three fractures found in Epstein's neck were "extremely unusual in suicidal hangings and could occur much more commonly in homicidal strangulation." The New York City [[Office of Chief Medical Examiner of the City of New York|Office of Chief Medical Examiner]], which ruled the death a suicide by hanging, said it stood "firmly" behind its finding. Baden earlier chaired the forensic pathology panel of the [[House Select Committee on Assassinations]], which reviewed the medical evidence in the assassination of [[John F. Kennedy]].[^1][^2][^3]

### Career

Baden served briefly as New York City's chief medical examiner before Mayor [[Ed Koch|Edward Koch]] dismissed him about a year later. From 1977 to 1979 he chaired the Select Committee's panel of forensic pathologists, which re-examined the Kennedy autopsy materials and supported the single-bullet conclusion of the [[Warren Commission]] while criticizing the conduct of the original autopsy at [[Bethesda Naval Hospital]]. He later testified or consulted in high-profile cases including the 1995 murder trial of [[O. J. Simpson]], and by 2020 estimated that he had performed more than 20,000 autopsies over a 45-year career.[^1][^3]

### The Epstein Autopsy

Epstein was found hanged in his cell in the Special Housing Unit of the [[Metropolitan Correctional Center, New York]] at about 6:30 a.m. on August 10, 2019. Dr. [[Kristin Roman]], the city medical examiner who performed the autopsy the next day with Baden present, initially listed the manner of death as "pending." Days later Chief Medical Examiner [[Barbara Sampson]] announced that she had reviewed additional evidence and ruled the death a suicide by hanging; she did not disclose the additional evidence, and sources told [[60 Minutes]] that one element was Epstein's apparent attempt on July 23. Toxicology found no medications or illegal drugs.[^1][^4]

Autopsy photographs showed fractures of the left and right thyroid cartilage and of the left hyoid bone. "I have never seen three fractures like this in a suicidal hanging," Baden said on 60 Minutes in January 2020. "Going over a thousand jail hangings, suicides in the New York City state prisons over the past 40 to 50 years, no one had three fractures." He cited petechial hemorrhages on Epstein's face, mouth, and eyes as often indicating strangulation, and noted contusions on both wrists, an abrasion on the left forearm, and deep hemorrhage in the left shoulder muscle. He said that no photograph existed of the body as it was found in the cell: "if this was called a suicide without all that information, it was a premature judgment." The medical examiner's office photographed at least two nooses made from orange bedsheet strips on the cell floor, and the one collected as evidence had both ends folded and hemmed rather than cut; [[Michael Thomas]], the officer who found Epstein, told investigators he ripped the cloth from the bunk.[^1][^5]

Sampson answered Baden's October 2019 [[Fox News]] appearance by telling the [[The New York Times|New York Times]] that "in general, fractures of the hyoid bone and the cartilage can be seen in suicides and homicides," and told the [[Associated Press]], "You can't draw a conclusion from one finding. Everything about the case has to be considered." Of the other pathologists consulted by 60 Minutes, one agreed that the three fractures were very uncommon and another said such neck trauma was associated with both hanging and strangulation, especially in older men; Epstein was 66.[^1]

### The Official Findings

The [[Department of Justice Office of the Inspector General|Justice Department's Inspector General]], in its June 2023 report on Epstein's custody, relied on the account of the medical examiner who performed the autopsy: that Epstein's injuries were consistent with suicide by hanging and that there were no defensive wounds, no marks on his hands, no broken fingernails or debris beneath them, no knuckle contusions, and no bruising other than an arm abrasion "likely due to convulsing from hanging." The report does not mention Baden or the fractures. A July 7, 2025 memorandum from the [[Department of Justice|Justice Department]] and the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation]] cited "the August 19, 2019 autopsy findings" of the medical examiner as consistent with suicide; the Inspector General dates the autopsy to August 11. In March 2026 the [[Miami Herald]] reported that Baden continued to maintain that the injuries were more consistent with strangulation.[^2][^4][^6][^7]
