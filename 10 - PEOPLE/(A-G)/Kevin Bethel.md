Kevin J. Bethel is the police commissioner of [[Philadelphia]], sworn in on January 2, 2024. On September 16, 2026, with Deputy Commissioner [[Frank Vanore]] and the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]]'s special agent in charge in Philadelphia, [[Wayne Jacobs]], he gave the briefing at which police said that 58 people had been identified in the material recovered in the [[Olney House Investigation]] and that five women shown in it were believed to be dead.[^1][^2]

### Career

Bethel was raised in Southwest Philadelphia and joined the [[Philadelphia Police Department]] in 1986. He served in Special Investigations, the Narcotics Strike Force, the Intelligence Unit and Internal Affairs, commanded the 17th District, and in 2009, as a captain aged 44, was promoted by Commissioner [[Charles H. Ramsey]] to deputy commissioner for patrol operations. He retired in 2016 and from 2019 to 2023 was chief of school safety for the [[School District of Philadelphia]], overseeing about 300 school safety officers. He holds degrees from [[Chestnut Hill College]] and [[Saint Joseph's University]] and is a graduate of the FBI National Academy.[^1]

In November 2023 Mayor-elect [[Cherelle Parker]] named him to succeed [[Danielle Outlaw]], and he was sworn in on the day of Parker's inauguration at a middle school in [[Kensington, Philadelphia|Kensington]], where he pledged to concentrate enforcement on the neighborhood's open-air drug markets: "enforcement means restoring law and order humanely and with dignity."[^1]

### The Olney House

At the briefing of September 16, 2026, Bethel said of the pace of the investigation: "We're going to dictate the pace."[^2]
