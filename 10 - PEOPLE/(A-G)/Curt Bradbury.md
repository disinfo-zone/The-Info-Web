Curt Bradbury is a [[Little Rock, Arkansas|Little Rock]] banker who spent his career with the firms of [[Jackson Stephens]].

On November 9, 1977, as an employee of [[Stephens Inc.]], he sat with Stephens and [[Bert Lance]] in Little Rock opposite [[Abdus Sami]] of the [[Bank of Credit and Commerce International]]. The subject was Lance's [[National Bank of Georgia]]. "Stephens and Bradbury allegedly recommended that BCCI purchase FGB stock or recommend the stock to its clients," two reporters wrote of [[Financial General Bankshares]], of which Stephens Inc. held 4.9 percent. Within two months four clients of the bank had bought into the company through an account at Stephens Inc.[^1]

In April 1985 [[Worthen Bank]] lost 52 million dollars of [[Arkansas]] state money in the failure of [[Bevill, Bresler and Schulman]]. Stephens Inc. underwrote a 35 million dollar stock issue, and Bradbury took charge of the bank. As its chairman in March 1992 he arranged a line of credit of 3.5 million dollars for the presidential campaign of [[Bill Clinton]], of which about 3 million was drawn and repaid. The campaign kept its federal election funds on deposit at the bank. With [[Mack McLarty]] he organized Little Rock fundraisers that brought in 2 million dollars, and he gave 10,000 dollars to the [[Democratic Party]]. Another account names the campaign's finance officer, [[David Watkins]], as the man who arranged the loan, and puts it at 3.9 million dollars in eleven installments.[^2][^3]
