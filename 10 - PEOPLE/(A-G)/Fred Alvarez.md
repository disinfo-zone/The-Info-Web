[[Fred Alvarez]] was the former vice-chairman of the [[Cabazon Indian Reservation]]. He was a victim of an execution-style triple homicide in Rancho Mirage, [[California]], that [[Cheri Seymour]] dates to June 29, 1981, along with his friends [[Ralph Boger]] and [[Patricia Castro]]. His murder occurred just six months before the murder of [[Paul Morasca]].[^1]

The Cabazon Indian Casino cardroom had been managed since September 1980 by [[Rocco Zangari]], under an agreement that the appellate panel described as appearing to be between Zangari and [[PN Associates, Inc.]], and that [[Arthur Welmas]] also signed. Zangari was discharged on November 5, 1981, according to the appellate panel, or on November 15, 1981, according to a January 1988 Press-Enterprise article.[^2][^3]

A paper submitted by the California Attorney General's office to the Senate Special Committee on Investigations on February 8, 1989 states that in approximately May 1981 Alvarez, as tribal vice chairman and security chief at the Cabazon cardroom, began to doubt and publicly criticized the Cabazon management; that, according to his sister, he believed money was being skimmed from the cardroom and told local newspapers his life was in danger; that he was removed as vice chairman; and that he and two others decided to consult a lawyer about a suit to open the casino's books. It states that on July 2, 1981 Alvarez and two non-Indians were found dead, shot in the head with a .38 caliber revolver the evening before, and that the case was never solved.[^4]

Alvarez had been chosen to manage the Cabazon Indian Reservation shortly before his murder. Reports related to the Alvarez triple-homicide, including those concerning "[[The Octopus]]", were later found in the "Paul Morasca Homicide Investigation File" in San Francisco, as the original Riverside files were missing.[^1]
