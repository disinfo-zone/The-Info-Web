Safa Haji al-Habobi was the director general of one of the weapons plants of [[Iraq]]'s [[Ministry of Industry and Military Industrialization]], the [[Nassr State Enterprise]], and "a director at several Iraqi front companies, including TDG and the British and U.S. branches of [[Matrix Churchill]]." Representative [[Henry B. Gonzalez]] described the ministry's buying organization, the [[Al-Arabi Trading Company]] of [[Baghdad]], as nominally private and in fact run from day to day by al-Habobi, "one of [[Hussein Kamel|Hussein Kamil]]'s former [[Special Security Organization (Iraq)|Secret Security Organization]] cronies." Its [[London]] holding company, the [[Technology Development Group]], bought Matrix Churchill in 1987, and its [[Paris]] arm, [[Babil International]], was listed in his name.[^1][^2]

On June 14, 1989, his network contracted with [[SerVaas Inc.]] of [[Indianapolis]] for a brass recovery plant, financed like its other American purchases by the [[Atlanta]] branch of the [[Banca Nazionale del Lavoro]]. The indictment returned in Atlanta on February 28, 1991, against the branch manager, [[Christopher Drogoul]], named al-Habobi as a defendant with [[Sadik Taha]] and Abdul Munim Rasheed of the [[Central Bank of Iraq]] and [[Raja Hassan Ali]] of the ministry.[^1][^2]

Al-Habobi told [[Paul Henderson]], the managing director of Matrix Churchill, who was reporting to British intelligence, "not only that [[Rome]] was fully aware of the Atlanta loans but that these were an extension of a government-to-government agreement between [[Italy]] and Iraq." [[Saddam Hussein]] named him oil minister in the summer of 1993.[^1]
