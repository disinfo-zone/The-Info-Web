Robert E. "Bud" Cramer Jr. was born in [[Huntsville]], [[Alabama]], on August 22, 1947. The [[Biographical Directory of the United States Congress]] lists a B.A. from the [[University of Alabama]] (1969) and a J.D. from the University of Alabama Law School (1972), service in the [[United States Army]] in 1972 and the [[United States Army Reserve]] from 1976 to 1978, work as an assistant district attorney of [[Madison County, Alabama]] (1973 to 1975), private law practice, and service as district attorney of Madison County from 1981 to 1990.[^1]

### House of Representatives, 1991 to 2009

The Biographical Directory records that Cramer was "elected as a Democrat to the One Hundred Second and to the eight succeeding Congresses (January 3, 1991-January 3, 2009)" and "was not a candidate for reelection to the One Hundred Eleventh Congress in 2008." Each of the nine terms was for Alabama's 5th Congressional District.[^1]

### Radiance Technologies board, from December 2015

On December 21, 2015 [[Radiance Technologies]] posted an [[AL.com]] article under the headline "Former Congressman Bud Cramer joins Radiance board of directors." The article states that Cramer "has joined the board of directors at Radiance Technologies," that he "retired in 2008 after serving nine terms in the U.S. House of Representatives representing Huntsville and north Alabama" and that he "is now working as a lobbyist in Washington." The article's photograph caption describes a December 4, 2015 ceremony at which Cramer "donated $50,000 to UAH to help store and preserve his documents from his time in Congress" ([[University of Alabama in Huntsville]]). [[Bill Bailey]], then president of Radiance, is quoted: "We could not be more proud to have Congressman Cramer joining the Radiance team. His experience in Washington and his insight will be a tremendous asset for our customers and our future growth." The article gives Cramer's statement: "Radiance Technologies has grown over the last 15 years to become one of the most respected aerospace and defense technology firms in the country and I am proud to join their team."[^2]

### 535 Group lobbying filings

Reports filed under the [[Lobbying Disclosure Act]] by [[535 Group]], LLC, registered as "Government Relations Consulting" in [[Washington, D.C.]], name Robert Cramer as a lobbyist. He is named as a lobbyist on the registrations and reports of the following clients, with the years of those filings and the general issue areas reported:

[[Northrop Grumman]] Systems Corporation (2021 to 2026; Defense, Budget/Appropriations, Taxation); [[United Launch Alliance]] (2021 to 2025; Defense, Budget/Appropriations, Aerospace, Transportation); [[Leidos]] (2023 to 2026; Homeland Security, Defense, Budget/Appropriations, Aviation); [[Technology Service Corporation]] (registration filed in the fourth quarter of 2022 only; Defense, Budget/Appropriations, Small Business; the client's later reports from 2023 through 2026 list another 535 Group lobbyist); [[Maxar Technologies]] (2021 to 2023; Defense, Budget/Appropriations, Aerospace); [[Capstone Research Corporation]] (2025 to 2026; Defense, Budget/Appropriations); [[Hawkeye 360]], through Reston Strategy Group, LLC (2021 to 2023; Defense, Budget/Appropriations); the MSPs for the Protection of Critical Infrastructure, "The Collective" (2023 to 2025; Computer Industry, Defense, Budget/Appropriations); the [[Boy Scouts of America]] (2021 to 2022; Bankruptcy, Budget/Appropriations); the City of Huntsville (2021 to 2026); the [[Huntsville-Madison County Airport Authority]] (2021 to 2026); [[Huntsville Hospital Health System]] (2021 to 2026); the Solid Waste Disposal Authority of the City of Huntsville (2021 to 2026); and the [[HudsonAlpha Institute for Biotechnology|HudsonAlpha Institute of Biotechnology]] (2021 to 2025).[^3]

### Berry v. Bailey

Cramer is the first of the directors listed in paragraph 8 of the second amended verified complaint in [[Berry v. Bailey]], (Doc. 33, filed August 21, 2024) by five former Radiance employees in the [[United States District Court for the Northern District of Alabama]] (removed April 25, 2024 from the [[Madison County Circuit Court]]). Paragraph 8(a) pleads: "Cramer who is an adult resident of Madison County, Alabama." The complaint does not describe him as an employee of Radiance, as it does [[Jim Scanlon]], [[Jed Deason]] and [[Carol Tevepaugh]]. It alleges that the board, which it defines to include Cramer, "voted to table the sale" of Radiance after receiving about 14 offers, that five of them were "in a range of two times (2X) the ESOP's last appraised value" and "approximately two hundred fifty million dollars ($250,000,000)," that Bailey "required individuals who did have knowledge of the offers, including the BOD members, to sign non-disclosure agreements," and that the board members "did no due diligence" on Bailey's representations. Count II pleads a direct state-law breach of fiduciary duty against the board members, Count IV the derivative counterpart, and Count VII a claim under the [[Employee Retirement Income Security Act]]. These are the plaintiffs' allegations.[^4]

[[Corey L. Maze]], the presiding judge, dismissed the state-law claims for lack of standing (direct claims) or ERISA preemption (derivative claims) and the ERISA claims for failure to exhaust the plan's claims procedure, and dismissed all claims without prejudice on September 18, 2025, without a ruling on the facts alleged.[^5]
