David Alexander Goslin was born in [[New York City]] on October 27, 1936, grew up after his father's death in Berkeley, [[California]], graduated in psychology from [[Swarthmore College]] in 1958 and took his doctorate in sociology at [[Yale University]] in 1962. He joined the [[Russell Sage Foundation]] in New York, "held adjunct teaching positions at six different universities and published five books in the field of sociology over a 13-year period," moved to [[Washington, D.C.]] in 1974 and "spent 13 years as Executive Director of the Commission of Behavioral and Social Sciences of the [[National Research Council]]," the commission under which the 1988 report *Enhancing Human Performance* was produced. He was elected a fellow of the [[American Association for the Advancement of Science]] in 1982. In 1987 he became the fourth president and chief executive of the [[American Institutes for Research]], serving until 2001; in his tenure AIR ran "a large-scale personnel evaluation of the U.S. Army that tracked the careers of more than 3 million Army personnel" and in 1994 acquired Pelavin Associates. He died on December 28, 2024.[^1]

In 1995, after the National Research Council declined the CIA's request for a review of Star Gate, the CIA met "with president and representatives of American Institutes of Research (AIR)" on May 10 and signed a contract on June 12 ([[1995 AIR evaluation of Star Gate]]); the report records that "Dr. David A. Goslin, President of AIR, served as coordinator." [[Edwin May]]'s commentary of 1996 objected that Goslin had also administered the NRC's 1988 report.[^3]
