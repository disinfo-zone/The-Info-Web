John H. "Jack" Gibbons (January 15, 1929, to July 17, 2015) was born in [[Harrisonburg, Virginia]], took bachelor's degrees in mathematics and chemistry from [[Randolph-Macon College]] in 1949 and a doctorate in physics from [[Duke University]] in 1954.[^1][^2] The [[White House]] biography states that he spent fifteen years at [[Oak Ridge National Laboratory]] studying atomic nuclei, with emphasis on the role of neutron capture in the nucleosynthesis of heavy elements in stars, became in 1973 the first director of the [[Federal Office of Energy Conservation]], directed the [[University of Tennessee]] Energy, Environment and Resources Center for two years, and in 1979 became director of the Congressional [[Office of Technology Assessment]], where he served two six-year terms before his presidential appointment on February 2, 1993 as Assistant to the President for Science and Technology and Director of the [[Office of Science and Technology Policy]].[^1] He was a member of the [[Council on Foreign Relations]].[^1] The [[National Academy of Engineering]] lists his years as 1929 to 2015, and the [[American Institute of Physics]] obituary gives his death at [[Crozet, Virginia]].[^2]

### Contacts with Laurance Rockefeller

Gibbons met [[Laurance Rockefeller]] and [[C. B. Scott Jones]] in April 1993, three months into his term, after a request from Rockefeller's attorney [[Henry L. Diamond]] of March 29, 1993; Rockefeller's letter of April 21, 1993 records that Gibbons said he had "not learned that the [[United States]] Government has a body of knowledge on UFOs or ETI that is being withheld from the public."[^3] In the further correspondence described under the [[Rockefeller Initiative]] Gibbons suggested approaching the [[Department of Defense|Defense Department]], proposed an informal inquiry into agency records on the 1947 [[Roswell Incident]], and received from Secretary [[Sheila Widnall]] an interim status report on May 24, 1994, the full Roswell report on February 27, 1996 (with the request to pass it to Rockefeller) and the second volume, *The Roswell Report: Case Closed*, on June 20, 1997. Rockefeller's letter of February 14, 1994 credited Gibbons with the suggestion to begin with a specific incident, and Gibbons's note of August 1994 to Rockefeller says he received the [[United States Air Force|Air Force]] material "yesterday."[^3] A handwritten note on Jones's letter of February 17, 1994, signed "JHG" and dated 3/29/94, reads: "Scott Jones used to be associated with Senator Pell. He was formerly a [[United States Navy|Navy]] pilot. The [[Human Potential Foundation]] is funded in part, I believe, by L.R."[^4] In a note to Rockefeller dated 8/19/94 he wrote that he had told Senator [[Claiborne Pell]] of the Air Force report the day before, and a note to "Clay" of the same date encloses the report's summary text and a lecture of his own and thanks Pell for lunch.[^5]

On August 4, 1995 he wrote to President [[Bill Clinton]] that Rockefeller would likely see the President in the [[Grand Teton National Park|Tetons]] and "will want to talk with you about his interest in extrasensory perception, paranormal phenomena, and UFO's"; that "I persuaded Rockefeller to not bother you with this issue but instead to let me talk with defense officials"; that "all evidence points to a failed U.S. Air Force balloon experiment, and no evidence of a 'flying saucer' or cover-up conspiracy"; and that "I've made no secret about my conviction that we must not be too diverted from more earthly imperatives."[^6] A draft letter from Rockefeller to the President of August 23, 1995 proposed that "Your Science Advisor, Jack Gibbons, might be the right person" to coordinate government information on the subject.[^7]

The OSTP files include a letter from the physicist [[Bruce Maccabee]] of January 2, 1997 stating that in April 1993 "at the request of Dr. [[Ronald Pandolfi]] of the [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]]" he had faxed Gibbons an information paper on UFOs, and Gibbons's reply of April 26, 1995 to the [[Government Accountability Office|General Accounting Office]] that his office had "no direct knowledge of what occurred at Roswell and no records, except for the information I received from the Air Force."[^8]
