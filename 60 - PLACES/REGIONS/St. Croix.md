St. Croix is one of the three major islands of the [[U.S. Virgin Islands]], with [[St. Thomas]] and [[St. John]], and includes the town of [[Christiansted]]. Under Danish rule it was the scene of the 1878 Fireburn uprising on its sugarcane plantations, and it later held the [[HOVENSA]] oil refinery, whose idled plant [[Cecile de Jongh]] told [[Jeffrey Epstein]] in 2015 that the [[Department of Defense]] would assess as a replacement for the prison at [[Guantanamo Bay detention camp|Guantanamo]]. Governor [[Albert Bryan]] finished school and worked on St. Croix, Governor [[Kenneth Mapp]] represented it in the Legislature, and a St. Croix environmental firm prepared the permitting for Epstein's post-hurricane docks on [[Great St. James]].[^1][^2][^3][^4][^5][^6]

### The Fireburn

On St. Croix in 1878, under Danish rule, plantation workers rose against their working and living conditions in the Fireburn, a post-slavery uprising in which four women, led by Queen Mary, set fire to some fifty sugarcane plantations. In 2009 [[Wayne James]], elected from St. Croix to the 28th [[Legislature of the Virgin Islands]], obtained legislative funds to copy and translate Fireburn records in the Danish National Archives and, after the territory began garnishing his salary over a 197,000 dollar tax levy, submitted fake invoices, causing the Legislature to pay him more than 90,000 dollars, of which he kept about 70,000 dollars. He received more than 1,600 pages of documents and never delivered them. A federal jury convicted him of wire fraud and theft of federal program funds on August 16, 2018; the prosecutor said he had "exploited a precious piece of Virgin Islands history for his own purposes."[^5]

Before the creation of the [[Virgin Islands Port Authority]], the federal Virgin Islands Corporation, established in 1949, kept its principal offices on St. Croix, where it grew sugarcane, manufactured raw sugar, generated electricity and ran hotels, a beach club and a golf course. The Port Authority's St. Croix office is in the terminal of [[Henry E. Rohlsen Airport]] at Estate Manning Bay.[^6]

### Fountain Valley

On September 6, 1972 five gunmen entered the Fountain Valley Golf Course and killed eight people and wounded eight more. The alleged leader, Ishmael LaBeet, was a Vietnam veteran with a bad-conduct discharge. LaBeet, Warren Ballentine, Beaumont Gereau, Meral Smith and Raphael Joseph were each convicted and sentenced to eight consecutive life terms. Michael Joseph, a St. Croix attorney whose brother Raphael was among the five, wrote in his 2014 book *Fountain Valley 1972* that LaBeet had shot at a police officer and hidden in the bush of the Grove Place area, that the five were tortured after their capture, and that the protracted manhunt damaged the island's economy as travel agents on the mainland dropped it from their recommended destinations. In 1984 LaBeet, then using the name Ismail Ali, hijacked a plane during a prison transfer and escaped to [[Cuba]]. Governor [[Alexander Farrelly]] pardoned Raphael Joseph in 1992. The course is now known as Carambola.[^7]

### The Refinery

The St. Croix refinery was operated by HOVENSA before it passed to new owners, and a 2011 federal consent decree resolved [[Clean Air Act]] violations by HOVENSA. Albert Bryan, who moved to St. Croix as a teenager and graduated from St. Dunstan's Episcopal High School in 1985, worked at the [[Hess Corporation|Hess]] oil operation there before entering government. In March 2015 Cecile de Jongh, the office manager of Epstein's companies and wife of former Governor [[John de Jongh]], informed Epstein that Governor Mapp was interested in repurposing the refinery, writing that the Department of Defense was "sending a team to St. Croix within the month to make a full assessment of the HOVENSA facility for use as a replacement for the prison at Guantanamo, or other military use in light of the proposed closing of GTMO." On November 16, 2016, after the election of [[Donald Trump]], Epstein asked Mapp whether he wanted to discuss "trump (sic) priorities? I think he would like your guantamo (sic) proposal." The [[University of the Virgin Islands]] board chairman Alex Moorhead, whom Cecile de Jongh described to Epstein in 2011 as "QUIET AND CONSERVATIVE," was a retired vice president for government affairs of HOVENSA.[^2][^3][^8]

The plant reopened under new owners as Limetree Bay. After a flaring event on February 4, 2021 and further air emissions incidents, the [[Environmental Protection Agency]] ordered Limetree Bay on May 14, 2021 to pause all operations, citing "the imminent and substantial endangerment to public health or welfare" posed by continued operation. The refinery was sold at a bankruptcy auction on December 21, 2021 to West Indies Petroleum Limited and Port Hamilton Refining and Transportation.[^1]

### Epstein's Money on St. Croix

Kenneth Mapp served three terms as a senator from St. Croix before his election as governor in 2014. On October 16, 2014 Cecile de Jongh asked Epstein how much he would give to the St. Croix District Democratic Party, and he agreed to give 15,000 dollars. As governor, Mapp raised money from Epstein for the Queen Louise Home for Children on St. Croix, reporting a public presentation of donations totaling 140,500 dollars and later acknowledging a further 20,000 dollars. Epstein's 2009 suit against [[Bear Stearns]] on behalf of [[Financial Trust Company]] was filed by the St. Croix lawyer [[John K. Dema]].[^9][^10][^11][^12]

In January 2018 Bioimpact, Inc., an environmental consulting firm in Kingshill, St. Croix, issued a revised report for Great St. Jim, LLC, Epstein's company owning Great St. James, on temporary barge landings and access docks "on the point closest to [[Little St. James]]," built after Hurricanes Irma and Maria to bring in emergency supplies and to house employees who had lost their homes. The report noted that the [[United States Army Corps of Engineers|Army Corps of Engineers]] had spotted emergency rubble ramps in "aerial surveillance" and required their removal pending permits.[^4]

### Later Ties

[[Tova Noel]], one of the two guards on duty at the [[Metropolitan Correctional Center, New York|Metropolitan Correctional Center]] the night Epstein died in August 2019, was born in [[Antigua]] and lived for several years on St. Croix. In February 2026 Senator [[Kenneth Gittens]] opposed the reconfirmation of three Port Authority board members, complaining of the lack of development on St. Croix compared with St. Thomas: "We talking one port, one VI." The 2020 census found that each of the territory's major islands, St. Croix among them, had lost population since 2010.[^13][^14][^15]
