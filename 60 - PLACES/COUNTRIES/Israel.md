The State of [[Israel]] was established in 1948. Its intelligence services are the [[Mossad]] (foreign intelligence), [[Shin Bet]] (internal security) and military intelligence, [[Aman]], whose signals arm is [[Unit 8200]]; a fourth agency under the defense ministry, the Science Liaison Bureau, [[LAKAM]], collected technical information abroad until the [[Jonathan Pollard]] case of 1985.[^1][^2]

### Nuclear Weapons

The chemist [[Ernst David Bergmann]], director of the chemistry division of the [[Weizmann Institute of Science]], chaired the Israeli [[Atomic Energy Commission]] from its founding in 1952; the commission sat under the defense ministry and [[Shimon Peres]]. [[France]] supplied the reactor and the underground reprocessing plant at [[Dimona]] after the [[Suez Crisis]] of 1956, in which Israel attacked [[Egypt]] in concert with France and [[United Kingdom|Britain]] and was forced by [[Dwight D. Eisenhower|Eisenhower]] to withdraw. Senior figures of the ruling [[Mapai Party]], among them [[Levi Eshkol]] and [[Pinhas Lavon]], opposed the program on grounds of cost. By 1968 [[Moshe Dayan]] had moved Dimona into full production without a formal cabinet decision.[^1]

In the first days of the [[Yom Kippur War]] of October 1973 the leadership under [[Golda Meir]] and Dayan armed and targeted the arsenal and informed [[Washington, D.C.|Washington]], a step that also served to force an American resupply of the army.[^1] In 1986 the Dimona technician [[Mordecai Vanunu]] gave photographs of the plant's underground production units to the *[[The Sunday Times|Sunday Times]]*; they gave American analysts their first extensive evidence of an Israeli capacity for thermonuclear weapons. Vanunu was lured from [[London]] to [[Rome]] by a woman working for the Mossad, [[Cindy Hanin Bentov]], drugged, and taken to Israel by ship for trial.[^1][^3]

### What Washington Knew

The [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]]'s photographic interpreters [[Arthur C. Lundahl]] and [[Dino A. Brugioni]] saw the first signs of the Dimona reactor on [[U-2]] imagery and had no doubt by the end of 1959 that Israel was pursuing a bomb. [[John F. Kennedy]] demanded inspections of Dimona and in 1962 authorized the first sale of [[United States|American]] weapons to Israel, [[HAWK missile|Hawk]] missiles, linked to Israel's consent to inspection visits that Israel controlled. Ambassador [[Walworth Barbour]], in [[Tel Aviv]] from 1961 to 1973, ordered his staff after the [[Six-Day War]] to stop reporting on nuclear weapons, told an intelligence briefer "Gentlemen, I don't believe a word of it," and after retiring joined the board of the American branch of [[Bank Leumi]]. [[Carl E. Duckett]], whose CIA [[Office of Science and Technology]] received the intelligence on the program, concluded in 1968 that Israel had three or four bombs; [[Lyndon B. Johnson|Johnson]] had the estimate buried. The estimate rested in part on the suspicion that [[Zalman Mordecai Shapiro]] of the [[Nuclear Materials and Equipment Corporation]] had diverted more than two hundred pounds of enriched uranium to Israel; the [[Jimmy Carter|Carter]] [[White House]] announced in 1977 that investigations had "failed to reveal" a diversion.[^1] During the Six-Day War, on June 8, 1967, Israeli forces attacked the American intelligence ship [[USS Liberty Incident|USS Liberty]], and Barbour urged Washington to play the incident down.[^1]

### Intelligence Operations in the United States

[[James Jesus Angleton]] ran the CIA's liaison with Israeli intelligence under the cryptonym [[KK MOUNTAIN]], its channel for the text of [[Nikita Khrushchev]]'s 1956 "secret speech." [[Richard Helms]] told his deputies that he believed Israel was "an open pipeline for pumping intelligence to Moscow."[^4][^5] After the [[Osirak bombing]] of June 7, 1981, [[William J. Casey|William Casey]] ordered a review that found Israel had used its [[KH-11]] satellite-sharing arrangement to obtain coverage of western Russia and Moscow.[^1]

[[Rafael Eitan]], head of LAKAM, recruited Pollard, a [[U.S. Navy|Navy]] intelligence analyst, in October 1981 according to [[Seymour Hersh]], three years earlier than Pollard and the government admitted; material from LAKAM reached Israeli intelligence in such volume that a code name, JUMBO, was added to its markings. Pollard's handler from 1984, the Air Force colonel [[Aviem Sella]], was indicted in the United States in 1987; Israel refused his extradition, promoted him and gave Eitan a senior post at the [[Israel Chemicals Company]]. Israel retained [[Leonard Garment]] to represent Sella; Garment withdrew, saying he was not sure whether his client was Sella or the Israeli government.[^1] Eitan is also the figure alleged to have obtained the [[PROMIS]] case-management software from the [[Department of Justice|Justice Department]] in 1983 and to have had it fitted with a hidden access point; the Justice Department's memoranda record only the delivery of a public-domain version to a "Dr. Ben Orr, a representative of the Government of Israel."[^6][^7]

### Arms to Iran

Israel sold arms to [[Iran]] after the revolution of 1979. [[Yaacov Nimrodi]], military attaché in [[Tehran]] from 1955 to 1968, sold the Iranian defense ministry Hawk missiles worth 135.8 million dollars in 1981; with [[David Kimche]] and [[Al Schwimmer]] he managed the first shipment of the [[Iran-Contra Affair|Iran-Contra]] channel, 96 [[TOW Missile|TOW]] missiles from Israeli stocks in August 1985.[^8][^9] According to [[Ari Ben-Menashe]], Prime Minister [[Menachem Begin]] had a committee formed in the fall of 1980, the [[Iran-Israel Joint Committee]], to supply Iran in its war with [[Iraq]], financed from its own profits through the [[Ora Group]], with funds held at [[Drexel Burnham]]; the Justice Department's 1994 review of the [[PROMIS Software Scandal]] recorded the broker [[John A. Belton]]'s description, given without documents, of Ora as "the Israeli half of a multi-billion dollar slush fund."[^2][^7] Ben-Menashe's account of Israeli participation in the [[October Surprise]] was rejected by the [[House October Surprise Task Force]] in its report of January 3, 1993, which described his testimony as lacking credibility.[^2][^10]

### Latin America and Southern Africa

The former Mossad officer [[Mike Harari]] was named by [[Jose Blandon|José Blandón]] before the [[United States Senate|Senate]] in February 1988 as the head of a network supplying arms to [[Central America]] and the [[Contras]] from [[Panama]], whose aircraft returned with drugs; the Israeli embassy said he acted "as a private citizen."[^11] According to Ben-Menashe, the arms company [[Eagle]] sold weapons to the [[Guatemala|Guatemalan]] military with Harari and [[Pesach Ben-Or]], [[Alfredo Stroessner]]'s [[Paraguay]] served as a conduit for materiel from [[South Africa]] to Dimona, and the [[South African Defense Force]] signed the mutual wartime agreement [[SIMWA]] with the [[Israel Defense Forces]] and invested in the nuclear program.[^2]

### Maxwell and Vanunu

[[Robert Maxwell]], owner of the *[[Daily Mirror]]*, controlled the Israeli computer company [[Degem]]. Hersh alleged in October 1991 that Maxwell and his foreign editor [[Nicholas Davies]] had links to Israeli intelligence and that Maxwell had relayed information to the Israeli embassy in London that led to Vanunu's capture; Maxwell called the claim a total invention. He died the following month and was buried on the [[Mount of Olives]] in the presence of the president, the prime minister and serving and former intelligence chiefs.[^1][^12]

### Surveillance Technology

Unit 8200 is the recruiting ground of the Israeli private surveillance industry; the founders of the spyware maker [[NSO Group]] are its alumni.[^13] In 2015 the former prime minister [[Ehud Barak]] arranged an investment of about one million dollars by [[Jeffrey Epstein]] in Reporty, later [[Carbyne]], which also had backing from [[Peter Thiel]]'s [[Founders Fund]].[^14] On January 12, 2024, [[Palantir Technologies]] announced "a strategic partnership with the Israeli Ministry of Defense to supply Palantir technology to help the country's war effort."[^15]
