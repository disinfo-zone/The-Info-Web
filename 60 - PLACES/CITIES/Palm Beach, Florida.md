Palm Beach is a town in [[Palm Beach County]], [[Florida]], on the barrier island between Lake Worth and the Atlantic. It was incorporated by its first charter in 1911 and has its own police department. [[Donald Trump|Donald Trump's]] club [[Mar-a-Lago]] and, from 1990 to 2019, [[Jeffrey Epstein|Jeffrey Epstein's]] house at [[358 El Brillo Way]] stand in the town. A 2005 complaint to the town's police about the house produced the first criminal investigation of Epstein, which the county's State Attorney took to a grand jury and federal prosecutors ended with a [[Epstein non-prosecution agreement|non-prosecution agreement]].[^1][^2][^3]

### Town and Society

The 1911 charter created the office of town marshal. Joseph Borman, occasionally employed before his election by the first mayor, Captain Dimick, served as marshal, tax collector, tax assessor, fire chief, voter registrar, code enforcement officer, building official, mosquito control officer and secretary of the town caucus until 1946, and became the first police chief when the [[Palm Beach Police Department]] was formed on October 17, 1922. From 1911 until the Second World War, society baseball games between the New York "Police" and the Philadelphia "Convicts," played at the present site of the tennis courts of [[The Breakers]] with Palm Beach police officers on both sides, raised money for the police pension fund; players included [[Woolworth Donahue]], [[E. F. Hutton]] and [[Walter P. Chrysler]]. The town's police dispatched for [[West Palm Beach, Florida|West Palm Beach]] from 1933, and military police patrolled Palm Beach during the war as a rest point for servicemen.[^1]

Mar-a-Lago was built in the 1920s for [[Marjorie Merriweather Post]]; its tunnel under South Ocean Boulevard leads to a beach cabana beside the [[Bath and Tennis Club]]. When Trump opened it as a club in 1995, only one Palm Beach club was open to Jews. Trump had first sought to subdivide the estate; after a year of hearings the Town Council voted the plan down, Trump sued the town for 50 million dollars, and the council approved a club with restrictions on traffic and noise. In December 1996, when the council refused to lift the restrictions, Trump sued again, alleging that the town discriminated against Mar-a-Lago in part because it was open to Jews and African Americans; the town said it was deciding a zoning matter. In 2004 Trump and Epstein bid against each other for the town's oceanfront mansion [[Maison de l'Amitie]], sold out of bankruptcy to Trump for 41.35 million dollars.[^2][^4][^5]

### The Epstein Investigation

Epstein bought 358 El Brillo Way, a waterfront house on the Intracoastal side, in 1990 for about 2.5 million dollars. In March 2005 a woman reported to the town's police that her fourteen-year-old stepdaughter had been molested by a wealthy man who lived on El Brillo Way. Under Chief [[Michael Reiter]], chief from 2001 to 2009, Detective [[Joseph Recarey]] took sworn statements from victims and witnesses, obtained Epstein's household trash through the supervisor of the Town of Palm Beach sanitation bureau, and matched the calls of Epstein's assistant [[Sarah Kellen]] to the girls against the arrival records of Epstein's aircraft at [[Palm Beach International Airport]]; the calls clustered "in the days just prior to [Epstein's] arrival or during the time Epstein was in Palm Beach." Epstein had given the department 90,000 dollars before the investigation began, and the money was returned after his arrest.[^1][^3][^6][^7]

Recarey's affidavit of May 1, 2006 recommended five felony counts. The county's State Attorney, [[Barry Krischer]], instead took the case to a grand jury, which heard two of the girls on July 19, 2006 and returned one count of felony solicitation of prostitution; Reiter wrote to Krischer that his office's handling was "highly unusual" and referred the case to the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation]]. On September 21, 2007 Krischer wrote to the federal line prosecutor, "Glad we could get this worked out for reasons I won't put in writing," and the non-prosecution agreement was signed three days later. Epstein pleaded guilty in state court in 2008 and served about thirteen months in the county stockade with work release, during which deputies of the [[Palm Beach County Sheriff's Office]], paid through an off-duty permit for the "[[Florida Science Foundation]]," escorted him from the foundation's office to his house for midday visits. After Reiter left in 2009, the town's police were headed by a Director of Public Safety until 2018.[^1][^3][^8][^9][^10]

### Registration and Residence

Epstein registered as a sex offender in the [[U.S. Virgin Islands]], and his filings with the territory described the Palm Beach house as a "vacation home." In a privacy waiver his lawyer filed with the FBI in 2010, he gave a [[St. Thomas]] address as his current residence and 358 El Brillo Way as his prior one. On February 19, 2019, after he was detained and refused entry in the [[Dominican Republic]], he flew on "to his vacation home in Palm Beach, Florida," and his May 2019 travel notice to the territory listed a stay "at my vacation home in Palm Beach, Florida" before trips to New York, [[New Mexico]] and Paris. The Virgin Islands government, in its complaint against his estate, valued the Palm Beach house at 12 million dollars. The estate sold it in March 2021 for 18.5 million dollars to the developer [[Todd Michael Glaser]], who demolished it and changed the address to 360 El Brillo Way.[^11][^12][^13][^14]
