Little Rock is the capital of [[Arkansas]].

### The Stephens Interests

[[Witt Stephens]] began trading municipal bonds in the city in 1932 and founded the firm that became [[Stephens Inc.]] the next year. His brother [[Jackson Stephens]] ran it from 1957. The bank data processor [[Systematics]], the telephone company [[ALLTEL]], and the [[Worthen Bank|Worthen Banking Corporation]] had their headquarters there, as does the [[Rose Law Firm]], which traces its origin to 1820.[^1]

On November 9, 1977, Jackson Stephens, his officer [[Curt Bradbury]], and [[Bert Lance]] met [[Abdus Sami]] of the [[Bank of Credit and Commerce International]] in Little Rock. Within two months four of that bank's clients had bought into [[Financial General Bankshares]] of Washington through an account at Stephens Inc.[^2]

### The Riadys

[[Mochtar Riady]] of [[Indonesia]] met the Stephens brothers in 1976, and the two families took control of Worthen together in 1983. [[James Riady]], who had been an intern at Stephens Inc., moved to the city to run the bank's international division, with [[John Huang]] as his [[Far East]] manager from February 1985. "One of them even lived in Little Rock for a few years," two reporters wrote of the Riadys in 1992. Huang, Riady, and the Little Rock lawyer [[Mark Grobmyer]] called on President [[Bill Clinton]] at the [[White House]] on June 23, 1994.[^2][^3]

### 1992 and After

In 1992 Worthen, under Bradbury, gave the Clinton campaign a line of credit of 3.5 million dollars, and Bradbury and [[Mack McLarty]] of [[Arkla]] organized Little Rock dinners that raised two million. After the election the Rose partners [[Hillary Rodham Clinton]], [[Vince Foster]], and [[Webster Hubbell]] went to Washington. The firm's remaining members "found irregularities in Hubbell's billings for 1989-92," and on December 6, 1994, he pleaded guilty in the federal court at Little Rock.[^4][^5]
