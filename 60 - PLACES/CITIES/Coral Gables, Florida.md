Coral Gables is a city in [[Dade County, Florida]], adjoining [[Miami]].

The [[Wackenhut Corporation]], founded in 1954 by [[George R. Wackenhut]] and three other former agents of the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]], had its headquarters there until it moved to [[Palm Beach Gardens, Florida|Palm Beach Gardens]]. The [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]] used the company from 1961 to 1967 to give cover to officers of its Western Hemisphere Division stationed in the Miami area. On May 3, 1967, [[George Walker (Wackenhut)|George Walker]] of the Coral Gables office handed the FBI a letter from a Miami man who said that before the assassination of President [[John F. Kennedy]] a Cuban in his boarding house had remarked that to kill Kennedy one had only to excite a Cuban with a high-powered rifle.[^1][^2]

On July 26, 1973, the office of the [[Watergate Special Prosecution Force|Watergate special prosecutor]] asked the CIA whether it had any working relationship with "the ITT Public Relations Office located at 495 Biltmore Way, Coral Gables, [[Florida]]," and whether [[Howard Hunt]] or [[G. Gordon Liddy]] had done undercover work for [[ITT Corporation|ITT]]. The agency's Cover and Commercial Staff answered on August 7 that it had no relationship with or information on the Coral Gables office and had never discussed ITT with the [[Robert R. Mullen Company]]. ITT's public relations man for [[Latin America]], [[Hal Hendrix]], a former Miami journalist, pleaded guilty in 1976 to withholding information from a Senate subcommittee about his CIA contacts.[^3]
