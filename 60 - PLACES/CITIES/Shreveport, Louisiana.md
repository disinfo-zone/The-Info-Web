Shreveport is a city in northwestern [[Louisiana]], the commercial center of the oil country that runs into [[East Texas]] and southern [[Arkansas]].

### The Hunts

[[H.L. Hunt]] married [[Frania Tye Lee|Frania Tye]] in [[Florida]] in 1925 under the name [[Franklin Hunt]], while married to [[Lyda Bunker Hunt]]. They lived in Shreveport and then in [[Dallas, Texas|Dallas]] and had four children. [[Hunt Oil Company]] kept an office in the city. In 1940 [[Ruth Ray Hunt|Ruth Ray]], of [[Idabel, Oklahoma]], came to Shreveport to find work, became a legal secretary at Blanchard, Goldstein, Walker and O'Quinn, and went to Hunt Oil when the partner Claude O'Quinn did. Hunt said he first noticed her waiting for a bus outside the office. She left in the fall of 1942 and bore his son [[Ray Hunt|Ray]] in [[New York City|New York]] the next April. In the first years of the [[World War II|Second World War]] Hunt had two employees move his son [[Hassie Hunt|Hassie]] from Dallas to Shreveport to [[Jackson, Mississippi]], "changing draft boards with each move."[^1][^2]

On November 11, 1975, the fiftieth anniversary of the Florida wedding, Frania Tye Lee sued Hunt's estate in the federal court in Shreveport, claiming a wife's share. At the trial in January 1978 Ray Hunt, as executor, acknowledged from the witness stand that his father was the father of her children. [[Margaret Hunt Hill]] waited in a motel, and Ruth Hunt stayed in Dallas on the advice of lawyers who feared she would be asked about the birth of her own children.[^1][^3]

### Southern Research

The [[Southern Research Company]], Inc., a private detective firm, was chartered in Shreveport on December 2, 1958, and still operates there. A credit report of 1960 in a [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]] security file describes its [[New Orleans]] branch, managed by [[Joseph Oster]], a former associate of [[Guy Banister]], as the office of a firm "whose home office is in Shreveport." That branch investigated [[David Ferrie]] for [[Eastern Air Lines]] in 1962 and 1963. In 1996 the Shreveport company's president, [[Thomas Ostendorff]], gave its Ferrie file, number 11-N-224, to the [[Assassination Records Review Board]].[^4]
