Delaware is a Mid-Atlantic state and the legal domicile of most of the corporations, limited partnerships and limited liability companies named on this site. Delaware law allows an entity to come into existence on a filing with the state and gives it a file number.[^1][^2] The state's courts appear as well, chiefly the [[Delaware Court of Chancery]], which heard the trustee suit over the [[Howard Hughes Medical Institute]], and the [[United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware]], where the FTX Chapter 11 case and several adversary proceedings involving SkyBridge-related parties were filed. In the Supreme Court record the state is the respondent in [[Franks v. Delaware]].

### Delaware General Corporation Law and the registry

Any person, partnership, association or corporation, without regard to residence, domicile or state of incorporation, may incorporate under the General Corporation Law by filing a certificate of incorporation with the Division of Corporations in the Department of State.[^3] The certificate must state the corporation's name, the address of its registered office in the state and the name of its registered agent at that address.[^4] The Limited Liability Company Act likewise requires a certificate of formation that sets out the name, the registered office and the registered agent, and the company exists from the moment of filing.[^2] The corporation law also permits the certificate to eliminate the personal liability of directors and officers to the corporation or its stockholders for monetary damages for breach of fiduciary duty, subject to enumerated exceptions.[^5] A stockholder may inspect the corporation's books and records for a proper purpose on written demand under oath, and the Court of Chancery hears the proceedings to compel it.[^6]

The federal Corporate Transparency Act, enacted on January 1, 2021 as part of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2021, directed the Financial Crimes Enforcement Network to collect beneficial ownership information from entities formed by a state filing.[^7] On March 26, 2025, FinCEN published an interim final rule (90 Fed. Reg. 13688) that confined the reporting requirement to entities formed under foreign law and registered to do business in the United States, exempting entities previously defined as domestic reporting companies from reporting beneficial ownership information.[^8] A final rule of August 14, 2026 (91 Fed. Reg. 52508) adopted the interim rule with changes, and also exempted reporting companies from submitting information about their U.S. person company applicants.[^9]

### Corporate domicile and the Division of Corporations

The Division of Corporations record for a Delaware entity gives a file number, a formation date and the name of a registered agent. [[GeoMiliTech Consultants Corp.]] appears under file number 2015058, with a formation date of August 15, 1983, and [[Corporation Guarantee and Trust Company]] of [[Wilmington, Delaware|Wilmington]] as registered agent.[^10] [[Christopher Byron]] wrote in 1991 that court records showed the company incorporated in July 1983.[^11] [[Mark Phythian]] gives August 15, 1983 as the date on which a company presented as private was formed in Delaware to sell or exchange weapons.[^12]

The [[Counter Terrorism Consultants, Inc.]] partnership, CounterTerrorism Consultants, L.P., was filed as a Delaware limited partnership under file number 2168210 on August 1, 1988.[^13] It registered in the District of Columbia as a foreign limited partnership on August 5, 1988, under file number 058088, with the corporation at 2300 M Street NW as its registered agent.[^14] The 1988 membership directory of the [[International Association of Chiefs of Police]] lists [[Carter Cornick]] as president of the corporation at that address.[^15] On August 2, 1988, [[United Press International]] reported that a group of former FBI agents and federal prosecutors had formed a consulting firm, a report that appeared the day after the partnership's filing.[^16]

The [[Commodity Credit Corporation]] holds its present federal charter under the Commodity Credit Corporation Charter Act of June 29, 1948. That act dissolved an earlier corporation of the same name organized under Delaware law and transferred its rights, duties, assets and liabilities to the new body, as the [[Government Corporation Control Act]] of 1945 required of wholly owned government corporations.[^17]

[[Discord]] Inc. is a Delaware corporation with its business address in [[San Francisco|San Francisco, California]].[^18] In his January 31, 2024 testimony before the [[United States Senate Committee on the Judiciary]], [[Jason Citron]] stated that he had co-founded the company in 2015, that more than 150 million people used the service each month, that more than 75 percent of them were outside the [[United States]], and that Discord sold no advertising and charged for subscriptions and server boosts.[^19]

### Epstein aircraft and the AdFin shareholding

[[Air Ghislaine, Inc.]] was a Delaware corporation with a registered address in Wilmington and an office address for [[Jeffrey Epstein]] in New York. It held fractional jet shares and helicopters from at least January 2000.[^20] Its name carried the first name of [[Ghislaine Maxwell]]. It was renamed Shmitka Air, Inc. on February 11, 2010, and Freedom Air International, Inc. on July 6, 2010.[^21] A forensic accountant's table of Epstein entities lists Freedom Air International as wholly owned by Epstein and cites a consent of the sole stockholder dated December 7, 2012.[^20] The company's filings with the [[Federal Aviation Administration]] list as signing officers Epstein's chief pilot [[Larry Visoski]] until the 2010 renaming.[^21]

[[JEGE, Inc.]] was a Delaware corporation through which Epstein owned the Boeing 727-31 registered N908JE, the aircraft the press called the [[Lolita Express]]. A 2007 federal draft indictment, never filed, describes Epstein as its principal owner, its president, sole director and sole shareholder, and says its sole business activities concerned the operation and ownership of that aircraft.[^22] A separate company, JEGE, LLC, was organized in [[St. Thomas]], [[U.S. Virgin Islands]], on October 19, 2012, with Epstein and Visoski as members.[^22]

[[Hyperion Air]], Inc. was a Delaware corporation with a registered address in Wilmington. It bought Epstein's Gulfstream jet N909JE in 1994. The same unfiled 2007 draft indictment describes Epstein as its president, sole director and sole shareholder, and says its sole business activities concerned that Gulfstream.[^23] Hyperion Air, LLC was organized in St. Thomas on October 19, 2012, and held his helicopters until after his death. The corporation's account at [[JPMorgan Chase]] carried Epstein's aviation cash withdrawals from about 2011, according to an expert report filed in the Virgin Islands litigation against the bank.[^20]

[[AdFin Solutions]], Inc., styled AD/FIN, was a Delaware corporation incorporated on April 25, 2011.[^24] It ingested and harmonized log-file data on winning bid transactions in programmatic advertising and sold pricing and media-audit analytics built on it.[^25] Its Series A preferred stock was bought in 2012 and 2013 by [[Jonathan Leitersdorf]], [[David Mitchell]], [[Tom Glocer]], [[Southern Trust Company]] and [[CVAFH I LLC]].[^26] The counterpart signature pages for the purchase agreement name Southern Trust Company, Inc., with Jeffrey Epstein as its president.[^26] The Schedule of Purchasers lists Southern Trust Company for 1,142,857 shares at $499,999.94 in a closing of April 10, 2013.[^27] CVAFH I LLC bought 800,000 shares for $350,000 on July 23, 2013, under a letter agreement that names [[Cantor Fitzgerald]], L.P. as the ultimate holding company of CVAFH.[^28] AdFin ceased operations in October 2019.[^29]

### Howard Hughes Medical Institute and the Court of Chancery

The Howard Hughes Medical Institute was incorporated in Delaware on December 17, 1953, with [[Howard Hughes]] as its sole trustee. The same day its incorporators established an executive committee to exercise such powers as the trustee designated. The institute was funded by the transfer of the electronics division of the Hughes holdings, the [[Hughes Aircraft Company]]. On March 1, 1957 the [[Internal Revenue Service]] ruled that the institute was a charitable corporation exempt from federal income tax.[^52] On July 3, 1968 Hughes caused a "Resolution of Trustee" to be adopted, delegating to the executive committee the duties and powers "which Delaware law had conferred upon the boards of directors of corporations organized for profit."[^52]

Hughes died on April 5, 1976 without naming a successor trustee or a means of selecting one. The affairs of the institute were then largely controlled by two majority members of the executive committee, identified in the opinions as [[F. W. Gay]] and [[Chester C. Davis]]. On July 10, 1978 the institute's restated certificate of incorporation provided that the corporation "shall be managed and controlled by an Executive Committee."[^53]

The Delaware Court of Chancery heard the first round in 1979. On June 26, 1979 the Chancellor, [[William Marvel]], considered a motion by [[Robert L. Shevin]], Attorney General of Florida, to intervene. Shevin argued that Florida had an interest in a charitable trust and in the [[University of Miami Medical Center]], which had received gifts from the institute. Marvel denied the motion without prejudice, finding that the Attorney General of Delaware was fully qualified to litigate the narrow issue before the court.[^53] The opinion records that the institute carried on medical research in laboratories associated with twelve university hospitals and employed more than 275 persons.[^53]

On October 26, 1979 the court considered a complaint filed jointly by the Attorney General of Delaware and [[William R. Lummis]], the Delaware ancillary administrator of the Hughes estate. The institute moved to dismiss the claim of Lummis for lack of standing. Lummis argued that he had succeeded to Hughes's position as trustee and, in the alternative, that as ancillary administrator he had a duty to see a qualified successor appointed. The opinion concluded that Hughes's powers as trustee did not of themselves constitute a property interest of the institute, citing [[In re Buck Trust]].[^52]

### The Court of Chancery and the BGC derivative suit

The Court of Chancery is a court of equity without juries. In 1979 it was headed by William Marvel, who had joined the court as a Vice Chancellor in 1954.[^53]

On October 5, 2018 the [[Roofers Local 149 Pension Fund]] filed a derivative complaint in the Delaware Court of Chancery against the directors of [[BGC Group]], [[Howard Lutnick]], [[CF Group Management]] and Cantor Fitzgerald. The complaint challenged BGC's purchase of [[Berkeley Point]] from [[Cantor Commercial Real Estate]] for 875 million dollars on September 8, 2017 and its simultaneous investment of 100 million dollars for a 27 percent interest in Cantor's remaining commercial mortgage-backed securities business.[^54] The opinion records that the Roofers fund and the Northern California Pipe Trades Trust Funds filed derivative actions in October and November 2018, and that the actions were consolidated on December 4, 2018 as C.A. No. 2018-0722-AGB.[^55]

In the memorandum opinion of September 30, 2019, Chancellor [[Andre G. Bouchard]] described BGC as a public company controlled by Lutnick that had paid 875 million dollars for a private company also controlled by Lutnick. The plaintiffs alleged that Lutnick stood on both sides of the transaction, held a 60 percent economic interest in Berkeley Point against 13.8 percent in BGC, and so had every reason to cause BGC to overpay. Cantor, CF Group and Lutnick controlled BGC through all of its Class B super-voting stock, giving them 60 percent of the voting power, and Lutnick owned about 60 percent of Cantor and held sole voting control of it.[^55] The defendants moved to dismiss on demand futility and, for the four outside directors, for failure to state a claim. Those directors were Linda Bell, Stephen Curwood, [[William Moran]] and John Dalton, appointed to a special committee on March 14, 2017. The court found that the complaint pleaded facts creating reasonable doubt about the independence of a majority of the board. As to Moran, it recited a professional relationship with Lutnick of about twenty years across four Cantor-affiliated boards, board compensation from Cantor affiliates of 931,986 dollars, and public and social contacts between their families. It denied both motions without findings on the merits.[^55]

The Delaware Supreme Court affirmed the Court of Chancery's final judgment of September 27, 2022 by order of August 10, 2023, on the basis of the Court of Chancery's memorandum opinion of August 19, 2022.[^56]

### The UPI injunction of 1985

On May 20, 1985, in Wilmington, Vice Chancellor Joseph Walsh of the Court of Chancery granted Douglas Ruhe and William Geissler, the owners of United Press International, a preliminary injunction that removed Luis Nogales as chairman of UPI's parent company and removed its board of directors. UPI was then operating under Chapter 11 protection.[^57] On May 28, 1985 a consent order in the [[United States Bankruptcy Court for the District of Columbia]] abolished UPI's own board and gave Nogales exclusive operating authority as chairman and chief executive officer.[^58]

### Franks v. Delaware

Franks v. Delaware was decided on June 26, 1978. The case arose from a Delaware Supreme Court holding that a defendant "under no circumstances may so challenge the veracity of a sworn statement used by police to procure a search warrant." The United States Supreme Court reversed and held that where a defendant makes a "substantial preliminary showing" that a false statement was included in a warrant affidavit knowingly, intentionally or with reckless disregard for the truth, and the statement is necessary to the finding of probable cause, the Fourth Amendment requires a hearing.[^63]

The doctrine was invoked in [[United States v. Shea et al. (2020)]], where a defense motion to suppress filed on August 13, 2021 argued that the warrant affidavit omitted material information about the confidential informant who provided the basis for the warrant.[^64]

### Valmy, Greenville and the Psychosynthesis Research Foundation

[[Roberto Assagioli]] wrote that the [[Psychosynthesis Research Foundation]] was created in 1957 with its own seat at Valmy in Greenville, Delaware, and that an international congress was held at Valmy in 1959.[^66] The London institute's history states that the foundation was created in New York under the patronage of the [[DuPont]] family and names [[Frank Hilton]] as its head in New York.[^67] A Saturday Review directory of July 12, 1969 lists the foundation in New York with Hilton as director.[^68]

On January 24, 1966, Governor [[Charles L. Terry Jr.]] of Delaware proclaimed the repeal of the charters of corporations that had failed for two years to pay assessed taxes, and the list printed in the Laws of Delaware includes the Psychosynthesis Research Foundation.[^69] The foundation's notice of December 1, 1967 gives its New York office and, as its registered office and treasurer, an address in Wilmington.[^70]

### Joe Biden and the DuPont family

[[Joe Biden]] represented Delaware in the United States Senate. The writer [[S. Dovey]] wrote that his first senatorial campaign in the early 1970s was funded and staffed by DuPont employees, that his victory party was held in a DuPont ballroom, and that his family soon moved into an old DuPont family mansion.[^71]

### Other Delaware references

[[Sarah Beth Clendaniel]] grew up in rural Cecil County, Maryland, a conservative area bordering Delaware and [[Pennsylvania]] in the northeastern corner of Maryland.[^72]

The western part of Pennsylvania was split off as the [[Diocese of Pittsburgh]] in 1843. At the death of Bishop [[John Neumann]] on January 5, 1860, the [[Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Philadelphia]], then the Diocese of Philadelphia, still included Delaware. Its estimated Catholic population was 200,000, with 157 churches and 147 priests.[^73]
