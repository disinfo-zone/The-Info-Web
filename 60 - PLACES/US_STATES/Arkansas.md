Arkansas appears in this record because of the airport at [[Mena, Arkansas]], where the smuggler [[Barry Seal]] kept his aircraft from late 1982 until 1984 and where investigators from the [[Internal Revenue Service|IRS]] and the [[Arkansas State Police]] worked from 1983. The [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]]'s own file copies, the [[Senate Foreign Relations Committee|Kerry subcommittee]] report and Arkansas press accounts are summarized on that page.

### Officials and investigations

[[Asa Hutchinson]], appointed United States attorney for the [[Western District of Arkansas]] in 1982, assigned the IRS investigator [[William Duncan]] to Seal, and his successor [[J. Michael Fitzhugh]] canceled Seal's grand jury appearance in December 1985 for "lack of credibility." A 1986 [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]] memorandum from [[Hot Springs]], obtained by the *[[Arkansas Times]]*, said Fitzhugh would be "withholding presentation" of the Mena investigation to the grand jury. Hutchinson later served as administrator of the [[DEA]] and governor of Arkansas.[^1][^2] Attorney General [[Winston Bryant]] said in 1995 that he spent three years trying to get Mena files from the [[U.S. Department of Justice|Justice Department]] and "all we ever got was a total run-around."[^2]

The Kerry subcommittee's 1988 report stated that grand jury probes of Seal's Mena associates were dropped over the protests of state and federal law enforcement officials, with the apparent reason that prosecution might reveal national security information.[^3] [[Charles E. Black]], deputy prosecuting attorney at Mena, asked Governor [[Bill Clinton]]'s office for $25,000 to impanel a state grand jury and said he received no reply; Clinton later said he had authorized the payment and "nothing ever came of that."[^1]

### Little Rock

The [[Little Rock]] bond dealer [[Danny Ray Lasater]] and Clinton's half-brother [[Roger Clinton]] pleaded guilty to drug charges. The *Arkansas Times* reported that the FBI's 1986 investigation of Lasater found that his pilot flew him and [[Patsy Thomasson]] to [[Belize]] on February 8, 1984, to look at a horse farm for sale by [[Roy Carver]], a ranch [[Billy Earle Jr.]] told investigator [[Russell Welch]] Seal had planned to fly to for a cocaine operation weeks before; it noted that no link between Clinton and Seal has ever been established.[^1]

### The aircraft

The C-123K "Fat Lady" that Seal kept at Mena is the aircraft the CIA Inspector General's file calls N4410F, and a DEA spokesman said in 1986 that it was the plane shot down over [[Nicaragua]] with [[Eugene Hasenfus]] aboard. [[Gary Webb]] wrote in *Dark Alliance* that Seal sold the plane back to the company he got it from and that in early 1986 it wound up with [[Southern Air Transport]]; the CIA Inspector General found no record that Southern Air owned or leased a C-123K, and a CIA memorandum of October 10, 1986 said the plane was sold as military surplus in 1983 to an undetermined purchaser.[^4][^5][^6] Webb also wrote that a 1983 [[U.S. Customs]] report said Seal's farm near [[Baton Rouge, Louisiana]] was allegedly a drop site for cocaine and marijuana flown in aboard a DC-4 that later flew supplies to the [[Contras]] through a company operated by the brother of [[Adolfo Calero]], [[Mario Calero]]; Webb is the sole source for that passage.[^7]
