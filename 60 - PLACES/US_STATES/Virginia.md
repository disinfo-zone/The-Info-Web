### Installations

From April 1964 to October 1967 the Soviet defector [[Yuri Nosenko]] was held in solitary confinement in a specially built cell at [[Camp Peary]], "The Farm," the [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]]'s training facility, on the judgment of [[James Jesus Angleton]] that he was a false defector.[^1] From September 1966 to September 1967 [[Manuel Contreras]], later head of Chile's [[DINA]], attended the Army's career officers school at [[Fort Belvoir]].[^2] Lieutenant Colonel [[Jim Channon]] wrote *[[First Earth Battalion|The First Earth Battalion]]* in 1979 as a concept paper of Task Force Delta at the headquarters of the Army's Training and Doctrine Command at Fort Monroe.[^3] [[Alan Standorf]], a civilian analyst at [[Vint Hill Farms Station]] outside Warrenton, "which is run by the army for the [[NSA|National Security Agency]]," died of a blow to the head around January 3, 1991, and was found in the back seat of his car at Washington National Airport on January 28.[^4] In June 1955 [[Nelson Rockefeller]]'s first [[Quantico I and II|Quantico panel]] met at the Marine Corps School in [[Quantico]].[^5]

### Residents and Meetings

[[Marion Pettie]], founder of [[The Finders]], was born in Culpeper in 1920, ran a communal property in the mountains near the town, and died there on October 24, 2003; he said [[Timothy Leary]] sent people down to it in the 1960s.[^6] [[Aldrich Ames]] bought a 540,000-dollar house in Arlington for cash in 1989, on a CIA salary of about 70,000 dollars.[^7] In September 1976 the investigator [[Gaeton Fonzi]] arranged for [[Antonio Veciana]] to meet [[David Atlee Phillips]] at a conference of the Association of Former Intelligence Officers in Reston.[^8] [[Sidney Gottlieb]] kept a goat farm in rural Virginia.[^9] [[Harry Snyder]] of Virginia Tech and Arlene Snyder, as Systemetrics of Blacksburg, wrote the 1979 critical evaluation of remote-viewing research held by the CIA.[^10] The Virginia editor [[James J. Kilpatrick]] was vice chairman of the segregationist [[Coordinating Committee for Fundamental American Freedoms]], which grew out of discussions that included Hugh V. White Jr., formerly of the [[Virginia Commission on Constitutional Government]], and was financed in large part anonymously by [[Wickliffe Draper]].[^11]

### Accelerationism

[[Nicholas Giampa]], seventeen, of Lorton, a follower of [[Atomwaffen Division]], shot his girlfriend's parents in their Reston home on December 22, 2017, and died in custody without trial.[^12] Members of [[The Base]] were arrested on January 16, 2020, four days before the [[Virginia Citizens Defense League]] Lobby Day rally in Richmond, at which they had discussed mass violence.[^13]
