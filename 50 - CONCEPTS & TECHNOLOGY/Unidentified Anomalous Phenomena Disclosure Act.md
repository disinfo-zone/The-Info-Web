The Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Disclosure Act (UAPDA) was submitted on July 13, 2023 by Senate Majority Leader [[Chuck Schumer]] and Senators [[Mike Rounds]], [[Marco Rubio]], [[Kirsten Gillibrand]], [[Todd Young]] and [[Martin Heinrich]] as Senate Amendment 797 to the fiscal 2024 [[National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2024|National Defense Authorization Act]] (S. 2226), concerning records on [[Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena|unidentified anomalous phenomena]] (UAP). It proposed a records collection at the [[National Archives and Records Administration]], a presidentially appointed Review Board modeled on the [[Assassination Records Review Board|board]] of the 1992 [[President John F. Kennedy Assassination Records Collection Act]], and federal eminent domain over recovered technology and "biological evidence of non-human intelligence" held by private parties. The Senate passed the defense bill with the text on July 27, 2023 by 86 votes to 11. The version enacted on December 22, 2023 as Public Law 118-31 kept a National Archives collection and dropped the Review Board and the eminent domain section.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

### Senate Amendment 797 as submitted

As printed in the Congressional Record of July 13, 2023, the amendment added a title establishing an "Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Records Collection." It required that each record be disclosed in full not later than 25 years after its creation unless the President certified grounds for postponement. The Review Board would have nine members appointed by the President with Senate consent, to serve until not later than September 30, 2030, with power to hold hearings, administer oaths, subpoena witnesses and documents, and, through the Attorney General, subpoena private persons. Nominees were barred if they had "any previous or current involvement with any legacy program or controlling authority," defined as government, commercial, academic and private sector endeavors to collect, exploit or reverse engineer technologies of unknown origin or examine biological evidence of non-human intelligence. The Board was to create a "Controlled Disclosure Campaign Plan" for material whose release was postponed and to brief the AARO. A $20,000,000 authorization for fiscal year 2024 was attached.[^1]

The Board's nominations were to follow recommendations from the Senate and House leaders, the Secretary of Defense, the National Academy of Sciences, "The UAP Disclosure Foundation," and the American Historical Association. A search of the [[ProPublica]] Nonprofit Explorer database found no organization named "UAP Disclosure Foundation." It listed the UAP Disclosure Fund of [[San Francisco]] (EIN 99-2834592), a 501(c)(4) with an [[Internal Revenue Service]] ruling date of July 2025, and a Sol Foundation Inc. of Carmel, [[Indiana]] (EIN 99-3863647), a 501(c)(3) private foundation (IRS foundation code 04) with a ruling date of August 2024 (see below). The text of S. 2226 as passed by the Senate on July 27, 2023 lists in the same position "Established nonprofit research organizations relating to unidentified anomalous phenomena." At the House Oversight hearing of November 13, 2024, retired Rear Admiral [[Tim Gallaudet]] told Representative [[Robert Garcia]] that he was "a member of the UAP Discloser [sic] Fund as an advisor and the Sol Foundation as a senior strategic advisor, which is advocating the same."[^1][^2][^5][^6][^7]

### Two organizations named Sol Foundation

Two separate tax-exempt organizations carry the name Sol Foundation in the IRS records. The Sol Foundation for Advanced Scientific and Policy Research, a [[California]] public benefit corporation led by [[Garry Nolan]] and [[Peter Skafish]], holds EIN 93-3649160, a 501(c)(3) ruling date of June 2024, and filed a Form 990 for 2024 reporting revenue of $937,625; its address of record is care of Nolan in [[Redwood City]], California.[^8] The Sol Foundation Inc. of [[Carmel, Indiana]] (EIN 99-3863647) is a private foundation with a ruling date of August 2024 whose Form 990-PF for the period beginning December 24, 2024 reports contributions received of $25,000,000, expenses of $89,482, assets of $24,928,494, no grants paid, and directors [[Lawrence Baker]], [[Patricia Baker]] and [[Charles Hertlein]]; that filing contains no reference to unidentified anomalous phenomena.[^9] The Senate Democrats' print of the amendment (header "BAG23A78 JDP S.L.C.") lists seventh among the bodies whose recommendations the President is to consider "The UAP Disclosure Foundation," a name that appears in no IRS record searched; the engrossed text of S. 2226 as passed on July 27, 2023 replaces it with "Established nonprofit research organizations relating to unidentified anomalous phenomena." Neither text names the Sol Foundation or the Sol Foundation Inc.[^10][^11]

### The scientists' letter of November 12, 2024

A letter dated November 12, 2024 to "Members of Congress" urged passage of the Act's review-board and public-access provisions, which it said "were deleted in conference" in 2023 and omitted from the 2025 defense bill despite "strong bipartisan support from the Senate Majority Leader." It was submitted for the record of the [[House Committee on Oversight and Government Reform|House Oversight Committee]] hearing of November 13, 2024 in the statement of Tim Gallaudet, and its signers included Nolan, Skafish, [[Avi Loeb]], [[Christopher Mellon]], [[Luis Elizondo]], [[Diana Walsh Pasulka]], [[John B. Alexander]], [[Michio Kaku]], [[Jeffrey J. Kripal]], [[Kevin Knuth]], the attorney [[Dillon Guthrie]] and [[Yuan Fung]], executive director of the [[UAP Disclosure Fund]]. The letter states that signatories joined as individuals and that their participation does not represent the organizations with which they are affiliated.[^12] The [[Sol Foundation]] white paper "UAP in Crowded Skies" (March 2024) cites Senate Amendment 797 in its bibliography.[^13]

### The Senate-passed text

The Senate-passed text, Division G of S. 2226, defines "unidentified anomalous phenomena" as any object operating or judged capable of operating in outer space, the atmosphere, ocean surfaces or undersea "lacking prosaic attribution due to performance characteristics and properties not previously known to be achievable," differentiated by observables including "instantaneous acceleration absent apparent inertia," "transmedium" travel and "multispectral signature control," and defines "technologies of unknown origin" and "non-human intelligence" ("any sentient intelligent non-human lifeform regardless of nature or ultimate origin"). The records covered include those held by the Executive Office of the President, the military departments, the Department of Energy "and its progenitors, the [[Manhattan Project]]," the [[Central Intelligence Agency]] "and its progenitor, the [[Office of Strategic Services]]," the [[National Reconnaissance Office]], the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]], the [[National Security Agency]], [[NASA]], the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation]] and "any private sector person or entity formerly or currently under contract or some other agreement with the Federal Government." Section 9011 allowed the Review Board to ask the Attorney General to petition courts to release information relevant to UAP held under seal or under a grand jury's injunction of secrecy. Section 9010(a) read: "The Federal Government shall exercise eminent domain over any and all recovered technologies of unknown origin and biological evidence of non-human intelligence that may be controlled by private persons or entities in the interests of the public good."[^2]

### Senate Amendment 797 and the enacted subtitle, text compared

As printed in the [[Congressional Record]] of July 13, 2023 (page S2953), Senate Amendment 797 numbered its sections __01 to __15. Its section __10, "Disclosure of Recovered Technologies of Unknown Origin and Biological Evidence of Non-Human Intelligence," reads in subsections (a) and (b) (the heading separators of the printed text are omitted in this and the following quotations): "(a) Exercise of Eminent Domain. The Federal Government shall exercise eminent domain over any and all recovered technologies of unknown origin and biological evidence of non-human intelligence that may be controlled by private persons or entities in the interests of the public good. (b) Availability to Review Board. Any and all such material, should it exist, shall be made available to the Review Board for personal examination and subsequent disclosure determination at a location suitable to the controlling authority of said material and in a timely manner conducive to the objectives of the Review Board in accordance with the requirements of this title." Subsection (c) directs the Review Board to decide "whether the material examined constitutes technologies of unknown origin or biological evidence of non-human intelligence beyond a reasonable doubt," whether it "qualifies for postponement of disclosure," and "what changes, if any, to the current disposition of said material should the Federal Government make to facilitate full disclosure." Subsections (d) and (e) give the Board access to testimony of "unidentified anomalous phenomena witnesses, close observers and legacy program personnel and whistleblowers" and direct it to solicit more.[^14]

Definitions in the same print bear on that section. Section __03(5) defined "controlling authority" as "any Federal, State, or local government department, office, agency, committee, commission, commercial company, academic institution, or private sector entity in physical possession of technologies of unknown origin or biological evidence of non-human intelligence." Section __03(7) defined "Government office" to include any department, office or agency "that has possession or control, including via contract or other agreement, of unidentified anomalous phenomena records." Section __03(10) defined "legacy program" as "all Federal, State, and local government, commercial industry, academic, and private sector endeavors to collect, exploit, or reverse engineer technologies of unknown origin or examine biological evidence of living or deceased non-human intelligence that pre-dates the date of the enactment of this Act." Section __03(22) defined an "unidentified anomalous phenomena record" by reference to the bodies that created or came into possession of it, ending with "(W) any State or local government department, office, agency, committee, or commission that provided support or assistance or performed work, in connection with a Federal inquiry into unidentified anomalous phenomena, technologies of unknown origin, or non-human intelligence; and (X) any private sector person or entity formerly or currently under contract or some other agreement with the Federal Government."[^14]

Section __04(a)(1)(C) of the print and Section 1841(a)(1)(C) of Public Law 118-31 (December 22, 2023) use the same words for the contents of the collection: "The Collection shall consist of record copies of all Government, Government-provided, or Government-funded records relating to unidentified anomalous phenomena, technologies of unknown origin, and non-human intelligence (or equivalent subjects by any other name with the specific and sole exclusion of temporarily non-attributed objects), which shall be transmitted to the National Archives in accordance with section 2107 of title 44, United States Code."[^14][^15] The enacted subtitle (Secs. 1841 to 1843) directs each "head of a Government office" to "identify and organize records in the possession of the Government office or under the control of the Government office relating to unidentified anomalous phenomena" (Sec. 1842(a)(1)(A)), and provides in Sec. 1842(a)(2)(C): "No unidentified anomalous phenomena record created by a person or entity outside the Federal Government (excluding names or identities consistent with the requirements of section 1843) shall be withheld, redacted, postponed for public disclosure, or reclassified." Section 1843(a) lists the grounds on which the original classification authority may postpone disclosure on a finding of "clear and convincing evidence," among them that disclosure "would reveal" an intelligence agent, source or method, or "any other matter currently relating to the military defense, intelligence operations, or conduct of foreign relations of the United States, the disclosure of which would demonstrably and substantially impair the national security of the United States."[^15]

### Senate passage and the House

[[Douglas Dean Johnson]], who reported on the measure throughout 2023 and supported it, wrote that it was unveiled on July 14, 2023, and "after some modifications" was added to the defense bill as part of a list of agreed amendments "without floor debate or a separate vote." The Senate passed S. 2226 on July 27, 2023 on a roll call of 86 to 11, and then inserted the same text into H.R. 2670, the House-passed defense bill. Johnson's summary also lists a separate provision associated with Gillibrand that would bar funds in fiscal 2024 for any classified program on UAP not briefed to the appropriate committees, congressional leadership and the AARO director, and would require any person under federal contract holding material relating to UAP to make it available to the AARO director and to provide "a comprehensive list of all non-earth origin or exotic unidentified anomalous phenomena material," and an addition of 27 million dollars to the AARO budget request by the Senate Armed Services Committee. He wrote that H.R. 2670 as passed by the House contained one UAP provision, an amendment by Representative [[Tim Burchett]], and that a second amendment, by Representative [[Mike Gallagher]], was "not made in order by the House Rules Committee, apparently due to some manner of objection informally lodged by leadership of the [[House Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence]]."[^2][^16][^17]

On December 1, 2023 [[The Hill]] reported that members of the House UAP Caucus ([[Anna Paulina Luna]], Burchett, [[Matt Gaetz]], [[Jared Moskowitz]] and [[Eric Burlison]]) held a press conference after "it was reported top leaders in both chambers were attempting to kill legislation related to the effort," and wrote that "there were reports that House Armed Services Committee Chair Mike Rogers (R-Ala.), and House Intelligence Committee Chair Mike Turner (R-Ohio) were looking to quash the act," who "appeared to have the support of Senate Minority Leader Mitch McConnell (R-Ky.) and new House Speaker Mike Johnson (R-La.)." Schumer said on the Senate floor on December 4, 2023: "House Republicans are also attempting to kill another commonsense, bipartisan measure passed by the Senate."[^18][^19]

### The Pentagon redline and the conference

On July 24, 2024 Johnson published a 33-page document headed "Informal views on S. 2226 Division G: Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Disclosure," marked as reviewed by the Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security (OUSD(I&S)), which states: "After conferring with NARA/ISOO the Department strongly urges consideration of the following revisions identified below." Johnson wrote that file data indicated it was transmitted to negotiators in late November 2023. The text of Section 9010 in the document reads, with struck and inserted words running together in the text layer, "The It is the sense of Congress that the Federal Government shall should exercise eminent domain appropriately over any and all recovered technologies of unknown origin," adds "in the territory of the [[United States]]," and in the heading of subsection (b) substitutes the AARO for the Review Board as the recipient of such material. Under 50 U.S.C. 3373 the AARO director is appointed by the Secretary of Defense in consultation with the Director of National Intelligence, reports to the Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security on administrative matters, and reports to the Deputy Secretary of Defense and the Principal Deputy Director of National Intelligence on operational and security matters.[^20][^21][^22]

Johnson wrote that [[Sean Kirkpatrick]], AARO's director until December 1, 2023, told [[Marik von Rennenkampff]] on July 17, 2024: "We convinced Congress last year not to go down that road." In the same interview, as quoted by Johnson, Kirkpatrick said AARO objected that the proposal duplicated the historical report Congress had assigned in 2022 and that "I talked to all of the commercial industry, and they're scratching their heads and they don't know what they're talking about." By email on July 23, 2024 Kirkpatrick told Johnson he could not confirm the authenticity of the OUSD(I&S) document but that it "does represent many of the objections I tried to articulate." He said that no one at the [[White House]], the [[National Security Council]] or in Congress asked him to soften his position, and that the eminent domain compromise was proposed because "the conspiracy frenzy was pushing this narrative of some prime contractor having this material" and "since we know nothing exists, we didn't feel it made our job harder."[^20]

The conference report on H.R. 2670 passed the Senate on December 13, 2023 by 87 votes to 13. On the floor that day Schumer and Rounds held a colloquy. Schumer said: "We have also been notified by multiple credible sources that information on UAPs has also been withheld from Congress, which if true is a violation of laws requiring full notification to the legislative branch." Rounds listed as the most significant shortcomings of the conference agreement "the rejection, first of all, of a government-wide review board composed of expert citizens, presidentially appointed and Senate confirmed," and "a requirement as a transparency measure for the government to obtain any recovered UAP material or biological remains that may have been provided to private entities in the past and thereby hidden from Congress and the American people." Schumer said: "It is really an outrage the House didn't work with us on adopting our proposal for a review board," and: "Now it means that declassification of UAP records will be largely up to the same entities that have blocked and obfuscated their disclosure for decades." He acknowledged the late [[Harry Reid]] as "a mentor, who cared about this issue a great deal."[^23][^24]

### The enacted law

The enacted law is Public Law 118-31, signed on December 22, 2023, in Title XVIII, Subtitle C, Sections 1841 to 1843. It directs the Archivist to begin the "Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Records Collection" within 60 days, requires each head of a government office to review and organize records within 300 days, requires records transmitted for disclosure to be open for inspection within 30 days and online within 180 days after that, and provides that no record "created by a person or entity outside the Federal Government" may be "withheld, redacted, postponed for public disclosure, or reclassified" except for names or identities. It contains no review board and no eminent domain provision.[^3]

Section 1687 of the same law, "Limitation on Use of Funds for Certain Unreported Programs," provides that none of the funds authorized by the Act "may be obligated or expended in support of any activities involving unidentified anomalous phenomena protected under any form of special access or restricted access limitations unless the Secretary of Defense has provided the details of the activity to the appropriate congressional committees and congressional leadership, including for any activities described in a report released by the All-Domain Anomaly Resolution Office in fiscal year 2024." Its subsection (b) provides that, consistent with Defense Department Instruction 3204.01 on oversight of independent research and development, independent research and development funding relating to UAP "shall not be allowable as indirect expenses for purposes of contracts covered by such instruction, unless such material and information is made available the appropriate congressional committees and congressional leadership." Section 7343, in the intelligence authorization division, contains a parallel funding limitation keyed to the [[Director of National Intelligence]] and a parallel independent research and development clause, and Section 7341 amends the reporting requirement of the AARO so that its director, rather than the Director of National Intelligence and the Secretary of Defense jointly, submits it.[^26]

### Section 1687 as enacted

The enacted text of Section 1687 of Public Law 118-31 prints subsection (b) with the words "made available the appropriate congressional committees and congressional leadership." Subsection (b) carries the note "50 USC 3373 note." Subsection (c) defines "appropriate congressional committees" as "the congressional defense committees" and "the Permanent Select Committee on Intelligence of the House of Representatives and the Select Committee on Intelligence of the Senate," and "congressional leadership" as the majority leader of the Senate, the minority leader of the Senate, the Speaker of the House of Representatives and the minority leader of the House of Representatives. It defines "unidentified anomalous phenomena" by reference to section 1683(n) of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2022 (50 U.S.C. 3373(n)) as amended by section 6802(a) of the Intelligence Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2023. The section runs from 137 Stat. 621 to 622.[^15]

### Contractors, lobbying filings and campaign receipts

Johnson wrote that he had "repeatedly stated that the gutting of the bill was due not to any lobbying campaign by powerful defense contractors, as some imagined, but to opposition from the Pentagon/AARO, the objections of which were largely adopted by several of the very senior Republican lawmakers who were among the small group of lawmakers who resolved the final contentious issues on NDAA."[^20] At the 2024 hearing Representative Nancy Mace asked Luis Elizondo which contractors receive material of unknown origin and what [[Lockheed Martin]]'s expertise is; he answered "Aerospace, ma'am."[^27]

### Reintroduction

On July 11, 2024 Rounds, "for himself and Mr. Schumer," submitted Senate Amendment 2610 to the fiscal 2025 defense bill (S. 4638) as Division I, the "Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Disclosure Act of 2024." It retained the nine-member Review Board, the September 30, 2030 termination date and the eminent domain section, authorized 20,000,000 dollars for fiscal year 2025, and in Section 1115 repealed Subtitle C of Title XVIII of Public Law 118-31. Johnson reported that the House-passed defense bill (H.R. 8070) contained no UAP provisions.[^20][^33] At the 2024 hearing Garcia said that "earlier this year," as part of the House defense authorization bill, he had filed an amendment to include the UAP Disclosure Act, "which would create a UAP Records Review Board with exercise of eminent domain over UAP-related material," that "the amendment was blocked," and that the Senate had included the Rounds-Schumer amendment.[^7]

On July 29, 2025 Schumer, "for himself, Mr. Rounds, and Mrs. Gillibrand," submitted Senate Amendment 3111 to the fiscal 2026 defense bill (S. 2296) as the "Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Disclosure Act of 2025." On October 9, 2025 the Senate adopted by voice vote a manager's package of 48 listed amendments that did not include Amendment 3111, and passed S. 2296 by 77 votes to 20.[^34][^35]

On July 22, 2026 Burlison announced that the House had adopted his UAP Disclosure Act as an amendment to the fiscal 2027 defense bill. His release states that it would create a permanent records collection at the National Archives, "establish an independent UAP Records Review Board," and "extend disclosure requirements to covered government contractors," and that it "would support President Trump's Presidential Unsealing and Reporting System for UAP Encounters (PURSUE)."[^36]
