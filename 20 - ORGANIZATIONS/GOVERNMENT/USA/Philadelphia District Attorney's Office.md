The Philadelphia District Attorney's Office is the prosecutor for the city and county of [[Philadelphia]]. It has been led since 2018 by [[Larry Krasner]]. In 2025 it prosecuted [[Eugene Horsch]] on drug counts, and after his arrest in June 2026 it charged him with illegal gun and drug possession; on August 6, 2026, it withdrew those charges in favor of the federal prosecution.[^1][^2][^3]

### District Attorneys

[[James C. Crumlish Jr.]] was district attorney from 1962 to 1966 and later sat as a judge of the [[Commonwealth Court of Pennsylvania|Commonwealth Court]] until his death in 1992.[^4] On January 19, 2012, the [[Supreme Court of Pennsylvania]], in an opinion by Chief Justice [[Ronald Castille]], held that the Philadelphia costs-of-prosecution statute did not permit the office to recover the salaries of its assistant district attorneys and county detectives from [[Gerald Garzone]] and [[Louis Garzone]], the funeral directors it had prosecuted in the body-parts case of 2008.[^5]

On March 21, 2017, a federal grand jury returned a 23-count indictment charging District Attorney [[R. Seth Williams|Rufus Seth Williams]] with bribery, extortion, and honest services wire fraud "in connection with tens of thousands of dollars' worth of concealed bribes that he received from two business owners in exchange for his agreement to perform official acts," and with defrauding a nursing home and family friends of money meant for a relative's care. The case was brought by the [[United States Attorney for the District of New Jersey|United States Attorney's Office for the District of New Jersey]] with the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]]'s Philadelphia Division, [[Internal Revenue Service|IRS]] Criminal Investigation, and [[Homeland Security Investigations]].[^6]

Krasner first ran for district attorney in 2017. On November 4, 2025, he won a third term against the Republican nominee, [[Patrick Dugan]], a former judge of the Philadelphia Municipal Court.[^1]

### The Horsch Prosecutions

[[R.C. Horsch]] said that staff of the office attended the 1973 premiere of his film [[The Erotic Memoirs of a Male Chauvinist Pig]] at the 1812 Theater on Chestnut Street.[^7] In March 2009 the office, through Assistant District Attorney [[Jill Fertel]], prosecuted him for approaching a nine-year-old girl in [[Kensington, Philadelphia|Kensington]].[^8]

Firefighters answered a small fire on the second floor of the house at 417 West Chew Avenue on May 18, 2025. A fire marshal and a police patrolman found a marijuana grow, a BCI Defense rifle of AR-15 pattern modified to fire automatically, a 12-gauge Stevens Model 67 shotgun with a sawed-off barrel, a 9mm Girsan MC28 pistol, and more than a hundred rounds of ammunition; the serial numbers had been destroyed on all three guns. According to a spokesperson for Krasner, the defense lawyer [[Jerome Brown]] told the court that the weapons had belonged to Eugene Horsch's father, who had died three days before the raid, and that Eugene Horsch had planned to dispose of them properly. He pleaded guilty to manufacturing drugs, prosecutors withdrew the gun charges, and he received three years of probation.[^2]

After his arrest near [[Independence Hall]] on June 19, 2026, the office charged him with illegal gun and drug possession. On the morning of August 6, 2026, Assistant District Attorney [[Alyssa Amoroso]] withdrew the charges, telling a city judge that the [[United States Attorney for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania|United States Attorney's Office]] would handle the case. Neither Horsch nor Brown attended the hearing.[^3] Amoroso, a 2017 graduate of the [[Villanova University]] law school, had interned in the office's Municipal Court Unit in 2016.[^9]

### Referral of Krasner

On September 15, 2026, in the federal habeas proceeding over Dennis Johnson's 2009 conviction for second-degree murder, Judge [[Paul S. Diamond]] of the [[United States District Court for the Eastern District of Pennsylvania]] wrote: "I have thus reluctantly concluded that Philadelphia's highest law enforcement official and one of his top assistants may have violated the law." He referred "the DAO, Mr. Krasner, and Stiegler to this District's [[United States]] Attorney's Office for investigation and other appropriate action," writing that a possible threat by Krasner "could amount to obstruction of justice," that efforts to mislead the court "might amount to conspiracy," and that the supervisor Matthew Stiegler "may have committed perjury while on the stand." Krasner said: "I respectfully and vigorously disagree," and "I look forward to appealing to the higher court as soon as the law allows."[^10]
