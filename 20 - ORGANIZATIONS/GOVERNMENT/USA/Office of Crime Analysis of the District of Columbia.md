The Office of Crime Analysis of the District of Columbia was a small governmental research and analysis unit operating within the DC government. It had no federal counterpart and appears to have operated primarily to support criminal justice planning and prosecution management for the District.

### Role in PROMIS Development

In 1969, the office administered the $60,000 LEAA grant (70-DF-047) to [[Peat, Marwick, Mitchell & Co]] that funded the design of the original [[PROMIS|Prosecutor's Management Information System (PROMIS)]] for the [[U.S. Attorney's office|DC U.S. Attorney's Office]]. [[Joan E. Jacoby]], then Director of the office, co-directed the design team alongside [[Charles R. Work]] (Deputy Director of the Superior Court Division of the U.S. Attorney's Office), with [[Bill Hamilton]] of Peat Marwick serving as project manager.[^1] A May 1971 system overview prepared for the office under LEAA Grant No. 70-DF-047 states that the design team "was headed by Mrs. Joan E. Jacoby, Director of the Office of Crime Analysis," and that the system became operational on January 1, 1971.[^3]

The office described PROMIS as the critical component of its own Project TRACE (Tracking, Retrieval and Analysis of Criminal Events), an offender-tracking system from arrest through sentencing and incarceration, and the LEAA funded both TRACE and PROMIS.[^3] The case evaluation systems used in PROMIS were developed by the office for the U.S. Attorney's office in 1969.[^4]

Jacoby is listed as director on the office's 1972 *Final Report: Project TRACE*.[^4] She subsequently became the first Executive Director of the [[National Center for Prosecution Management]], a nonprofit established with LEAA funding through a grant to the [[National District Attorneys Association]] to provide technical assistance to prosecutors nationwide.[^2][^5]
