The [[Office of Naval Research]] (ONR) is an agency within the [[U.S. Navy]] responsible for the science and technology programs of the [[U.S. Navy]] and Marine Corps. It funds basic and applied research to develop new technologies and capabilities for naval operations.[^1]

In the context of psychic research, the ONR funded the development of the superconducting-shielded magnetometer used by [[Arthur Hebard]] at [[Stanford University]], which was later used in [[Hal Puthoff]]'s experiments with [[Ingo Swann]]. The ONR also provided contracts to scientists like [[Allan H. Frey]] for research on the effects of microwave radiation on the human body.[^1]

### The Jessup annotated copy

The introduction to the Annotated Edition of [[Morris K. Jessup]]'s [[The Case for the UFO]] states that the annotated paperback arrived at ONR in a manila envelope addressed to "Admiral N. Furth, Chief, Office of Naval Research, Washington 25, D.C.," postmarked [[Seminole, Texas]], 1955, marked "Happy Easter"; that in July or August of that year it appeared in the incoming correspondence of Major [[Darrell L. Ritter]], USMC, of the Aeronautical Project Office; that Captain [[Sidney Sherby]], on reporting aboard, obtained it from Ritter; and that Sherby and Commander [[George W. Hoover]], Special Projects Officer, "indicated direct interest in some of the material therein" in light of "the possibility of discovering clues to the nature of gravity." The [[Varo Manufacturing Company]] of [[Garland, Texas]] "offered to re-publish the book together with all notations in a very limited edition."[^2] [[Gray Barker]], in his 1973 preface to the facsimile, wrote that Admiral Furth received the envelope in April 1956 and that Sherby and Hoover "were deeply involved in satellite development."[^2] ONR's house publication reported that Rear Admiral [[Rawson Bennett II]] relieved Rear Admiral [[Frederick R. Furth]] as Chief of Naval Research on December 31, 1955, that Furth had served two years as Chief, that his duties had included the "Vanguard" satellite project, and that he was to join the [[ITT Corporation|International Telephone and Telegraph Corporation]].[^3] The first [[Carlos Allende|Allende]] letter to Jessup, dated January 13, 1956, advised him to "contact Rear Admiral Ransom Bennett for verification of info herein. Navy Chief of research."[^2]

### McDonald, 1966

[[Paul E. McCarthy]] wrote that [[James E. McDonald]]'s ONR project monitor, [[Jim Hughes (Office of Naval Research)|Jim Hughes]], told him in May 1966 that, because McDonald's work was for ONR, it would pay the trip expenses to read the Blue Book files at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]; that McDonald wrote Hughes that he wished a briefing on "the rumored Navy studies of UFOs" and asked whether ONR might fund his travel for UFO research; and that this began "a controversy within a controversy" over use of ONR atmospheric research funds for UFO research. McCarthy also wrote that in early 1968 McDonald sent Hughes copies of the [[Condon Committee]] "Low memo" and [[National Academy of Sciences]] correspondence, in light of "the day when he would apply to the ONR for funding to do UFO work."[^4][^5]
