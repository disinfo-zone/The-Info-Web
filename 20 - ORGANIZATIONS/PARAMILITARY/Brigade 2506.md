Brigade 2506 was the force of Cuban exiles organized, trained and equipped by the [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]] for the landing at the [[Bay of Pigs]] on April 17, 1961. It took its name from the membership number of a trainee, Carlos Rafael Santana, who died in a training accident on September 8, 1960. At the invasion it numbered about 1,400 to 1,500 men in six infantry battalions, with five tanks, sixteen B-26 bombers and transport aircraft. Of its members 114 were killed and about 1,179 captured.[^1][^2] On December 21, 1962, the New York lawyer James B. Donovan, recruited by [[Robert F. Kennedy]], signed an agreement with [[Fidel Castro]] exchanging 1,113 surviving prisoners for 53 million dollars in food and medicine. At the Orange Bowl in Miami on December 29, the brigade gave President [[John F. Kennedy]] its flag, which he said he would return "to a free Havana."[^2]

### Veterans

Veterans of the brigade formed the core of later CIA operations against Cuba.[^3] [[Bernard Barker]], [[Howard Hunt]]'s contact in Miami for the break-ins at the office of [[Daniel Ellsberg]]'s psychiatrist and at the [[Watergate]], had served under Hunt in the Bay of Pigs operation; he and the burglars [[Eugenio Martinez]] and [[Frank Sturgis]] were Bay of Pigs veterans.[^3][^5] [[Felix Rodriguez]], a veteran, took part in the capture of [[Che Guevara]] in Bolivia and in the 1980s ran the Contra resupply operation at [[Ilopango Airbase]] in [[El Salvador]] as "Max Gomez."[^3] [[Felipe Rivero]], a veteran, founded the [[Cuban Nationalist Movement]] with [[Guillermo Novo]] and [[Ignacio Novo]].[^6] [[Luis D. Elizondo III]], father of [[Luis Elizondo]], fought with the brigade and was held in Cuba for two years.[^7]

### The Veterans' Association and the Letelier Case

The brigade's veterans' association was one of five groups that formed [[CORU]], the Coordination of United Revolutionary Organizations, under [[Orlando Bosch]] in the [[Dominican Republic]] on June 11, 1976, together with Bosch's Acción Cubana, the Cuban Nationalist Movement, the Cuban National Liberation Front and the 17th of April Movement. "We were conspiring there," Bosch later said. "Planning bombings and killings. Everything was planned there."[^4]

In early September 1976, three weeks before the assassination of [[Orlando Letelier]] in Washington, the brigade's president, Roberto Carballo, Manuel Camargo and [[Bernardo de Torres]] called on Admiral [[Ronald McIntyre]] of the Chilean naval mission there. They told him the leadership was committed to uniting anti-Castro Cubans and ending the bombings of small groups in Miami; the "only discordant note" was Carballo's feeling "over the detention in Chile and handover to US authorities of the Cuban Otero," meaning [[Rolando Otero]].[^8] An undated memorandum to Colonel [[Manuel Contreras]] of Chile's [[DINA]], written after October 1976, reported that "the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]] reached the conclusion that the perpetrators of the attack on Letelier were 4 members of Brigade 2506," naming Roberto Carvallo (Carballo), Carlos Pascual, Vicente Vásquez and Guillermo Novo, who "would have been deceived and paid by a Castro agent."[^9] By May 1977 the Letelier grand jury had summoned Armando López Estrada of the veterans' association among fifteen Cuban exiles.[^6]
