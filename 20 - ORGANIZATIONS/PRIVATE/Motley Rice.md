Motley Rice LLC is a plaintiffs' litigation firm founded in [[Mount Pleasant, South Carolina]], in April 2003 by [[Ron Motley]] (1944 to 2013) and [[Joe Rice]] (1954 to 2026), who as lead private counsel for state attorneys general negotiated the 246 billion dollar tobacco [[Master Settlement Agreement]] of 1998. On contingency, it represented the [[Government of the United States Virgin Islands]] in its 2020 suit against the estate of [[Jeffrey Epstein]], which settled in 2022 for 105 million dollars, and in [[Government of the United States Virgin Islands v. JPMorgan Chase Bank]], which settled in 2023 for 75 million dollars. The firm stood to receive more than 23 million dollars of the first settlement and received 20 million dollars of the second.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

### The Firm

Motley and Rice began working together in the 1970s in asbestos litigation at a firm in Barnwell, South Carolina, later Ness, Motley, Loadholt, Richardson & Poole. The firm credits itself with more than 50 cases in the aviation litigation arising from the [[September 11 attacks]] and with representing more than 6,600 family members, survivors and first responders in continuing litigation; with the terrorism-financing case Linde v. [[Arab Bank]] under the [[Anti-Terrorism Act]]; as co-lead negotiator of the [[Deepwater Horizon]] class settlement with [[BP]]; and as co-lead counsel for more than 2,700 communities in the National Prescription Opiate multidistrict litigation.[^1][^2]

### The Estate Case

On January 15, 2020 Attorney General [[Denise George]] sued Epstein's estate in the Superior Court of the Virgin Islands under the territory's Criminally Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act, naming the co-executors [[Darren Indyke]] and [[Richard Kahn]] and Epstein's companies. According to [[Federal Bureau of Investigation|FBI]] notes of her October 2, 2023 interview, George consulted Governor [[Albert Bryan]] before filing and was told, "He's dead go for it," and Motley Rice's forensic accountants traced companies and limited liability companies connected to Epstein's [[Southern Trust Company]]. When the firm began interviewing estate employees, who she said "had nondisclosure agreements and were informed if questioned by law enforcement to say nothing and alert Kellerhals," Bryan's chief legal counsel, [[David Bornn]], a former employee of the estate lawyer [[Erika Kellerhals]], called George to relay that Kellerhals wanted to know what she was investigating. According to the same notes, George told the FBI that Bryan later asked her, "originally when I said go ahead I didn't think it was going to go this far. I can't get you to let this go?" Bryan has denied wrongdoing in his dealings with Epstein.[^5][^6]

The case settled on December 1, 2022. The estate agreed to pay 105 million dollars in cash, to return more than 80 million dollars in economic development tax benefits obtained through Southern Trust's certificate, to give the territory half the proceeds of the sale of [[Little St. James]], and to pay 450,000 dollars for remediation on [[Great St. James]]. Motley Rice stood to receive more than 23 million dollars of the 105 million. Indyke and Kahn were not charged and have denied wrongdoing.[^3][^4][^5]

### The JPMorgan Case

George filed the territory's suit against [[JPMorgan Chase]] on December 27, 2022 before Judge [[Jed Rakoff]] in the [[U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New York]], alleging that the bank had knowingly facilitated and profited from Epstein's sex trafficking while keeping him as a client from 1998 to 2013. Bryan removed her four days later. The territory's lawyers of record included [[Linda Singer]] and Elizabeth Paige Boggs of Motley Rice.[^7][^8]

In February 2023 the firm sought to serve [[Leslie Wexner]] with a document subpoena. According to its sworn declaration, Wexner's longtime [[Columbus, Ohio|Columbus]] lawyer John W. Zeiger of [[Zeiger, Tigges & Little]] did not return calls about accepting service, and his partner Marion Little said that Zeiger would not accept it "without being given a detailed description of the Subpoena's contents," and, after hearing a general description, "instructed me not to send him the Subpoena." The territory moved on February 17, 2023 to serve Wexner by certified mail.[^9]

Singer examined JPMorgan's chief executive, [[Jamie Dimon]], on May 26, 2023, and objected on the record to "counsel coaching the witness on answers," which she said "has happened a number of times." Dimon testified that the bank "did not need introductions to anybody."[^10]

On May 28, 2023 JPMorgan produced a 22-page summary of its internal "Project Jeep" review. On June 7, 2023 Motley Rice asked Rakoff to reopen depositions, citing the summary's "more than four pages of Epstein referrals to JPMorgan," including "[[Andrew Farkas]], [[Boris Nikolic]], [[Bill Gates]], and [[Peter Mandelson]]"; emails showing that the former [[Bear Stearns]] chairman [[Alan Greenberg]] had gone to general counsel [[Stephen Cutler]] "for an exception to the felony policy" for Epstein; a January 19, 2011 message from a JPMorgan Securities employee to Epstein ("Ace would love to speak to Jeffrey"); and a 2008 email stating that "JPM requires top of the house ok for clients who are convicted felons. (ie PCS Legal to Asset Mgt Legal to Cutler to Jaime Daimnon [sic])." The bank's counsel at [[WilmerHale]] answered that "top of the house" meant Cutler. In August 2023 Rakoff ordered production of records concerning newly identified women Epstein had paid, but denied the request "to compel production of all documents and information concerning 'Project Jeep' or other investigations by JPMorgan that occurred after Epstein's 2019 arrest."[^11][^12]

On September 26, 2023 JPMorgan agreed to pay the territory 75 million dollars without admitting wrongdoing: 30 million dollars to organizations combating human trafficking, 25 million dollars to strengthen the territory's law enforcement, and 20 million dollars in attorneys' fees. The territory received about 180 million dollars in Epstein-related settlements after Motley Rice's fees; as of January 2026 the Bryan administration had not filed the accounting of the money that a 2024 territorial law requires.[^5][^6][^13]
