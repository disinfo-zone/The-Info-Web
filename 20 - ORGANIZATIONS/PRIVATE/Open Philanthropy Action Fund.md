The Open Philanthropy Action Fund is the 501(c)(4) social welfare organization of [[Dustin Moskovitz]] and [[Cari Tuna]], whose donations are not tax-deductible. Its current legal name is Coefficient Giving Action Fund, following the November 2025 renaming of [[Open Philanthropy]] as Coefficient Giving. It holds EIN 81-2644663, with an [[Internal Revenue Service|IRS]] ruling date of August 1, 2018, and files in care of [[Apercen Partners]] LLC at 314 Lytton Avenue, [[Palo Alto]], the address from which the couple's private foundation, the Good Ventures Foundation, also files.[^1][^2][^3] In 2018 the *[[Stanford Social Innovation Review]]* writer [[Marc Gunther]] listed it among the seven entities in which Tuna served "as a director or major donor," describing it as a 501(c)(4) "that supports nonpartisan advocacy."[^4]

### Officers

The fund's officers and directors have included the three directors of the [[Good Ventures]] Foundation and the chief executives of Open Philanthropy, none of them paid by the fund. On the return for 2017 Tuna was president and director, [[Holden Karnofsky]] executive director, [[Divesh Makan]] secretary and director, and Moskovitz a director, with [[Tom Van Loben Sels]] as treasurer. [[Elie Hassenfeld]], who had cofounded [[GiveWell]] with Karnofsky, was a director in 2017 and 2018, and received 186,582 and 222,900 dollars in those years from a related organization. [[Alexander Berger]] joined as a director on the return for 2018, when Karnofsky was styled chief executive officer. Karnofsky and Berger were each listed as co-CEO and director for 2021 and 2022, matching their titles at Open Philanthropy from June 2021, and the return for 2023 listed both as "co-CEO thru 7/23/23," with Berger as chief executive thereafter. On the return for 2024 Berger was chief executive and director and Karnofsky, who had left Open Philanthropy's staff in April 2024 and joined [[Anthropic]] in January 2025, a director only.[^1][^5]

### Finances

The fund reported 55.22 million dollars in contributions for 2016, its first year, and none for 2017. It reported contributions of 4.81 million dollars for 2018, 1.34 million for 2019, 16.0 million for 2020, 66.52 million for 2021, 48.82 million for 2022 and 77.71 million for 2023, about 270.4 million dollars over the eight years. Its expenses were 4.98 million dollars in 2016, 24.12 million in 2019, 89.32 million in 2021 and 77.51 million in 2023. From 2021 its liabilities exceeded its assets: at the end of 2021 it reported 54.06 million dollars in liabilities against 40.72 million in assets, and its net assets stood at minus 18.52 million dollars at the end of 2022 and minus 16.70 million at the end of 2023. It reported gross sales of securities of 51.37 million dollars in 2016, 63.97 million in 2021, 49.80 million in 2022 and 77.37 million in 2023.[^2]

### Real Justice PAC

On October 29, 2019, the fund gave 750,000 dollars to [[Real Justice PAC]], a [[San Francisco]] hybrid PAC with a non-contribution account, which reported the receipt on its 2019 year-end report as "Other Federal Receipts" to its "NON-CONTRIBUTION ACCOUNT," listing the fund's address as 314 Lytton Avenue, Suite 200, Palo Alto. It is the only itemized receipt from the fund in [[Federal Election Commission]] records.[^6] Tuna had given Real Justice PAC 1.9 million dollars in her own name between March 2017 and April 2019.[^7]

### Lobbying

The fund registered as a federal lobbying client on February 22, 2021, describing itself as a "non-profit studying and promoting effective giving for policy, advocacy and social welfare." Its first registrant, the law firm [[Arnold & Porter]] Kaye Scholer, reported 50,000 dollars in the first quarter of 2021 for work on "federal support for rapid antigen testing" and terminated the engagement in the third quarter with no further activity. [[Tarplin, Downs & Young]] reported 270,000 dollars in 2021 and a final 20,000 dollars in the first quarter of 2022 on "pandemic preparedness, innovation, supply chain, biodefense" and appropriations, naming S. 1260, the U.S. Innovation and Competition Act of 2021; the budget resolution S. Con. Res. 14; H.R. 3, the Lower Drug Costs Now Act; and H.R. 5376, the Build Back Better Act.[^8]

The [[Kyle House Group]] registered the fund in April 2023 for "education and advocacy in support of Open Philanthropy," amended to "education and advocacy regarding U.S. foreign assistance policies and programs, with a focus on cost-effectiveness and innovation." It reported 170,000 dollars for the last three quarters of 2023, 90,000 dollars a quarter in 2024 and 110,000 dollars a quarter from 2025, on the House and Senate State and Foreign Operations appropriations bills, including H.R. 4665 and H.R. 8771 in 2024, H.R. 5300 and H.R. 7006 in 2025, and H.R. 8595, the National Security, Department of State, and Related Programs Appropriations Act, 2027, in 2026. Through the second quarter of 2026 the three firms had reported about 1.53 million dollars in lobbying income from the fund, and none of the filings listed artificial intelligence as an issue.[^8]
