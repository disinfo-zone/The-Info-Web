The Washington Examiner is a political publication in [[Washington, D.C.]] owned by the Denver billionaire [[Philip Anschutz]]. It grew out of Journal Newspapers Inc., the [[Alexandria, Virginia]] publisher of the *Northern Virginia Journal*, the *Prince George's Journal* and the *Montgomery Journal*, which Anschutz bought in 2004.[^1] It launched as a free paper in 2005.[^2]

### Anschutz purchase

On September 30, 2004 Anschutz, a founder of [[Qwest Communications]] and an investor in [[D.C. United]], announced the purchase of Journal Newspapers through [[Clarity Media Group]], "a newly created investment arm" headed by the former [[Denver Post]] publisher [[Ryan McKibben]] and the paper's former chief financial officer Frederick Anderson. Clarity did not disclose financial terms. Anschutz also owned the *[[San Francisco Examiner]],* a majority stake in [[Regal Entertainment Group]] and four [[Major League Soccer]] teams. The three suburban dailies had a combined circulation of 115,000 and had passed through several owners and heavy layoffs; Clarity said it would add "regional, national, and world news" while keeping the free tabloid format. McKibben said: "I'm not going to say the Washington Times or The Washington Post is our competition."[^1]

### Examiner chain

Anschutz had bought the *San Francisco Examiner* for 11 million dollars in 2004 and extended its "Eagle" masthead to his other Examiner papers. By June 2009 the *Washington Examiner* distributed 260,000 copies on Thursdays, with advertisers including [[Macy's]], [[Southwest Airlines]] and [[Citibank]], and Clarity had closed its *Baltimore Examiner*. That month the sale of *[[The Weekly Standard]]* to Anschutz was announced; Dirk Smillie of *[[Forbes]]* wrote that the magazine lost an estimated 5 million dollars a year on a circulation of 80,000.[^2]

### Coverage of effective altruism

In November 2025 the Examiner reporter [[Robert Schmad]] published "Effective Altruism-linked nonprofit placed fellows in key government offices, secured influence," on the [[Horizon Institute for Public Service]], followed in April 2026 by "Documents show how a tech-connected nonprofit shaped Biden-era AI policy."[^3][^4] In December 2025 *[[Semafor]]* noted a critical Examiner piece on the donor ties of the [[Tarbell Center for AI Journalism]].[^5]
