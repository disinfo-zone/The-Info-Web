The Morkhoven Workgroup was an NGO that played a central role in exposing the international child pornography network operated through the [[Apollo Bulletin Board Service]] from Zandvoort, Netherlands. Private investigator [[Marcel Vervloesem]] investigated the disappearance of [[Manuel Schadwald]] for the Morkhoven Workgroup, tracing connections from [[Lothar Glandorf]] to [[Gerrit-Jan Ulrich]] and the Apollo BBS in June 1998. When Ulrich handed Vervloesem encoded disks containing evidence of the network, Vervloesem passed them to his colleague [[Gina Pardaens-Bernaer]] within the Morkhoven Workgroup, who made copies before they were given to police.[^1]

Pardaens-Bernaer identified a perpetrator linked to the [[Marc Dutroux]] network in snuff film materials from the Apollo Disks before her death in November 1998. Members of the Morkhoven Workgroup also believed they had identified [[Katrien de Cuyper]] in pornographic photos found on the Apollo Disks. In February 2001, the Morkhoven Workgroup tracked down a transvestite named [[Robert Jan Warmerdam]] in Amsterdam, who claimed to have known both [[Robbie Van Der Plancken]] and Marc Dutroux, leading to further revelations about the connections between Dutch and Belgian trafficking networks. Vervloesem was later convicted by an Antwerp court in 2008 for publication of child pornography and sexual abuse of three minors, which allowed facts concerning the Apollo investigation to be dismissed ad hominem.[^1]

### Zandvoort material

On July 18, 1998 La Repubblica reported that the group held thousands of pornographic documents found in the Dutch house of Gerrit Ulrich, the man killed the previous month in Pisa. Its spokesman said the Belgian and Dutch files showed the same names and linked the network to branches in the Philippines and Eastern European countries.[^2] The 2022 parliamentary commission wrote that the group had handed 90,000 images to Interpol, and that the NOVA current-affairs programme had received material through the group; it also noted media reports of the founder's 2008 conviction (four years, Antwerp appeal court) and 2013 acquittal on appeal in a nephew case.[^3] Hulse wrote that the group was set up in 1990.[^4]

### Letters to the Dutch Parliament

The Eerste Kamer's lists of incoming documents record four letters from the Werkgroep Morkhoven:

- A letter from the group's chairman on a child pornography network in Zandvoort, listed as of interest to members of the Justice and Development Cooperation committees (sitting of September 22, 1998).[^5]
- A letter on the disappearance of children, sexual exploitation and torture of children, listed as of interest to members of the Foreign Affairs, European and Defence committees (sitting of April 20, 1999).[^6]
- A letter on a child trial, disappearance, abduction and youth prostitution, listed for the Justice, European and Interior committees (sitting of September 24, 1996).[^7]
- A letter asking for a thorough investigation of the perpetrators in the Temse, Hulst, Utrecht and Madeira video tapes, listed for the Foreign Affairs committee (sitting of October 1, 1996).[^8]
