Giving What We Can is a society whose members pledge to give at least ten percent of their income to the charities they judge most cost-effective. By its own account it was "the brainchild of Toby Ord, a philosopher at Balliol College, Oxford," who, inspired by the ideas of ethicists such as [[Peter Singer]], decided in 2009 to commit a large part of his income to charity and worked with a fellow Oxford philosopher, [[William MacAskill]], to form a society of people who would do the same. Ord, his wife, the physician Bernadette Young, and MacAskill launched it in November 2009; within a year 64 people had joined, pledging 21 million dollars, and it took on full-time staff in the summer of 2012. It lists as early supporters the [[Future of Humanity Institute]], Balliol College and the Oxford Uehiro Centre for Practical Ethics.[^1] Singer took the pledge in November 2009.[^2]

[[Toby Ord]] set his own living allowance at about 18,000 pounds a year, giving away the rest toward a lifetime target of one million pounds.[^3] MacAskill went on to found [[80,000 Hours]] with [[Benjamin Todd]] in 2011 and to write the longtermist *What We Owe the Future.*[^4]

### Effective Ventures and FTX

Giving What We Can became one of the projects hosted by [[Effective Ventures]], the umbrella of two charities, Effective Ventures Foundation in the United Kingdom and Effective Ventures Foundation USA, alongside the [[Centre for Effective Altruism]], 80,000 Hours and the EA Funds.[^5] In 2022 Effective Ventures and its projects received about 27 million dollars from [[Sam Bankman-Fried]]'s [[FTX]] and the FTX Foundation. On December 13, 2023, it agreed to return 26,786,503 dollars to the FTX bankruptcy estate, a sum equal to all of the money received, and its boards then decided to spin out every project as an independent organization.[^5][^6]
