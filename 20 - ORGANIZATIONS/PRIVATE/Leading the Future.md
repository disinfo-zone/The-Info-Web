Leading the Future is a super PAC, a political committee that may make only independent expenditures, registered with the [[Federal Election Commission]] on August 15, 2025, under identifier C00916114. Its address is 675 South Green Valley Parkway, #1333, [[Henderson, Nevada]], and its treasurer of record is [[Britney Gonzalez]] of [[Mountain Brook, Alabama]]. The email contact on its filings uses the domain of the [[Crosby Ottenhoff Group]], which the committee also pays as a compliance consultant.[^1][^2] Its leaders, [[Zac Moffatt]] and [[Josh Vlasto]], described the [[RAISE Act]] of the New York Assembly member [[Alex Bores]] in November 2025 as "ideological and politically motivated legislation" and said that "bills like the RAISE Act threaten American competitiveness, limit economic growth."[^3]

### Donors

Through its report of July 15, 2026, Leading the Future had received 75,100,000 dollars in contributions and 75,788,224.10 dollars in total receipts, had disbursed 44,755,780.65 dollars and held 31,032,443.45 dollars in cash.[^4] The contributions came from four sources:

- A16Z Capital Management LLC of [[Menlo Park, California]], the management company of [[Andreessen Horowitz]], gave 25,000,000 dollars on August 22, 2025, and another 25,000,000 dollars on February 11, 2026. Each check was attributed on the filing, as a partnership contribution, half to [[Ben Horowitz]] of [[Las Vegas]] and half to [[Marc Andreessen]] of [[Los Altos, California]], at 12,500,000 dollars each.
- [[Greg Brockman]], president and co-founder of [[OpenAI]], gave 12,500,000 dollars on September 12, 2025.
- [[Anna Brockman]] gave 12,500,000 dollars on the same day.
- Perplex AI Inc., the company behind [[Perplexity]], gave 100,000 dollars on August 22, 2025.[^1]

The remaining itemized receipts are interest and similar income on the committee's account at [[Chain Bridge Bank]].[^1]

### Transfers

Almost all of the committee's spending consists of transfers to other committees. On each of four dates, December 15, 2025, and February 24, May 19 and June 8, 2026, it gave 5,000,000 dollars to a committee called [[Think Big (PAC)|Think Big]] and 5,000,000 dollars to one called [[American Mission (PAC)|American Mission]], 40,000,000 dollars in all. It also gave 3,000,000 dollars to [[American Mission Florida]] on March 4, 2026, and 500,000 dollars to [[Build American AI]] on October 9, 2025. These transfers came to 43.5 million of its 44.76 million dollars in disbursements. The rest went mostly to [[Prusik Partners]] for strategy, management and communications consulting, to the law firm [[Wiley Rein]], to [[Schoen Survey Research]] for polling (89,000 dollars in October 2025) and to Crosby Ottenhoff.[^2]

### Think Big

Think Big (FEC identifier C00923417) reported 23,021,600.71 dollars in receipts, 20,760,549.23 dollars in disbursements and 17,224,117.88 dollars in independent expenditures.[^5] Its largest single target was Bores, then a candidate in the Democratic primary for [[New York]]'s 12th congressional district;[^6] the FEC's aggregation of its independent expenditures by candidate shows 8,145,697.68 dollars spent opposing him, in 55 transactions, all through the vendor [[Lantern Production Consultants]].[^5] In February 2026 *[[TechCrunch]]* reported that Leading the Future had "already poured $1.1 million into ads attacking Bores" when the [[Anthropic]]-funded [[Public First Action]] began spending in his support.[^6] Think Big also reported expenditures supporting other candidates, among them [[Jesse Jackson Jr.]] and [[Melissa Bean]] in [[Illinois]], [[Ben McAdams]] in [[Utah]], [[Ritchie Torres]], [[Joseph Morelle]] and [[Yvette Clarke]] in New York, [[Jimmy Panetta]] in [[California]], [[Rob Menendez|Robert Menendez]] in [[New Jersey]], [[Greg Stanton]] in [[Arizona]] and [[Valerie Hoyle]] in [[Oregon]].[^5]

The counter-campaign for Bores was run by the Jobs and Democracy PAC, funded through the Public First super PAC, which the FEC's aggregation shows spent 15,026,937.99 dollars supporting him.[^7]

### American Mission

American Mission (FEC identifier C00916692) reported 23,943,009.62 dollars in receipts, 13,412,103.80 dollars in disbursements and 10,853,994.82 dollars in independent expenditures. Its expenditures, placed through [[Summit Ridge Media Group]], supported candidates in many states, among them [[Andy Barr]] in [[Kentucky]], [[Julia Letlow]] in [[Louisiana]], [[Clay Fuller]] and [[James Kingston]] in [[Georgia]], [[Chris Gober]] and [[Jessica Hart Steinmann]] in [[Texas]], [[Aaron Flint]] in [[Montana]], [[Laurie Buckhout]] in [[North Carolina]] and [[Kevin Hern]] in [[Oklahoma]].[^5] Hern also received independent expenditures in his support from the Defending Our Values PAC of the Public First Action group.[^7]
