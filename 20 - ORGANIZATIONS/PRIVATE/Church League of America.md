The Church League of America was founded in [[Chicago]] in 1937 to oppose left-wing and Social Gospel influence in the Protestant churches. Its first director was [[Frank J. Loesch]], head of the [[Chicago Crime Commission]]. It grew in the 1950s under Major [[Edgar C. Bundy]], a former officer of [[United States Air Force|Air Force]] intelligence, and moved to [[Wheaton, Illinois]], in 1961. It kept files on individuals and organizations and sold reports from them.[^1]

### The Wackenhut Files

The [[Wackenhut Corporation]] had files on 2.5 million people by 1971, built in part on the research files of [[Karl Baarslag]], which he had given or sold to it. After the [[Fair Credit Reporting Act]] took effect the company reduced its holdings to between 225,000 and 250,000 files, and in 1975 it gave many of them to the Church League, which "was not bound by the legislation, but had a similar political outlook and goals as the Wackenhut Corporation." [[George R. Wackenhut|George Wackenhut]] was a contributor to the league, and the company kept the right to consult what it had given away. "For years," one writer on the private security industry observed in 1978, "Wackenhut relied upon the dossiers of the Church League of America."[^1][^2]

The league dissolved in 1984. Its files went to [[Liberty University]] in [[Lynchburg, Virginia]], which gave them in 1992 to the [[Tamiment Library]] of [[New York University]]. The Wackenhut series fills eleven boxes and runs from 1931 to 1973. Its folders include "Subversive Activities," in 1,064 numbered serials, the [[John Birch Society]], the [[Minutemen (anti-Communist organization)|Minutemen]], the [[Highlander Folk School]], the [[National Committee for a Sane Nuclear Policy]], and the [[NAACP]].[^1]
