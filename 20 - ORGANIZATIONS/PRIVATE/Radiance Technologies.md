Radiance Technologies is a 100 percent employee-owned prime contractor headquartered in [[Huntsville]], [[Alabama]], that describes its work as cybersecurity, systems engineering, prototyping and integration, and operational and strategic intelligence, including scientific and technical intelligence.[^1] Its Alabama corporate record shows incorporation in Madison County on June 1, 1998 under the name CDES, Inc., with a legal name change to Radiance Technologies, Inc. on February 2, 1999; company releases give 1999 as the founding year.[^2] A release dated September 9, 2026 gives more than 1,200 employee-owners.[^3]

### Corporate record

The [[Alabama Secretary of State]] entity record (000-195-724, status "Exists," nature of business "ANY LAWFUL ACTIVITY") lists five directors (John A. Dennis, William C. Bailey, A. Brown Ballard, James E. Deason and Gary L. Rigney) and one incorporator (John Dennis), entered May 19, 2014 with restated articles. Authorized capital is $10,000 and paid-in capital $1,000. The recorded mergers into the corporation are Missile Systems & Technologies, Inc. (November 3, 2004), Open Source Publishing Incorporated, a Virginia corporation (July 20, 2005), Symbiont Enterprises, Inc. (June 6, 2007) and Ares Corporation, a Virginia corporation (May 2, 2008). The registered agent was changed on November 25, 2025 from Paige R. Nix to Seth P. Crochet at 310 Bob Heath Drive, Huntsville. A company release of July 31, 2025 is titled as the promotion of Crochet to executive vice president and general counsel.[^2][^4]

The website of the joint venture [[Radiance Ignite Technologies]] describes William C. Bailey, Jr. as having joined Radiance in 2001, with fourteen years of military service that included work as a research and development engineer at the [[Foreign Technology Division|National Air and Space Intelligence Center]] (NASIC) and at [[Phillips Laboratory]], now the [[Air Force Research Laboratory]].[^5] Company releases record the acquisition of Phased n Research (March 31, 2025), the acquisition of [[Verus Research]] (June 2, 2025), a campus expansion at Jan Davis Drive in [[Cummings Research Park]] (ribbon cutting March 18, 2025), the appointment of the former [[SAIC]] chief executive Tony Moraco to the board of directors (August 12, 2025), and a partnership with Bright Path Research, a service-disabled veteran-owned joint venture with Offset Strategic Services (February 2025).[^6]

### National Air and Space Intelligence Center work

A Radiance release dated October 6, 2014 states that Radiance, "along with its Dayton partners," was awarded a $960,000,000 indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity contract for the Advanced Technical Exploitation Program II (ATEP II), covering research, development, system sustainment and intelligence production using geospatial-intelligence and non-nuclear measurement and signature intelligence data at NASIC at [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]]. The release quotes Radiance president Bill Bailey.[^9]

A September 23, 2019 report in the [[Huntsville Business Journal]] states that Radiance was awarded a General Services Administration blanket purchase agreement for scientific and technical intelligence support to NASIC, with a five-year base period, five one-year options and a $997 million ceiling, most work to be performed at Wright-Patterson in [[Beavercreek, Ohio]]. The team named is [[Battelle Memorial Institute]], [[COLSA Corp.]], [[Northrop Grumman]], [[Parsons Government Services]] and [[Teledyne Brown Engineering]]. [[Bryan Johnson]], then Radiance chief operating officer, is quoted: "Our win is the culmination of years of hard work that started with a single task supporting NASIC as a subcontractor in 2001."[^10] NASIC is the successor organization to the Foreign Technology Division, whose history at Wright-Patterson includes [[Project Blue Book]].

### Missile and Space Intelligence Center and the COMET contract

On September 9, 2026 Radiance announced that it had been selected as an awardee on the Contract Operations for Missile Evaluation and Testing (COMET) multi-award task order contract supporting the [[Missile and Space Intelligence Center]] (MSIC), part of the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]]. The release gives the contract value as $14 billion and lists five task areas: foundational and technical intelligence analysis, foreign materiel exploitation, information technology operations, modeling and simulation, and business processes, with a period of performance from July 21, 2026 to January 1, 2037. It states that Radiance leads its own team and holds positions on two other winning teams, one led by SAIC and one through Radiance Ignite Technologies. The Huntsville outlet [[256 Today]], reporting on September 14, 2026 that MSIC is at [[Redstone Arsenal]], gave the ceiling as "more than $14 billion."[^3][^11]

The solicitation text (HHM40225R0025) and the questions-and-answers matrix are quoted on the page for the Missile and Space Intelligence Center. One answer in the matrix states that there is "no predetermined cap on the total number of awards."[^12]

Radiance's release of June 2, 2023 states that [[Peraton]] had been awarded the "SCAR" (Countering Advanced Conventional Weapons Analytical Requirements) task order under the Defense Intelligence Agency's Solutions for Intelligence Analysis 3 program, valued at $284 million with a one-year base and four one-year options, with Radiance on the team; the release describes "analysis of foreign weapon systems across all MSIC's missions and disciplines, including air and missile defense systems, ballistic missiles, anti-tank guided missiles, and directed energy weapons." Radiance executive vice president Karl Walli is quoted: "While Radiance has supported MSIC for over 15 years, we are excited to have this opportunity to expand our support to MSIC's critical mission."[^13]

The Government Accountability Office docket B-424401.1 records a protest by Radiance Ignite Technologies, Inc. against solicitation HHM40225R0025 (agency Defense Intelligence Agency), filed April 9, 2026 and dismissed April 22, 2026; proposals were due April 27, 2026.[^14] Science Applications International Corporation (SAIC) stated in a Form 8-K exhibit dated August 31, 2026 that it "was awarded a position on the estimated $14 billion" contract.[^15]

### Hires from the UAP Task Force

Radiance announced on April 22, 2022 (a Friday) that [[Travis Taylor]], described in the announcement as a scientist, author, engineer and television star, had joined as a principal research scientist. Chief executive Bill Bailey was quoted: "Dr. Taylor is an outstanding and highly qualified scientist whose inventive way of thinking will be a great asset for Radiance."[^18]

A Radiance release dated May 12, 2022, "John F. Stratton Jr. Joins Radiance Technologies as SME for Intel Strategy," announced the hiring of [[Jay Stratton]] (John F. Stratton Jr.), "recently retired" from his role as Defense Intelligence Senior Executive with the [[Office of Naval Intelligence]], as a member of the chief executive's strategy team for scientific and technical intelligence work. It describes him as "the first Director of the Unidentified Aerial Phenomenon Task Force (UAPTF)," and quotes Radiance president [[Tim Tinsley]]: "Jay's extensive experience in the Intelligence Community will be valuable in identifying and cultivating new strategic teaming opportunities within both industry and government."[^19] [[George Knapp]] of [[KLAS-TV]] wrote on May 24, 2022 that "In January 2021 ... Stratton was surprisingly transferred out of the job" at the [[UAP Task Force]], and that Taylor said of Stratton, "he and I have been colleagues for a long time." Knapp wrote that Stratton "has never spoken publicly about his work and is not commenting now."[^20]

### Statements by company officials

In an 8 News Now report by Knapp posted October 13, 2022 (Pacific time), Tinsley was asked whether the company would take a contract to take apart a UFO and learn how it works. He answered: "Why not. I'm waiting for one of those." Of work at [[Nellis Air Force Base]] he said, "I can't tell you a lot. I can tell is you is we've supported the [[Las Vegas]] community, Nellis Air Force Base, for over 20 years." Asked whether the company worried about the perception that it reverse engineers UFOs, he said, "No, we provide customer solutions. Whatever our customers want us to work on and support, then that's what we'll do." Knapp wrote that Radiance "isn't able to confirm the allegations" about crash retrievals, that Stratton urged Radiance to hire Taylor, and that when Radiance announced the two hires "the news releases didn't hide the connection." Taylor told Knapp: "Jay and I are working on growing the capabilities, doing reverse engineering of foreign materials. If we were to get something to reverse engineer, we would absolutely want to do that."[^21]

### Allegation by Daniel Sheehan

On December 3, 2023 [[Liberation Times]] published an interview with the attorney [[Daniel Sheehan]], whom it identified as president of the [[New Paradigm Institute]], about opposition to the [[Unidentified Anomalous Phenomena Disclosure Act]] in the fiscal 2024 National Defense Authorization Act. Sheehan said that Representatives [[Mike Rogers]] and [[Mike Turner]] had faced pressure from private defense contractors and the intelligence community, and, asked to name the entities, named [[Lockheed Martin]], [[Raytheon]], Radiance Technologies and the [[Central Intelligence Agency]] Directorate of Operations. An Internet Archive copy of the article from December 2023 adds, after "Directorate of Operations," the words "run by David Marlowe, its current Deputy Director"; the present version of the article does not carry them. Sheehan said that the CIA disperses non-human materials to aerospace companies and that Radiance is one of its newest customers: "Lockheed Martin, Raytheon and, now Radiance Technologies, are given particular discrete aspects of the technology to try to figure it out."[^22]

The same article states that Liberation Times "has been unable to further substantiate the involvement of these organisations", that "there is no evidence to suggest that Radiance Technologies, Raytheon, Lockheed Martin or the CIA are influencing Representatives Turner or Rogers", and that "multiple sources have told Liberation Times that the allegations made regarding Radiance Technologies are untrue and that two of its employees, Jay Stratton and Travis Taylor are long-time advocates for more transparency on this topic and are in no way involved in any coverup." It reports that Radiance maintains an office in Beavercreek, Ohio, in Turner's district, and cites a 2016 ribbon cutting there by Turner and a 2020 opening of a 104,000 square foot Huntsville facility at which Rogers was a guest of honour.[^22]

### Founding and early customers

The Alabama record shows incorporation as CDES, Inc. in 1998, while the company's own pages give March 15, 1999 as the date operations began; the 2017 About Us page names [[George Clark]], [[John Dennis]] and [[Scott Dublin]] as the founders, who "launched operations in a 3,000 square foot facility in Executive Plaza" in [[Huntsville, Alabama]] with an initial subcontract supporting the [[United States Army Space and Missile Defense Command]] through the NRC SETAC contract. The page states that the founders' goal "was to be employee-owned" and that the company ended its first calendar year with 11 employees and $1.2 million in revenue.[^25] A 2005 company release quotes Clark as president.[^26] A 2002 company page lists a Hattiesburg, Mississippi signal research facility, an Oxford, Mississippi office and an office in Fairfax, Virginia.[^27]

### Foreign materiel exploitation pages, 2001 to 2006

A Radiance web page captured on October 27, 2001 is titled "Foreign Materiel Exploitation." It states that Radiance "assists the government in understanding the operations of foreign sensor and weapon systems, to ensure that Free World military forces can exploit weaknesses of foreign systems and can avoid strengths," and lists "Reverse Engineering," "RTSS Development," "Capability/Vulnerability Analysis," "Algorithm Analysis" and "Data Link Exploitation," with Bryan Johnson as the contact.[^28] A page captured on September 27, 2002, titled "Intelligence Community Support," lists "[[Foreign Materiel Exploitation|Foreign Material Exploitation]]," "Software Reverse Engineering," "Hardware Reverse Engineering" and "[[ELINT]], COMINT, FISINT, and MASINT Analysis," with Bill Bailey as the contact.[^27]

A Dayton office page captured in January 2004 describes the office as a branch of the company's Intelligence Systems Division at 2900 Presidential Drive in Fairborn, Ohio: "Our primary customers are the intelligence and research organizations at Wright-Patterson AFB. We specialize in the analysis of foreign weapon systems and the development and implementation of emerging technologies to advance US weapon systems."[^29] A December 2005 programs page lists "Foreign Electronic Systems Capabilities (FESC)" for the [[National Air and Space Intelligence Center]] as a subcontract to Syracuse Research.[^30] A job posting captured May 16, 2006 (HR328, Senior Engineer, Dayton) describes "FME" duties: "Candidate will process, exploit, report and disseminate data collected on various threat systems."[^31]

### Open Source Publishing and Eliot Jardines

On August 19, 2005 Radiance announced the acquisition of Open Source Publishing, Incorporated of Leesburg, Virginia, founded in 1996 by [[Eliot Jardines]]; the unit became the Open Source Intelligence Division.[^26] The [[Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] announced on December 7, 2005 that Jardines had been named the first Assistant Deputy [[Director of National Intelligence]] for Open Source, and the release states that the firm "was acquired by Radiance Technologies, Inc., where he served as the director of the Radiance Open Source Intelligence Division."[^32] A Congressional Record declaration of September 21, 2009 lists a request by Representative Mike Turner under H.R. 2647 (Air Force research, development, test and evaluation) titled "Open Source Research Centers," with Radiance Technologies at 3100 Presidential Drive, Fairborn, Ohio as the requesting entity; the amount is not stated.[^33]

### Former National Air and Space Intelligence Center and Missile and Space Intelligence Center officials

A Radiance release of November 20, 2018 announced that [[Curtis Rowland]] had joined as chief intelligence strategist. It states that he entered federal service in July 1982; that he held "board leadership roles, including on the [[Joint Foreign Materiel Board]] of Directors, Foreign Denial and Deception Committee and the Scientific and Technical Intelligence Committee"; and that from 2014 he was chief scientist of the National Air and Space Intelligence Center at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base, leading "a team of 3,000."[^34] A release of March 2, 2020 promoted him to executive vice president and chief strategy officer and corporate lead executive for the Dayton office and states that he "led a team of 4,000."[^35]

Company leadership pages captured in November 2019 give Bailey's background as fourteen years of military service including work as a research and development engineer at the National Air and Space Intelligence Center and Phillips Laboratory, and describe Cindy Santy as having "led scientific and technical intelligence analysis of foreign weapon systems at the Defense Intelligence Agency's Missile and Space Intelligence Center." ### Sole-source award under the ESOP pilot program

Defense Logistics Agency Aviation at Huntsville awarded SPRRA226C0009 to Radiance on April 10, 2026 for range improvement and modernization at the [[Reagan Test Site]] (Kwajalein Atoll) for the Army Space and Missile Defense Command: $30,159,000 obligated against a ceiling of $149,683,593 through April 10, 2031, coded "NOT AVAILABLE FOR COMPETITION." The redacted justification and approval states the authority as "DFARS 206.302-5(b), Employee Stock Ownership Plan (ESOP) pilot program, NDAA Provision 874, and amended NDAA Provision 872," approved by an Office of the Under Secretary of War memorandum signed by Col. Steven R. Green Jr. on December 23, 2025. It states that Radiance has invested in "laboratory and integration spaces that meet ICD 705 requirements for classified facilities," that a sources-sought notice of January 20, 2026 drew no responses, and that two small businesses, McDonald Technical Solutions and Polaris Sensor Technologies, answered the synopsis, "Neither of these vendors are ESOP companies." The same document calls Radiance "an employee-owned small business" and states "Radiance, a small business, is the only known source with the current capability to successfully complete the range modernization."[^71]

The Defense Logistics Agency award notice for SPRRA226C0009 (SAM.gov notice posted April 14, 2026) reads "AWARD NOTICE OF CONTRACT SPRRA226C0009 TO RADIANCE TECHNOLOGIES, IAW 10 U.S.C. 2304(C)(1)FAR 6.302-5 AUTHORIZED OR REQUIRED BY STATUE," "SINGLE PURCHASE, COST PLUS FIXED FEE (CPFF) EFFORT" and "TOTAL AMOUNT OF CONTRACT: $149,683,593.02." Its justification states that the contract is a "sole source follow-on for the current SPRRA224C0020 contract with Radiance," which "was awarded as a bridge from a prior effort"; that Radiance developed its range-operations capability "much at their own expense, over a 6-year period"; and that funding is fiscal year 2025 and 2026 research, development, test and evaluation money of the USASMDC Program Office (United States Army Space and Missile Defense Command). It lists planned projects including replacement of the ALTAIR tracking radar ultra high frequency transmitter high voltage power supply and development of "a multi-band multi-static radar system," names the programs that plan missions at the Reagan Test Site (Air Force Minuteman III and LGM-35 Sentinel, Army Patriot and Terminal High Altitude Area Defense, Navy Conventional Prompt Strike, Space Force "classified Space systems testing," and "Golden Dome"), and states that the program "was approved by the Secretary of War to have a DX priority rating." It lists the existing work as "upgrades to current sensor subsystems that will restore the systems to Full Mission Capability for critical major defense acquisition programs including the Air Force LGM-35A, Army Dark Eagle, Navy Conventional Prompt Strike."[^51] The predecessor SPRRA224C0020 was announced by an award notice of July 15, 2024 reading "UNUSUAL AND COMPELLING ONE TIME BUY" with a "CEILING ON CONTRACT: $15M"; a redacted justification for unusual and compelling urgency (solicitation SPRRA2-24-R-0063) was posted August 13, 2024.[^51]

Section 874 of the [[National Defense Authorization Act]] for Fiscal Year 2022 (Pub. L. 117-81; 10 U.S.C. 3204 note), as amended by section 872 of the National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2024 (Pub. L. 118-31), authorizes the Department of Defense to award certain follow-on contracts noncompetitively to employee-owned businesses. The [[Defense Federal Acquisition Regulation Supplement]] implementation, published in the [[Federal Register]] on October 10, 2024 (Docket DARS-2024-0017, RIN 0750-AM01, effective November 25, 2024), defines a qualified business at 270.101 as "an S corporation as defined in 26 U.S.C. 1361(a)(1) for which 100 percent of the outstanding stock is held through an employee stock ownership plan as defined in 26 U.S.C. 4975(e)(7)." It limits awards to "[o]ne sole-source, follow-on contract per predecessor contract" unless waived, to qualified businesses with at least a satisfactory rating for the predecessor contract in the [[Contractor Performance Assessment Reporting System]], and to businesses that certify they will not pay more than 50 percent of the amount paid by the government to subcontractors; the authority "expires on December 27, 2029." DFARS 206.302-5(b) lists the pilot among the statutory exceptions to full and open competition. In the rule's response to public comment, the Department stated that neither section waived the requirement for a justification and approval and that the [[Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Sustainment]] "implemented section 874 via a contract policy memorandum dated November 8, 2022, and limited participation in the pilot program to nine contractors."[^72]

The justification for SPRRA226C0009 (solicitation SPRRA226R0040) states that a synopsis ran on the Governmentwide Point of Entry from January 27, 2026 through February 10, 2026, and lists among Radiance's capabilities "investment in tools and fixtures that enable highly skilled technical personnel to design, develop and test candidate technologies for range improvements," "business processes that enable identification of vendors of unique, niche technologies required to execute RTR I&M projects" and "acquisition of companies to provide comprehensive radar technical expertise for hardware and software prototyping." It states that a new vendor would need "18 months to 2 years" to replace the capability. The federal procurement record for the award, signed April 10, 2026, lists one offer received, solicitation procedure "SSS" ("ONLY ONE SOURCE"), the Defense Logistics Agency as awarding agency, extent competed code "B" ("NOT AVAILABLE FOR COMPETITION"), a performance period of April 10, 2026 to April 10, 2031, and the description "material, other direct costs (ODC), total direct labor, and travel to meet the needs of the customer, US Army Space and Missile Defense Command's (SMDC) Reagan Test Site (RTS)."[^73]
