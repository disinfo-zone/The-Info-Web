Radiance Ignite Technologies is a Huntsville, Alabama joint venture of [[Radiance Technologies]] and [[Ignite Fueling Innovation]], Inc.; its website describes it as "An SBA Approved SDVOSB Mentor Protege Joint Venture" and states that "Radiance Technologies and Ignite have joined forces."[^1] No record opened states which of the two companies is the mentor and which the protege, and the Small Business Administration agreement is not publicly listed.

### Alabama record

The [[Alabama Secretary of State]] lists Radiance Ignite Technologies, LLC as entity 001-144-448, a domestic limited liability company with status "Exists," place of formation Alabama and formation date July 10, 2024. The one scanned document is a Certificate of Formation of four pages dated July 10, 2024. The press announcement of the venture is dated October 14, 2024, three months and four days later; both dates are recorded here as they appear in their sources. The registered agent is [[Clayton Hinchman]], with a registered office in Huntsville. The principal address is "Not Provided" and the nature of business is blank; the record lists no members, managers or organizer.[^2]

The registered office is the same suite on The Bridge Street that the Alabama record gives for Ignite Fueling Innovation, Inc. (entity 000-214-606, formerly Alatec, Inc.), while Radiance Technologies, Inc. lists a registered office on Bob Heath Drive in Huntsville. Ignite's record shows its registered agent's address as a Discovery Drive address until November 9, 2022; the Alabama corporation The Sol Foundation (001-041-463), formed September 23, 2022, gave Ignite Fueling Innovation, Inc. at that Discovery Drive address as its registered agent.[^2][^3]

### Announcement

On October 14, 2024 Radiance and Ignite announced "the formation of a new joint venture, Radiance Ignite Technologies." The release, issued by Radiance through PR Newswire, lists capabilities in technical intelligence, directed energy, space operations, electromagnetic spectrum operations, artificial intelligence and machine learning, and systems engineering, and states: "From developing advanced foreign materiel exploitation techniques to creating next-generation predictive analysis tools for space operations, Radiance Ignite Technologies will offer a comprehensive suite of services designed to meet the evolving needs of modern defense and intelligence communities."[^4]

[[Bill Bailey]], Radiance chief executive, is quoted as vice chairman of the venture: "Teaming up with Ignite will bring together the best of both worlds to tackle the toughest challenges our government faces." Clayton Hinchman, Ignite chief executive, is quoted as president and chairman: "This joint venture serves as an incredible opportunity to combine our collective expertise and capabilities while leveraging Ignite's SDVOSB status."[^4] The Huntsville outlet 256 Today carried the announcement the same day.[^5]

### Leadership

The venture's About page lists Hinchman as president and chairman and Bailey as vice chairman. It states that Hinchman became chairman and chief executive of Ignite in May 2020, medically retired from the [[U.S. Army]] as a captain after wounds sustained in Iraq, and holds degrees from the [[United States Military Academy]] and [[Georgetown University]]. It states that Bailey joined Radiance in 2001 and served as a research and development engineer at the [[Foreign Technology Division|National Air and Space Intelligence Center]] and [[Phillips Laboratory]].[^1]

### Capabilities and awards

The website lists six capability areas: intelligence (missile warning and tracking, threat modeling, all-source analysis), missile defense, space (including space domain awareness), artificial intelligence and machine learning (including a "Signals Intelligence Alert & Predication System"), electromagnetic spectrum operations and directed energy.[^1]

The only award attributed to the venture in the records opened is its position as one of three teams that include Radiance on the Contract Operations for Missile Evaluation and Testing (COMET) multi-award task order contract of the [[Missile and Space Intelligence Center]], announced by Radiance on September 9, 2026, with a period of performance from July 21, 2026 to January 1, 2037 and a task area titled foreign materiel exploitation.[^6] A [[USAspending]] search of prime awards and subawards for "Radiance Ignite," "RI Tech" and "RITech" (October 3, 2026) returned no recipient of that name; the website does not state the venture's unique entity identifier.[^7]

### COMET solicitation

The Contract Operations for Missile Evaluation and Testing (COMET) requirement of the Missile and Space Intelligence Center ([[Defense Intelligence Agency]], [[Redstone Arsenal]]) was advertised on [[SAM.gov]] through the [[Virginia Contracting Activity]]. A draft solicitation was posted on May 28, 2025 (HHM402-25-R-0025), a second draft on November 24, 2025, and the request for proposals (notice HHM40226COMET, form number HHM40225R0025) on March 2, 2026; the response date of April 3, 2026 was extended to April 10, 2026. The notices describe a multiple-award indefinite-delivery, indefinite-quantity contract with a five-year base ordering period and a five-year option, full and open competition, task orders of cost-plus-fixed-fee, firm-fixed-price and time-and-materials types, a Top Secret facility clearance and TS/SCI clearances for personnel. The contracting officer named in the notices is Noryem Maldonado.[^8]

The solicitation states that "Awards will be made to all Offerors determined to be 'Qualifying Offerors'", that no comparative ranking, tradeoff or best-value determination is made at the contract award level, that no cost or price evaluation is conducted for the award, and that "twenty (20%) of the IDIQ awards are set aside for Small Business", with a 30 percent small business participation requirement. Its clauses include the limitations on subcontracting provision stating that "In a joint venture comprised of a small business protege and its mentor approved by the Small Business Administration, the small business protege shall perform at least 40 percent of the work performed by the joint venture."[^9]

### Foreign materiel exploitation

The draft performance work statement for the contract ("PWS DIA MSIC MATOC Draft v1," posted February 13, 2025) lists five task areas and defines the second as follows: "Foreign Materiel Exploitation (FME) involves the reverse engineering of foreign weapon systems to analyze and understand their capabilities and vulnerabilities. The Center's exploitation laboratories are equipped with advanced capabilities in several critical areas, including software reverse-engineering (SWRE), radiofrequency (RF), electro-optical/infrared (EO/IR) analysis, materials science, computed tomography (CT) imaging, and microelectronics." It adds that "A foundational component of FME is Explosive Ordnance Exploitation (EOE)", involving Explosive Ordnance Disposal personnel, and that key support includes "transportation, storage, lifecycle management, and program management". The center's mission is stated as scientific and technical intelligence analysis of "foreign weapon systems".[^10] The Radiance release of September 9, 2026 names the same five task areas.[^6]

### Other awardee announcements

[[Parsons]] announced a position on COMET on August 26, 2026 (trade press dated the announcement August 25 and 27), Modern Technology Solutions Inc. on September 2, 2026, Radiance Technologies on September 9, 2026, and Intuitive Research and Technology Corporation on September 17, 2026. [[SAIC]] is identified as the lead of one team in the Radiance release. The Radiance release states that the contract's period of performance runs from July 21, 2026 to January 1, 2037, and the Huntsville Business Journal of September 10, 2026 reported the Radiance award separately from the contract announcements of the [[United States Department of Defense]] for that week.[^11]

### Earlier Radiance and Ignite contracts

USAspending lists two subawards from Radiance Technologies to Ignite Fueling Innovation, Inc. (formerly Alatec, Inc.): $906,326.71 dated November 4, 2013 under an Army [[Space and Missile Defense Command]] contract (W9113M13D0006, order 0003) for "independent programmatic and engineering support" and related studies, and $98,665 dated June 11, 2026 under Air Force order FA860426FB159 (identity, credential and access management software sustainment). Ignite is also listed as a subawardee under prime contracts held by SAIC (2023 to 2026), Parsons Government Services (2024 to 2026) and Intuitive Research and Technology Corporation (2025), three of the companies that announced COMET positions.[^12] In 2017 a profile published by the [[Business Council of Alabama]] reported that Ignite had entered a [[NASA]] Shared Services Center mentor-protege agreement with SAIC and had had an employee stock ownership plan since 2012.[^13]

In June 2023 Radiance announced that it had joined [[Peraton]] on the $284 million SIA 3 Countering Advanced Conventional Weapons Analytical Requirements task order for the same center; Karl Walli of Radiance said Radiance had supported the center for over 15 years.[^14]

### Federal registration

The Alabama Secretary of State record for entity 001-144-448 was fetched again on October 3, 2026 and was unchanged: formation date July 10, 2024, one scanned document (Certificate of Formation, four pages), no members or managers listed. USAspending contains no prime award or subaward under the venture's name.[^15] The company website describes an "SBA Approved SDVOSB Mentor Protege Joint Venture"; the [[Small Business Administration]] rules for such ventures are at 13 C.F.R. 125.8 (joint ventures) and 125.9 (mentor-protege program), the latter providing that SBA must approve the mentor-protege agreement before the two firms may submit an offer as a joint venture and receive the exclusion from affiliation.[^16]
