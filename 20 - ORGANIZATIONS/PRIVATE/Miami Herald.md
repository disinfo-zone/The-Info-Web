The [[Miami Herald]] is a daily of [[Miami]] that [[Gary Webb]] described in 1998 as the flagship paper of [[Knight-Ridder]], the owner of the [[San Jose Mercury News]]. Its name appears in CIA files in connection with the Agency's Miami station and with a 1974 letter from [[William Colby]] to [[Bernard Barker]]; in the [[Federal Bureau of Investigation]]'s Wackenhut file; in the Senate record on the Contra supply network; and in the 2018 reporting on the [[Epstein non-prosecution agreement]].

### Cover arrangements at the Miami station

[[Carl Bernstein]] listed the Herald in [[Rolling Stone]] on October 20, 1977 among news organizations with which, "according to Agency officials, CIA files document additional cover arrangements," and wrote that "cover arrangements with the Herald, according to CIA officials, were unusual in that they were made 'on the ground by the CIA station in Miami, not from CIA headquarters.'" Earlier in the article he had included the Herald in a list of organizations that cooperated with the Agency, and quoted a former CIA official that the list was "just a small part."[^1] The [[Church Committee]] stated in 1976 that the Agency had about 50 relationships with American journalists or media employees and that more than a dozen American news organizations and publishing houses "formerly provided cover for CIA agents abroad," without naming them.[^2]

Webb wrote in a note to *Dark Alliance* that after the Mercury News series he learned that Knight-Ridder "has a history of collaborating with the CIA," citing the December 26, 1977 [[New York Times]] series, and that his colleague [[Georg Hodel]] "discovered a CIA cable in a Miami court file suggesting that a longtime editor on the Latin American desk of Knight-Ridder's flagship paper, The Miami Herald, had an operational relationship with the CIA during the 1960s." Webb did not name the editor or reproduce the cable.[^3]

A CIA letter of March 28, 1974, signed by Colby as Director, thanks Barker, one of the [[Watergate]] burglars, for his statement of March 16, 1974 "to the Miami Herald" and for "the favorable references you made to this Agency and to [[Richard Helms|Ambassador Helms]]," and adds: "We are distressed that the incident which called for our apology occurred, and we appreciate your understanding of the matter." The letter was originated by [[John F. Blake]], Acting Director of Security, on March 21, 1974. The incident is not described.[^4]

### Hank Messick and Wackenhut

An [[FBI]] memorandum written after the January 9, 1967 newspaper reports that Florida Governor [[Claude Kirk]] had appointed the [[Wackenhut Corporation]] as a private force for his "war on crime" records that the Bureau had "made available lead information to [[Hank Messick]], veteran crime reporter of the 'Miami Herald,' whose articles on organized crime and corruption have brought about various grand jury probes in [[Dade and Broward Counties]] resulting in indictments of the sheriffs of these counties in 1966." The memorandum instructed that the Miami office be cautioned not to become involved with the venture.[^5] Messick, a contract rackets writer for the Herald, resigned on December 13, 1966 to join Wackenhut and later resigned from it, stating: "I have reluctantly concluded a cruel hoax is being perpetrated upon the people of Florida."[^6]

### Castle Bank, 1980

On April 19, 1980 the Herald reported under the headline "CIA May Have Ended Nassau Bank Probe" that, according to sources, the CIA "may have forced federal investigators in Miami to abandon" an inquiry, code-named [[Project Haven]], into [[Castle Bank and Trust]] of Nassau, which "may have laundered CIA funds for the [[Bay of Pigs]] invasion of Cuba" and moved CIA money for other Latin American operations. A former IRS investigator was quoted: "I've had suspicions for a long time that the CIA was involved." The article identified the Miami lawyer [[Paul Helliwell]], who died in 1976 and represented the bank, as "a key link to the theory of CIA involvement."[^7] A clipping of the article is in the CIA's own reading-room files.

### The Contra supply network, 1985 to 1987

The chronology in the 1988 report of the Senate subcommittee chaired by [[John Kerry]] cites the Herald repeatedly. On July 21, 1985 the Herald quoted the mercenaries [[Steven Carr]] and [[Peter Glibbery]], who said they had flown weapons from [[Fort Lauderdale]] to the Contras; on July 28, 1985 FBI agent [[Kevin Currier]] opened a [[Neutrality Act]] investigation "on basis of reading July 21, 1985 article in Miami Herald." After the Herald wrote about the April 1986 embassy episode involving prosecutor [[Jeffrey Feldman]] in [[Costa Rica]], an embassy official named Kotula told reporters that Feldman had lied. The chronology lists Herald articles of April 30, 1986 (that [[Oliver North]]'s fundraising may have violated the [[Boland Amendment]]), May 14, 1986 (that Kerry's staff was examining charges of gunrunning and drug smuggling), and June 1, 1986, which quoted a [[Department of Justice|Justice Department]] official that the FBI and [[DEA]] had "run down each and every one" of the allegations and found "no credible or substantive evidence," and that congressional efforts "smacked of McCarthyism."[^8]

On June 8, 1986 the Herald ran a front-page story by [[Alfonso Chardy]] reporting that the [[National Security Council]] and the CIA had managed the Contras during the Boland Amendment period through a network overseen by North, citing [[Robert Owen]] and [[John Singlaub]] among those who helped him and naming [[John Hull]] as one of Owen's contacts. The [[Associated Press]] story of [[Robert Parry]] and [[Brian Barger]] making similar allegations followed on June 10.[^9]

The Kerry report also cites the Herald's articles headed "America's Cocaine Connection" of November 29 and December 2, 1987 on the [[Medellin Cartel]], including the description of the Ochoa family ranch as "so huge it encompasses several towns inside its borders."[^10]

Webb's notes to *Dark Alliance* cite Herald articles of 1984 to 1991 on Contra fundraising, the [[La Parrilla]] restaurant frequented by Contra supporters, and a Florida bank closed by state regulators in 1987, among them the Herald's 1993 obituary of [[Aristides Sanchez]], which stated that he "became one of the Contras' top political and military strategists, plotting logistics, buying supplies and delivering weapons."[^11]

### Epstein, 2018 to 2019

On November 28, 2018 Julie K. Brown published "Perversion of Justice" in the Herald on the 2007 agreement between [[Jeffrey Epstein]] and the [[U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of Florida]] under [[Alexander Acosta]]. The Justice Department Office of Professional Responsibility later summarized the series as reporting that Acosta "entered into an 'extraordinary' deal" that permitted Epstein to avoid federal prosecution, immunized co-conspirators and concealed the terms from victims, and that the report, which included interviews of eight victims and public documents, "painted a portrait of federal and state prosecutors who had ignored serious criminal conduct by a wealthy man with powerful and politically connected friends." On December 3, 2018 Senator [[Ben Sasse]] wrote to the office citing the Herald report and asking it to open an investigation; on February 6, 2019 the department told Sasse that it had.[^12]
