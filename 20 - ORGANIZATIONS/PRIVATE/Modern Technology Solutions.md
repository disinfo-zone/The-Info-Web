### Founding and leadership

[[Phil Soucy]] and [[Tom McMahan]] founded Modern Technology Solutions, Inc. in 1993; the company's 2014 website says they did so "with a single defense contract and a few computers." The company's biography of Soucy states that he graduated from [[Norwich University]] with a commission in the [[United States Air Force]], served more than 20 years as an officer, first as a weapons systems officer, then as a flight test engineer, and finally as "a program manager for the [[Assistant Secretary of the Air Force]] for Acquisition's Special Projects branch"; that he is a graduate of the [[United States Air Force Test Pilot School]]; and that he was co-president and chief executive of the company for 20 years before becoming chairman of the board. Its biography of McMahan states that he graduated from the [[Missouri University of Science and Technology]] (then University of Missouri-Rolla) and served 25 years in the Air Force, and that "When he retired in 1993 as a colonel, Tom had oversight responsibility for all Air Force special access programs and was a primary advisor to the Secretary of the Air Force and Chief of Staff regarding special technology programs with emphasis on stealth and counter stealth technology, development, and operational utilization," and that he served as co-president from 1993 until 2013. The 2014 founders page gives the shorter statement that "When he retired in 1993 as a colonel, Tom had oversight responsibility for all Air Force special access programs." The staff biographies of both founders list expertise in low observable and counter low observable technology and electronic countermeasures.[^1][^2]

[[Kevin Robinson]] was announced as president and chief executive officer on February 10, 2014, succeeding Soucy, who remained chairman. The announcement states that Robinson "joined MTSI in 1997 as an entrepreneur and director of the Acquisition and Technology group" after "multiple leadership roles at GRCI (now [[AT&T Federal]])," and that the company had "over 580 employees, based at nine offices across the [[United States]]."[^3] The headquarters address listed on federal award records is Shawnee Road, [[Alexandria, Virginia]].[^4]

### Employee Stock Ownership Plan

The Modern Technology Solutions, Inc. Employee Stock Ownership Plan and Trust (Form 5500 plan 003, sponsor employer identification number 54-1670018) is described in its audited statements as "a leveraged employee stock ownership plan ('ESOP') established effective January 1, 2008," restated January 1, 2023. The audit for plan year 2024 records two purchases of company stock by the plan: "On December 5, 2008, the Plan purchased 295,203 shares of Company common shares at a purchase price of $10,978,600 or $37.19 per share," and "During April 2013, the Plan purchased 357,336 shares at a cost of $62.00 per share, for a total purchase price of $22,148,632." Both were financed by the company sponsor and collateralized by unallocated shares; the notes state that the plan purchased stock "financed by an installment loan that was due to the Company, and installment loans due to stockholders," and that "The loan has been repaid in full." The 2024 statements' schedule of investments lists the 2013 purchase as 357,236 shares, while the audits for plan years 2017 and 2019 give it as "368,862 shares at a cost of $62.00 per share, for a total purchase price of" $22,869,444.[^5] The plan's recordkeeper is [[Principal Financial Group]]; the plan's assets are "held by the trustees of the Plan," who are not named in the financial statements.[^6]

On May 2, 2017 the company announced that it was "now officially 100% employee owned," with the headline "MTSI Fulfills ESOP Promise Ahead of Schedule." The announcement describes the change as "the completion of an employee stock ownership plan (ESOP) initiated in 2008" and quotes McMahan: "Many years ago, one of the best decisions in the company's history to date was made by opting against an opportunity to be bought out." It states that the company had "over 730 employees, based at 20 offices and field sites worldwide."[^7]

The audited statements give the per-share value of company stock at $78.50 (December 31, 2016), $100.25 (2017), $120.00 (2018), $171.75 (2019), $453.75 (2023) and $569.25 (2024). The fair value of the plan's holding of company common stock was $296,044,196 at December 31, 2023 and $371,400,901 at December 31, 2024, and the stated maximum put-option commitment was $293,544,196 and $366,400,901 for those dates. The company advanced the plan $2,500,000 in 2023 (repaid in 2024) and $5,000,000 in 2024 "to cover the Plan and Trust's repurchase obligations."[^6][^5] Form 5500 line 6a(2) active participants at the end of the plan year were 710 (2017), 837 (2018), 1,020 (2019), 1,178 (2020), 1,263 (2021), 1,433 (2022), 1,978 (2023) and 2,125 (2024).[^5]

### National Air and Space Intelligence Center work

The [[National Air and Space Intelligence Center]] (NASIC) selected five companies for NOVASTAR. MTSI's announcement of August 19, 2022, citing Defense.gov, describes it as "a 11-year indefinite-delivery/indefinite-quantity (IDIQ), small business set-aside contract providing Scientific and Technical Intelligence (S&TI) support services to NASIC," and states that "MTSI has supported [[Wright-Patterson Air Force Base]] (WPAFB) in Beavercreek, Ohio since 2006," that "Over the past 16+ years, MTSI has had the privilege of supporting NASIC in the delivery of differentiated all-source intelligence analysis, threat Modeling and Simulation (M&S), and Foreign Materiel Exploitation (FME)," and that for NOVASTAR it partnered with [[Ball Aerospace]], [[Leidos]] "and their wholly owned subsidiary" [[Dynetics]], [[Decibel Research]], [[Mile Two]], [[Awetomaton]] and [[AIS]]. The announcement quotes Robinson, [[Andrew Norton]] as NOVASTAR program manager and [[Austin Smith]] as senior director of the National Security Division.[^8] The federal procurement record for MTSI's NOVASTAR indefinite-delivery contract is FA860422DB007 ("NOVASTAR, NASIC S AND TI ANALYTIC CAPABILITY SUPPORT CONTRACT"), signed August 19, 2022, with a ceiling of $4,797,000,000 and performance through August 18, 2027; the parallel contracts FA860422DB005, FA860422DB006, FA860422DB008 and FA860422DB009 are held by [[Altamira Technologies]], [[Epsilon C5I]], [[Radiance Technologies]] and [[Xandar]].[^9] On February 16, 2023 MTSI posted that "NASIC awarded its first two Task Orders under the S&TI Analytic Capability Support (NOVASTAR) IDIQ Contract to MTSI and its teammates."[^10]

USAspending lists nine MTSI orders under the NOVASTAR and NASIC science and technical analytic capability descriptions, all awarded by [[Air Force Life Cycle Management Center]] contracting activity FA8604 (total obligated amounts as listed): FA860422FB357 ($5,000; September 2 to October 31, 2022); FA860423FB048 ($1,899,683.83; February 2, 2023 to February 1, 2024); FA860423FB055 ($4,657,246.68; March 1, 2023 to March 4, 2024); FA860423FB162 ($1,630,402.52; May 1 to December 31, 2023); FA860423FB335 ($7,251,000, with a potential value of $8,994,514; September 8, 2023 to September 7, 2027; funding office "NASIC GS"); FA860423FB383 ($1,046,016; September 22, 2023 to September 21, 2026); FA860423FB463 ($2,999,249; September 29, 2023 to September 28, 2024); FA860423FB472 ($2,796,558; September 29, 2023 to August 28, 2025); and FA860424FB099 ($5,318,807; March 5, 2024 to March 4, 2026). The descriptions of FA860423FB383 and FA860423FB463 read "NATIONAL AIR AND SPACE INTELLIGENCE CENTER SCIENCE AND TECHNICAL ANALYTIC CAPABILITY SUPPORT."[^9]

### Missile and Space Intelligence Center

MTSI announced on September 2, 2026 a position on the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]]'s Contract Operations for Missile Evaluation and Testing (COMET) vehicle. [[GovConWire]] reported that MTSI "said Wednesday it will support MSIC in delivering foundational military intelligence and scientific and technical intelligence on foreign weapon systems for DIA, the [[Department of War]] and national-level intelligence organizations." Robinson is quoted: "This work strengthens our nation's ability to understand and counter foreign weapon threats."[^11] The [[Missile and Space Intelligence Center]] vehicle and its other awardees are described on the Radiance Technologies page.

### Sole-source order under the ESOP pilot

General Services Administration order 47QFSA26F0035 to MTSI was signed September 8, 2026 with a period of performance beginning September 15, 2026, under OASIS+ indefinite-delivery contract 47QRCA25DU655 (awarded to MTSI on April 15, 2025). Its description reads: "MODERNIZATION & ENGINEERING SUPPORT (MES III) RESEARCH & DEVELOPMENT EFFORT FOR AIR NATIONAL GUARD RESERVE COMMAND TEST CENTER  STATUTORY AUTHORITY: EMPLOYEE STOCK OWNERSHIP PLAN PILOT PROGRAM AUTHORIZED BY SEC 874 OF FY22 NDAA & SEC 872 OF FY24 NDAA." Obligated funds are $14,619,921.14 against a potential value of $152,786,536.43 through March 14, 2032; the contract type is time and materials, one offer was received, and the record codes the competition as "FULL AND OPEN COMPETITION" and "SUBJECT TO MULTIPLE AWARD FAIR OPPORTUNITY." The funding office is listed as "162 FW AATC CORMIER."[^4]

The same funding office appears on the two earlier orders for modernization and engineering support: Army order W9133L16F0027 ("MODERNIZATION AND ENGINEERING SUPPORT," signed July 25, 2016, awarded by the [[Army National Guard Readiness Center]], $41,288,734.96, to July 26, 2021) and GSA order 47QFSA21F0098 ("MODERNIZATION AND ENGINEERING SUPPORT II - MES II," signed July 23, 2021, $74,766,753.59 obligated against a potential $97,651,727.90, performance to January 26, 2027, under OASIS contract GS00Q14OADS721).[^4] On October 2, 2026 GovConWire reported that MTSI "said Wednesday it received the order from the General Services Administration's Assisted Acquisition Services," that the order is for "testing and integration on Guard and Reserve aircraft and mission systems," with supported areas including "Joint Terminal Attack Controller and special warfare capabilities; command, control, communications, computers, intelligence, surveillance and reconnaissance systems; domestic operations; cybersecurity; logistics software; and test process training," and quoted [[Vance Drenkhahn]], executive vice president of MTSI's defense services division, on the company's work with "the [[Air National Guard]] [[Air Force Reserve Command]] Test Center, or AATC."[^14] The authority is described on [[DoD ESOP Pilot Program]]; the Roundtable podcast of October 21, 2023 gives Matt Pearce's account of the Roundtable's request that the pilot reach Defense Department contracts placed through General Services Administration vehicles.[^15]

[[Venn Strategies]], the lobbying registrant for the [[Employee-Owned Contractors Roundtable]], listed Modern Technology Solutions among the "affiliated organizations" on its Roundtable reports for the first, second and fourth quarters of 2019 (amended reports filed December 4, 2020), the first and third quarters of 2020 (amended, December 2, 2020), the fourth quarter of 2020, the first through third quarters of 2021, the second quarter of 2022 and the first quarter of 2023. The Roundtable's reports for 2021 give as their issue "Promoting ESOP related small business procurement policies as part of the FY2022 National Defense Authorization Act to provide incentives for 100% employee-owned businesses in federal contracting."[^16]
