Americans for Responsible Innovation (ARI) is a [[Washington, D.C.]] nonprofit that lobbies on the regulation of artificial intelligence. It states that it "was founded in late 2023 by former Congressman [[Brad Carson]] (President) and tech entrepreneur [[Eric Gastfriend]] (Executive Director)," and that its mission is to "help U.S. policymakers develop a thoughtful governance framework for rapidly advancing technologies that protects the public from harm while continuing to foster innovation."[^1] Its public launch was announced on March 6, 2024, in a letter signed by the two men as co-founders, describing themselves as "a former member of Congress and health tech startup founder, respectively," and stating that ARI was "funded by individuals who share our concern for responsible innovation, rather than by industry."[^2] Its offices are at 750 17th Street NW, Suite 1200, in Washington. A sister 501(c)(3), the [[Center for Responsible Innovation]], operates alongside it.[^1]

### Funding

ARI describes itself as "a 501c4 nonprofit organization" and lists as its supporters "organizations including [[Inclusive Abundance Action]], [[Omidyar Network]], and Coefficient Giving and ... individual donors including Brad Carson & Julie Carson, Eric Gastfriend & Caroline Mehl, and Steve Newman." It states that it does "not take money from for-profit corporations or non-U.S. entities" and that it is "run fully independently of our donors." It adds that it is "recommended for giving by [[Founders Pledge]]."[^1] Coefficient Giving is the name adopted in November 2025 by [[Open Philanthropy]], the grantmaker funded by [[Dustin Moskovitz]] and [[Cari Tuna]].

The organization is registered with the [[Internal Revenue Service]] as Americans For Responsible Innovation Ltd, EIN 93-3248564, under section 501(c)(4). For 2023 it reported revenue of 501,673 dollars, 500,100 dollars of it contributions, expenses of 23,064 dollars and no executive pay. For 2024 it reported revenue of 5,244,469 dollars, almost all of it contributions, expenses of 2,592,128 dollars and net assets of 3,130,427 dollars; executive compensation came to 1,095,356 dollars, 42.3 percent of expenses. The 2024 return listed [[Douglass Calidas]], senior vice president of government affairs, at 438,631 dollars; [[Satya Thallam]], senior vice president of government affairs from May 2024, at 265,156 dollars; Gastfriend, executive director, at 253,159 dollars; [[Adam Pastrich]], chief of staff from June 2024, at 102,711 dollars; Carson, president, at no salary; and [[Thomas Adelson]], board member and treasurer from December 2024.[^3]

### Staff and Advisers

Carson is described on ARI's staff page as a former president of the [[University of Tulsa]], former congressman, [[Under Secretary of the Army]] and acting [[Under Secretary of Defense]]; Gastfriend as the former chief executive and president of DynamiCare Health, "a digital health startup with awards from NIH & FDA." Calidas, listed as Doug Calidas, is a former chief of staff to Senator [[Amy Klobuchar]] and former chief counsel to Senator [[Joe Manchin]]. The chief of staff, [[Blair Mallin]], was director of operations to [[Samantha Power]] at [[USAID]] and to Klobuchar. Other staff came from the [[Bureau of Industry and Security]] (policy director [[Rachel Hovde]]), the [[Department of the Treasury]] and [[Tanium]] (Pastrich), the offices of Senator [[John Barrasso]] and the [[Department of Transportation]] ([[Mitch Kominsky]]), the Navy staff at [[The Pentagon]] ([[Jared Wilhelm]]), the [[National Security Innovation Network]] of the [[Department of Defense]] ([[Michael Dila]] and [[Morgan C. Plummer]]), the [[Air Force Research Laboratory]] ([[George Dougherty]]) and the [[House Select Committee on the CCP]]. [[McKenna Fitzgerald]], in external relations, is a former research manager at the [[ML Alignment & Theory Scholars|MATS]] AI-safety training program and former deputy director of the [[Global Catastrophic Risk Institute]].[^4]

Its board of advisors, which "have no official authority in our decision-making process," comprises [[Scott Aaronson]] of the [[University of Texas at Austin]]; [[Erik Brynjolfsson]] of the Stanford Digital Economy Lab; [[Gillian Hadfield]] of [[Johns Hopkins University]], a "former Senior Policy Advisor, Open AI"; [[Samuel Hammond]] of the [[Foundation for American Innovation]]; [[Stuart Russell]], director of the [[Center for Human-Compatible AI]] at the [[University of California, Berkeley]]; and the former congressman [[Tim Ryan]].[^4]

### Lobbying

ARI registered as its own lobbyist in the first quarter of 2024, reporting 50,000 dollars in lobbying expenses, with Calidas as its lobbyist; in the second quarter it reported 220,000 dollars, listing Calidas and Thallam and naming the House, the Senate and the [[National Institute of Standards and Technology]] among the bodies lobbied. Its quarterly expenses were 230,000 and 200,000 dollars in the second half of 2024; 260,000, 330,000, 450,000 and 470,000 dollars in the four quarters of 2025, or 1.51 million dollars for the year; and 480,000 and 620,000 dollars in the first two quarters of 2026. Under both issue codes it used, computer industry and science and technology, the filings give the same description: "Issues related to the responsible governance of artificial intelligence and other emerging technologies."[^5]

Four outside firms have been registered to lobby for ARI. [[Kountoupes Denham Carr & Reid]] has filed for it continuously since the first quarter of 2024, its quarterly fee rising from 20,000 to 90,000 dollars; the lobbyists on its first registration were [[Lisa Kountoupes]], a former deputy assistant to the president and House liaison and former assistant director for legislative affairs at the [[Office of Management and Budget]]; [[Clayton Patrick Hayes]], a former chief of staff to Manchin and deputy legislative director to Senator [[Evan Bayh]]; [[Adam Hechavarria]], a former legislative director to Senators [[Todd Young]] and [[Kelly Ayotte]]; [[MJ Kenny]], a former deputy floor director for the Senate Democratic whip and aide to Senator [[Richard Durbin]]; and [[David Paul Peluso]], a former chief of staff to Representatives [[Cathy McMorris Rodgers]] and [[Gus Bilirakis]]. [[Navigators Global]], formerly DC Navigators, filed from the first to the third quarter of 2024 at 40,000 to 50,000 dollars a quarter, with the lobbyists [[Andy Keiser]], [[Ryan Berger]] and [[Philmore B. Anderson]]. [[Mindset Advocacy]] registered in the second quarter of 2025 at 80,000 dollars a quarter, and [[Neil Hill Global Strategies]] in the fourth quarter of 2025 at 20,000 to 40,000 dollars.[^5]

Kountoupes Denham Carr & Reid and Navigators Global were also registered as outside lobbying firms for [[Anthropic]], the first reporting 10,000 dollars from Anthropic in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the second 30,000 to 60,000 dollars a quarter in 2026.[^6]

### ARI PAC

Americans for Responsible Innovation Ltd. Political Action Committee (ARI PAC), [[Federal Election Commission]] identifier C00893883, registered on December 11, 2024, as a lobbyist-registrant political action committee of a membership organization, at ARI's address and with Gastfriend as treasurer. Through its report of July 15, 2026, it had received 390,475 dollars in contributions, 99.2 percent of them from individuals, had given 329,000 dollars to federal candidates and held 49,150.51 dollars in cash.[^7]

Its itemized receipts consist almost entirely of 5,000-dollar contributions, the annual limit on an individual's contribution to a political action committee, many of them repeated in 2025 and 2026. The employers entered on the filings include Anthropic, for [[Todor Markov]], [[Peter Lofgren]], [[Evan Hubinger]], [[Daniel Ziegler]], [[Jan Leike]], [[Scott Johnston]], [[Steven Bills]], [[Andrew Charles Callahan]], [[Samuel R. Bowman]], [[Jascha Sohl-Dickstein]] and [[Adam Jermyn|Adam S. Jermyn]]; Open Philanthropy, for its program director [[Claire Zabel]] and for [[Howard Lempel]], each giving twice; [[METR]], for [[Benjamin West]], [[Sydney Von Arx]], [[Lawrence Chan]] and [[Tao Lin]]; [[Redwood Research]], for its chief executive [[Michael Shlegeris]], [[Ryan Greenblatt]], [[Vivek Hebbar]], [[Aryan Bhatt]] and [[Alex Mallen]]; the [[Alignment Research Center]], for [[Eric Neyman]]; [[OpenAI]], for [[Julie Steele]] and [[Dillon Bowen]]; and [[Google DeepMind]], for [[Kate Woolverton]]. Gastfriend himself gave 5,000 dollars on February 21, 2025, and again on May 9, 2026, listing his employer as ARI.[^8] Several of the same donors (Lofgren, Leike, Jermyn, Bowen and Woolverton) gave again in 2026 to the super PAC Public First, affiliated with [[Public First Action]], Lofgren giving 999,900 dollars and Leike 400,000 dollars.[^9]

Among the PAC's other donors was [[Derek Kaufman]] of [[Greenwich, Connecticut]], who gave 5,000 dollars listing "Inclusive Abundance" as his employer.[^8]
