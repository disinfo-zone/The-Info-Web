Ignite Fueling Innovation, Inc. is an [[Alabama]] domestic corporation based in [[Huntsville]], formed on February 2, 2001 in Madison County and known as Alatec, Inc. until a name change recorded on May 16, 2016. The Alabama Secretary of State record (entity 000-214-606) gives its status as "Exists" and its nature of business as "PERFORM ENGINEERING/TECHNICAL SERVICES." Authorized capital is $250,000 and paid-in capital $100,000. The incorporators listed are [[John Hamburg]], [[Dan Cleary]], [[Mike Jury]] and [[Larry Anderson]].[^1]

### Office address

The Federal Election Commission registration of Ignite's first political action committee (C00610691, treasurer Brandon R. Umphrey) gives the committee's address as 530C Discovery Dr NW, Huntsville, with Ignite as the connected organization. USAspending recipient addresses on Ignite awards of 2022 and 2024 read "530C Discovery Dr," one with "Ste 450."[^2]

### Alabama registry record

The Secretary of State record shows a Certificate of Formation (7 pages), Articles of Amendment dated May 23, 2016 (3 pages) recording that the legal name was "Changed From Alatec, Inc." on May 16, 2016, and further Articles of Amendment dated September 4, 2019 (3 pages) and March 26, 2024 (1 page). The page prints incorporators only and lists no officers; annual reports for 2000 through 2024 are listed.[^1]

The registered-agent history on the same record reads as follows. On August 12, 2010 the agent was changed from Larry D. Anderson. On August 5, 2016 an agent (Anderson, at an address on Pratt Avenue) was replaced. On January 4, 2019 the agent was changed from [[Jason Shelton]], at an address on Jan Davis Drive NW. On November 9, 2022 a Registered Agent Change (2 pages) records the agent as changed from Shelton at a Discovery Drive NW address in Huntsville, with the agent's mailing address changed from the same address. The current agent is [[[[Clayton Hinchman]]|Robert Clayton Hinchman]], with a registered office on The Bridge Street in Huntsville.[^1]

### Huntsville conference, June 2022

The Scientific Coalition for UAP Studies held its Anomalous Aerospace Phenomena Conference at the Rocket City Tavern in Huntsville on June 3 to 5, 2022. [[Garry Nolan]] was a panelist on "The National Security Implications of Scientifically Studying UAP," moderated by Joseph DiNoto (described in the program as a former intelligence community officer supporting "special operations, space, and missile defense forces") with [[Rich Hoffman]] (an Army Materiel Command Headquarters contractor at [[Redstone Arsenal]] and executive board member of the coalition) and two other panelists.[^3]

### The Sol Foundation filings in Alabama

The Alabama Secretary of State holds a name reservation (RES036922) for "The Sol Foundation," issued for the exclusive use of Garry Nolan for one year beginning July 28, 2022. A Certificate of Formation for a domestic nonprofit corporation of that name (entity 001-041-463) was filed September 23, 2022. It gives a Discovery Drive NW address in Huntsville as both principal office and registered office, and names Ignite Fueling Innovation, Inc. as registered agent; the Sol filing names no individual as agent. Ignite's own record shows its individual agent, Jason Shelton, at a Discovery Drive NW address in Huntsville until the change recorded November 9, 2022, which is 47 days after the Sol formation. The stated purpose of the Sol corporation is "The Sol Foundation supports research into and the understanding of Unidentified Aerial Phenomena."[^4][^5]

The Alabama corporation filed a Statement of Intent to Dissolve and Articles of Dissolution on September 15, 2023, a year and three weeks after its formation. The Articles of Dissolution have the box "No plan of distribution was adopted" checked, name no recipient of assets and do not state whether the corporation held any. See [[Sol Foundation]] for the directors and officers named in the filings.[^5]

The Alabama name search run on October 3, 2026 for the address text returned no entity by that name, and the search form does not search by address. No other entity at the Discovery Drive address was identified.[^1]

### Leadership

A [[Business Alabama]] report of July 20, 2017 describes the company as led by president and chief executive officer Jason Shelton, chief operating officer [[Brandon Umphrey]], chief marketing officer Clayton Hinchman and chief financial officer [[Trent Poff]]. The same report says that the company was "known as ALATEC until last year's purchase of and merger with [[USi]]," that it had about 250 employees serving federal and commercial customers in 17 states, that it was founded by "a group of retired military officers," that in 2006 it won its first two prime contracts, for modeling and simulation analytics at [[White Sands Missile Range]], New Mexico and at [[Fort Leavenworth]], Kansas, and that it established an employee stock ownership plan in 2012.[^6]

By October 2024 the announcement of the [[Radiance Ignite Technologies]] joint venture identified Hinchman as chief executive officer of Ignite.[^7] The Alabama record lists Hinchman as the registered agent in 2026.[^1]

Shelton joined Alatec in 2009 with responsibility for business development, after working at [[Camber Corporation]], and served 12 years in the [[U.S. Army]] and Army National Guard as an Air Defense Artillery officer working "principally in Joint and Coalition integration of missiles and radar systems around the globe."[^8] On May 22, 2020 Ignite announced that Hinchman would succeed him as chief executive officer, with Shelton remaining chairman of the board and Rich McAdams as president.[^9] The Federal Election Commission lists Shelton as "Chairman" of Ignite on contributions to the company's committee in 2016 and 2018.[^10]

Hinchman graduated from the [[United States Military Academy]], served as an infantry officer with the [[10th Mountain Division]] and Special Operations Task Force 17, and retired medically as a captain after wounds from an improvised explosive device during a night raid in Iraq. His decorations include the Combat Infantryman Badge, the Bronze Star, the Purple Heart and the Meritorious Service Medal; he was an honor graduate of Ranger School. He worked for the [[Joint Improvised-Threat Defeat Organization]] and Washington nonprofits, served on the board of the [[Elizabeth Dole Foundation]], holds a master's degree from [[Georgetown University]], founded and led the company USi until Ignite acquired it on April 1, 2016, served as Ignite's chief marketing officer for three years, then as president and chief operating officer of Systems, Products, Solutions, Inc. before becoming Ignite's chief executive officer in May 2020.[^9][^11] Ignite's Our Leadership page states that the company has "a minority-owned Employee-Owned Stock Owner Plan (ESOP)."[^12]

Hinchman was a Republican candidate for Alabama's 5th congressional district in the 2018 cycle (Federal Election Commission candidate H8AL05208, statement of candidacy first filed June 20, 2017). His committee, Hinchman for Congress (C00648162), reported receipts of $253,800.31 and loans of $100,000 for the cycle; Ignite's committee contributed $2,700 to it on May 18, 2017, and the Radiance Technologies, Inc. Political Action Committee contributed $5,000 on March 21, 2018.[^10] In the Republican primary of June 5, 2018 he received 34,739 votes (38.7 percent) to 54,928 (61.3 percent) for [[Mo Brooks]], of 89,667 cast in the district.[^13]

Trent Poff has been chief financial officer since 2016 and is listed as vice chairman; he joined in 2014 and was previously at Camber. Jessica Wilkerson is chief operations officer (vice president from June 2023), with 17 years as an Army civilian at Program Executive Office Missiles and Space, Program Executive Office Aviation and the Aviation and Missile Command; her team supports the TRADOC Analysis Center at White Sands Missile Range, Program Executive Office Missiles and Space and the Space and Missile Defense Command. Stephen Mitchell has been chief information officer since January 2024 and works on Army Materiel Command information technology contracts.[^12]

[[Julie Praino]] is Senior Vice President, a position Ignite's release of August 31, 2026 says she took in August 2026 to lead business development, capture and proposal development. The release says she supported air and missile defense programs including the [[Upgraded Early Warning Radar]], [[Terminal High Altitude Area Defense]], the [[AN/TPY-2]] radar and [[PATRIOT]] through industry partners including [[Raytheon]], held profit and loss responsibility for a portfolio above $600 million, and holds degrees from [[Bates College]] (physics), [[Boston University]] (electrical engineering) and [[Boston College]] (MBA).[^12] She is one of five named inventors on U.S. patent application 2020/0407525 A1, "Porous Polymer Membranes Comprising Vertically Aligned Carbon Nanotubes," published December 31, 2020 from an application filed November 27, 2018 under a provisional of November 27, 2017. The other named inventors include Jerry Shan and Richard Castellano of [[Rutgers University]] and Francesco Fornasiero of [[Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory]]. The applicants are Rutgers and Lawrence Livermore National Security, LLC. Its government interest statement reads: "This invention was made with government support under grant number BA12PHM123 awarded by The Defense Threat Reduction Agency." Assignment records show two of the inventors assigning their rights to Chasm Technologies Inc. (later [[Chasm Advanced Materials]]) on May 28 and 29, 2020, and the [[United States Department of Energy]] holding a confirmatory license from Lawrence Livermore National Security recorded July 27, 2020. The specification says the nanotubes were functionalized by Chasm Technologies before use.[^14] The same grant number appears in a 2017 American Physical Society abstract by Castellano, Shan, Eric Meshot and Fornasiero.[^15] Lawrence Livermore's Biosecurity Center describes the [[Defense Threat Reduction Agency]]-funded effort as a breathable membrane for protective clothing against chemical and biological agents.[^16]

[[Logan Grumbach]] joined Ignite as a strategic threat capabilities analyst in 2023 and was promoted to Vice President in May 2024, according to the company, leading its [[United States Space Force]] and intelligence portfolios, including support to the Space Force "Space Security and Defense Program," support to the [[National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency]] full-motion GEOINT mission, and the partnership with [[Virgin Galactic]] "to establish Spaceflight as a Service for government missions." The company says he spent 15 years in missile defense at [[Lockheed Martin]] and [[Boeing]] on programs including Next Generation Interceptor, Glide Phase Interceptor, Multi-Object Kill Vehicle, Redesigned Kill Vehicle, Terminal High Altitude Area Defense and Long Range Discrimination Radar, holds a Ph.D. in aerospace systems engineering and is a part-time instructor at the [[University of Alabama in Huntsville]].[^12]

His curriculum vitae at the University of Alabama in Huntsville lists him from May 2023 to May 2024 as lead scientist/engineer and futurist on "a direct congressional tasking through the United States Space Force (USSF) and elements of the Intelligence Community (IC)," including a 500-plus page futures study for the Space Force "to be delivered to Congress," technical whitepapers on "quantum radar, neutrino science, brain-computer interface, and metamaterials," and briefings to "Congressional Staff" and the Director of the Missile and Space Intelligence Center; the CV does not name the "multi-billion-dollar US company" with which it says he was chief technical coordinator for a partnership.[^17]

[[Lonnie Dunbar]] III joined Ignite in 2024 as a program manager and became Vice President in 2025, with a portfolio the company describes as "IT, space, electronic warfare, programmatic, and facility efforts" for customers including the Space and Missile Defense Command, the [[Missile and Space Intelligence Center]], the Air Force [[350th Spectrum Warfare Wing]] and [[United States Special Operations Command]]. According to the company he served in the Army Signal Corps for over 24 years, including with the [[White House Communications Agency]], and as a sergeant major was senior enlisted leader of the [[Space and Missile Defense Center of Excellence]]. The Army published his remarks in that role in 2024.[^12][^18]

### Subcontract and teaming record

USAspending subaward records show Ignite as a subcontractor to [[Radiance Technologies]] on an Army order issued by the Army Contracting Command at Redstone Arsenal: prime order 0003 under indefinite-delivery contract W9113M13D0006, base period labor, $5,197,210, October 31, 2013 to March 31, 2016, performed in Huntsville, with a subaward of $906,326.71 reported November 4, 2013 described as "provide independent programmatic and engineering support, create/develop program documentation, conduct scientific and engineering studies... and analyze and assess work performed by other contractors." A second Radiance subaward to Ignite, $98,665, was reported on June 11, 2026 under Air Force order FA860426FB159, "ICAM Collection Development and Support" (Air Force Life Cycle Management Center, Dayton, $12,955,000 total, June 12, 2026 to March 11, 2029).[^22]

Ignite also appears as a subawardee to Torch Technologies on Army Space and Missile Defense Command contract W9113M18FD009 (Future Warfare Center, Decision Support Directorate) from 2018 to 2023, to [[Science Applications International Corporation]] on NASA orders from 2019 to 2023 (the mentor-protege relationship reported in 2017), to Parsons Government Services and ITA International on General Services Administration orders, to [[Intuitive Research and Technology Corporation]] on Army order W31P4Q25F0049 (Strike Systems Engineering and Technical Assistance, July 2, 2025, $185,259), and to ManTech, IBM, General Dynamics Information Technology and Boeing on other orders. Repeated monthly reports make the totals unreliable.[^22]

### Award descriptions and capability claims

Of Ignite's 214 prime award records (summed award amount $202.5 million: Army $83.65 million, Missile Defense Agency $67.15 million, Air Force $51.74 million), the awarding offices are Army Mission and Installation Contracting Command offices at Fort Huachuca, Fort Leavenworth, Fort Hood and Fort Sill, the Missile Defense Agency, the Air Force Life Cycle Management Center and the 55th Contracting Squadron. No awarding office is the [[Defense Intelligence Agency]], the Missile and Space Intelligence Center or the [[National Air and Space Intelligence Center]]. Descriptions include "Hybrid Threat, Complex Operational Environment (HTCOE) development and analysis support" (order 0011, $904,812, 2014), "Cognitive Behaviors for Units and Soldiers" (order 0042, $450,105, 2009), "IEW support" (order 0065, $849,933, 2011) and, in 2025, "Joint Electromagnetic Spectrum Ops Center" (FA460025F0048, $4,255,740, performed in San Antonio). The company's capabilities page lists "Cognitive EW," "All-Source Intelligence Analysis" and "Threat Modeling and Red Teaming."[^7][^23]

Ignite's September 24, 2026 announcement states that it was selected as an awardee on the $14 billion Contract Operations for Missile Evaluation and Testing (COMET) contract supporting the Missile and Space Intelligence Center "through our joint venture with Radiance Technologies," to provide "Scientific and Technical Intelligence (S&TI), foundational military intelligence, and analytical-enabling services to MSIC located at Redstone Arsenal," and lists the Defense Intelligence Agency among its customers.[^23]

### Radiance Ignite Technologies

Radiance Technologies and Ignite announced the joint venture Radiance Ignite Technologies, headquartered in Huntsville, on October 14, 2024. The announcement named Hinchman, chief executive officer of Ignite, as president and chairman of the venture and [[Bill Bailey]], chief executive officer of Radiance, as vice chairman, and said the venture would offer capabilities in technical intelligence, directed energy, space operations, electromagnetic spectrum operations, artificial intelligence and machine learning, and systems engineering.[^7] The Alabama record for Radiance Ignite Technologies, LLC (entity 001-144-448) gives a formation date of July 10, 2024, about three months before the announcement, and shows a Certificate of Formation of that date (4 pages), Hinchman as registered agent with a registered office on The Bridge Street in Huntsville, and no principal address or nature of business.[^24] A USAspending search for the recipient name returned no awards on October 3, 2026.[^21]
