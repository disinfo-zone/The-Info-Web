The Odyssey Foundation was the legal front entity for [[John David Norman]]'s [[Odyssey Network]] trafficking operation, operating from Dallas, Texas. The writer S. Dovey wrote that the foundation was connected to [[Educational Foundation for Youth]] in Illinois, which derived tax-exempt status as a subsidiary of offshore companies. The Traverse City Record-Eagle reported that a Chicago Secretary of State clerk described the Educational Foundation for Youth as "a non-profit arm of a profit-making corporation," an import-export business incorporated in 1962 and involuntarily dissolved in 1975 for failure to pay state franchise taxes, and that the Illinois Secretary of State's Springfield office had no record of the foundation.[^1] Through these front organizations, Norman operated a nationwide system of "sponsors" who provided housing for boys in exchange for sexual access, creating an interlocking web spanning multiple states. Dovey wrote that the Odyssey Foundation was linked to [[Francis Shelden]]'s [[North Fox Island]] operation through shared sponsors and the [[Hermes]] newsletter.[^2] The Record-Eagle reprint names Shelden as the owner of North Fox Island and as a figure whom Gerald Richards connected to the camp.[^1]

### Dallas Investigation, 1973

The Dallas police announced on August 15, 1973, that a raid the previous evening had uncovered the Odyssey Foundation, which operated with a San Diego post office box and the Epic International name. Membership cost $15 a year plus $3 for the booklets, the young men were called "fellows" and the clients "sponsors," and the police put the catalogue files at 50,000 to 100,000 names. A spokesman for [[Odyssey House]] of New York said it had no connection with the group.[^3] The writer [[Robin Lloyd]] wrote in 1976 that a suspected connection with the Houston murders, later proved incorrect, shut the foundation down before it got started.[^4]

A letter from the State Department to Senator [[John C. Culver]] dated June 27, 1977, says the department's Los Angeles security office reported the Dallas investigation in August 1973, describing a ring that used the name "Odyssey Foundation" to supply young males to people responding to its solicitations. On August 23, 1973, the Dallas police asked for passport information about a juvenile travelling in Europe under the foundation's auspices.[^5] A Homewood, Illinois, police supplementary report dated November 5, 1973, describes an adult recruited by mail into Epic International, which the report places in Dallas, and states that four checks totaling $662 were mailed to a post office box in Dallas.[^6]

### Connection to The Finders

When the two [[The Finders|Finders]] members arrested in Tallahassee, Florida in February 1987 were found transporting six malnourished children, an address book in their possession contained names that appeared in FBI Dallas field office file 145-0 relating to the Odyssey Foundation's child prostitution investigation. This address-book overlap, documented in FBI records cited in secondary sources and referenced in the FBI Vault's November 2019 Finders document release, represents the primary documented link between the two operations. No direct organizational relationship or personal contact between Norman and The Finders' leadership has been established in accessible primary sources.[^2]
