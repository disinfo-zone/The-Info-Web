FTX Trading Ltd., doing business as FTX.com, was a centralized digital-asset derivatives exchange controlled by [[Sam Bankman-Fried]], who also controlled the trading firm [[Alameda Research]], "a primary market maker on FTX." It held itself out as "the safest and easiest way to buy and sell crypto" and told customers that their assets were held in "custody" and segregated from its own.[^1] It raised "more than $1.8 billion from equity investors," about 1.1 billion dollars of it from some ninety investors in the United States.[^2]

### The Fraud

On December 13, 2022, the [[Commodity Futures Trading Commission]], the [[Securities and Exchange Commission]] and the [[U.S. Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York]] brought parallel charges. The CFTC alleged that from at least May 2019 through November 11, 2022, customer assets "were routinely accepted and held by Alameda and commingled with Alameda's funds," and that Alameda, Bankman-Fried and others had used them for "luxury real estate purchases, political contributions, and high-risk, illiquid digital asset industry investments." At Bankman-Fried's direction, FTX employees wrote into the exchange's code an "allow negative flag" and "effectively limitless line of credit" that let Alameda withdraw billions of dollars in customer assets; the features were not disclosed. The commission put customer losses at more than 8 billion dollars and thanked the Securities Commission of [[The Bahamas]] for its assistance.[^1] The SEC charged that the concealment began at least in May 2019 and included Alameda's exemption from the exchange's risk controls and its exposure to "overvalued, illiquid assets such as FTX-affiliated tokens."[^2]

Bankman-Fried gave about 40 million dollars in the 2022 election cycle, mostly to Democratic candidates and committees, and later described a parallel stream of undisclosed donations to Republicans; prosecutors charged that the money came from customer funds.[^3] Alameda's chief [[Caroline Ellison]], the FTX co-founder [[Gary Wang]] and the engineering head [[Nishad Singh]] pleaded guilty and testified against him. A jury convicted him on seven counts on November 2, 2023, and on March 28, 2024, Judge Lewis Kaplan sentenced him to twenty-five years and ordered forfeiture of 11.02 billion dollars.[^4]

### Effective Altruism

Bankman-Fried had been drawn into the "earning to give" wing of [[Effective Altruism|effective altruism]] by [[William MacAskill]].[^5] The [[FTX Future Fund]], launched in February 2022 under [[Nick Beckstead]] with [[Leopold Aschenbrenner]], Avital Balwit, MacAskill and Ketan Ramakrishnan, had by June 2022 made 262 grants totaling about 132 million dollars; its team resigned on November 11, 2022, and its grantees then faced clawback claims from the bankruptcy estate.[^6][^7][^8] [[Effective Ventures]], the umbrella of the [[Centre for Effective Altruism]], [[80,000 Hours]] and [[Giving What We Can]], returned 26,786,503 dollars, all it had received from FTX and the FTX Foundation, under a settlement agreed on December 13, 2023.[^9]
