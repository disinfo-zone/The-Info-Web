[[Brunswick Corporation]] is a company headquartered in [[Lake Forest, Illinois|Lake Forest]] whose Defense Division, renamed the Technical Group in 1991, gave its address as [[Costa Mesa, California|Costa Mesa]] on an October 1976 cover sheet for the [[Rifleman's Assault Weapon]] preserved in the [[Danny Casolaro]] papers. The company's annual reports for 1993 to 1995 describe the Technical Group, its divestiture, and a Costa Mesa facility that remained unsold.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

### The Defense Division in the Casolaro papers

The "Promotional Material" folder of Casolaro's research files, published as photographs on the [[Internet Archive]], contains a Brunswick item marked by hand "BD|AI|PM-03." Its first photograph is a cover sheet reading "RAW / Rifleman's Assault Weapon / for / Urban Warfare / October 1976," with the imprint "BRUNSWICK CORPORATION, DEFENSE DIVISION, 3333 HARBOR BLVD. • COSTA MESA • CA 92626 USA" and the legend "PROPRIETARY DATA." The second photograph is a line drawing of a tube-launched weapon on a bipod, which shows faintly through the cover sheet. No other pages of the package were photographed, and the cover's top right corner carries the page marking "01-498."[^4]

The restrictive legend on the cover reads: "INFORMATION AND DATA SET FORTH HEREIN ARE PROPRIETARY TO AND THE PROPERTY OF BRUNSWICK CORPORATION AND SHALL NOT BE DISCLOSED OUTSIDE THE GOVERNMENT OR BE DUPLICATED, USED OR DISCLOSED IN WHOLE OR IN PART FOR ANY PURPOSE OTHER THAN TO EVALUATE THE SAME PROVIDED, THAT IF A CONTRACT IS AWARDED TO THE BRUNSWICK CORPORATION AS A RESULT OF OR IN CONNECTION WITH THE SUBMISSION OF SUCH DATA, THE GOVERNMENT SHALL HAVE THE RIGHT TO DUPLICATE, USE OR DISCLOSE THESE DATA TO THE EXTENT PROVIDED IN THE CONTRACT." [^4]

The folder's 21 photographs run in this order: the [[Industrias Cardoen]] sales sheet ("BD|AI|PM-04"), the printed fuel-air munitions page numbered 207 ("BD|AI|PM-02"), the Brunswick cover and drawing ("BD|AI|PM-03"), the [[Counter Terrorism Consultants, Inc.]] resume packet ("BD|AI|PM-05"), and the Inter-Probe brochure ("BD|AI|PM-01"). The CTC cover page's typed title shows through the back of the Brunswick drawing sheet.[^4]

### Rifleman's Assault Weapon

The U.S. Army hired Brunswick in May 1977 to refine the design of the Rifleman's Assault Weapon (RAW), and the work was done by the company's California-based defense division. The Marine Corps examined the weapon and reportedly fielded it in limited numbers, and Brunswick later sold the rights to the project to the defense contractor L-3.[^5]

### Technical Group and the 1993 divestiture

The company's annual report for 1993 names [[Robert C. Sigrist]] as president of the Technical Group, "known as the Defense Division prior to 1991," and says he had held that post since 1988. The Technical Group, the report states, "manufactures and sells composite structures for aircraft, helicopters, spacecraft, propulsion systems, missiles," and a long list of other products that includes "tactical weapons; flight decoys and target training systems; relocatable and mobile shelter systems; and chemical protective detectors/alarms." The same report says those products "are sold to the [[United States Department of Defense|U.S. Department of Defense]]; major defense prime contractors."[^1]

In February 1993 the board approved plans to divest the Technical Group, which the 1993 report calls "the only remaining business in the Company's Technical segment." The company recorded a 26.0 million dollar estimated loss on the divestiture in 1992, and an additional 12.2 million dollar estimated loss in 1993 reflecting offers the company was reviewing.[^1]

### Sale to Technical Products Group, 1994 and 1995

On December 1, 1994, Brunswick executed a contract for the sale of substantially all the assets of the Technical Group, with a closing expected in the first half of 1995. The report for 1994 excludes "the assets associated with the Company's facility in Costa Mesa, California," and says negotiations for those assets were continuing. Item 1 of the same report says the company and the buyer had settled with the government "all issues related to the Federal Grand Jury investigation of the Company's Marion, Virginia facility," while the notes to the financial statements say the company "is finalizing a settlement acceptable to the buyer and the government" of the same issues.[^2]

The sale closed on April 28, 1995, to Technical Products Group, Inc., described in the 1995 report as "a newly formed company controlled by TPG Holdings in Atlanta, Georgia." The buyer took the Marion, Virginia, Lincoln, Nebraska, Camden, Arkansas, and DeLand, Florida operations. The Costa Mesa unit was excluded, and the 1995 report states that its assets were "fully reserved as of Decmeber [sic] 31, 1995" while the company "continues to seek a buyer."[^3]

### Costa Mesa after the sale

The 1995 report records a provision of 11.5 million dollars in the second quarter of 1995 for lower than anticipated selling prices on the businesses sold. The Costa Mesa plant remained with Brunswick after the sale, and the 1995 report describes the unit as one the company still seeks to sell.[^3]
