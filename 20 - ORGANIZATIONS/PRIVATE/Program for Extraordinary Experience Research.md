The Program for Extraordinary Experience Research (PEER) was a project of the [[Center for Psychology and Social Change]] of [[Cambridge, Massachusetts]], founded in 1993 by [[John E. Mack]] to investigate reports of encounters with non-human beings and to support the people reporting them.[^1] The Center's later name is the [[John E. Mack Institute]], whose history states that "the next year [after the 1992 renaming], with funding from Laurence Rockefeller, the Center formed" PEER, and that the program concluded in 2002.[^2]

### Funding

The Center's newsletter *CenterPiece* of September 1993 announced "PEER Research on UFO Abductions": Mack, a founder and board member, was "completing a book detailing more than a dozen case studies," and the project, "Funded by a grant from the Human Potential Foundation," would be under the Center's auspices, with [[Karen Wesolowski]] as executive director, [[Pam Kasey]] coordinating support, and [[Caroline McLeod]] and [[Barbara Corbisier]] heading research.[^3] The [[Human Potential Foundation]] was run by [[C. B. Scott Jones]]; a handwritten note of [[John Gibbons]] signed JHG and dated 3/29/94 says the Foundation "is funded in part, I believe, by L.R.," that is, by [[Laurance Rockefeller]].[^4] Rockefeller invited Mack to his [[JY Ranch]] roundtable of September 1993, and a photograph published by [[Paradigm Research Group]] with its own caption names Mack among those present.[^5] In a letter to Gibbons of April 26, 1994 Rockefeller wrote that "the publication of John Mack's book has brought renewed attention to the abduction phenomenon."[^6] The Center's newsletter of February 1994 announced Mack's book *[[Abduction: Human Encounters with Aliens|Abduction]]*, due for release in the spring of 1994, a PEER support program and a PEER academic conference planned for the spring of 1995.[^7]

*PEER Perspectives* no. 2 (Winter 1998/99) states that PEER was "founded in 1993 by [[Harvard University|Harvard]] professor of psychiatry John E. Mack," is "funded primarily through private donations and also through research grants" from the [[Fetzer Institute]], the [[Lifebridge Foundation]] and the [[National Institute for Discovery Science]], and reports that a panel of nine physical scientists chaired by [[Peter Sturrock]] and "funded by the Laurance S. Rockefeller Fund" had concluded in a report of June 1998 that UFOs are worthy of serious scientific study; it adds that in five and a half years PEER had received letters from individuals in some fifty countries.[^8] Separately, [[Ben MacIntyre]] of the London *[[The Times|Times]]* wrote in May 1995 that Mack's Center for Psychology and Social Change "is supported by an annual grant of $250,000 from Laurance S. Rockefeller," and the [[Valley News]] of May 20, 1995 reported that Rockefeller "reportedly donates a quarter million dollars a year" to the Center while a Rockefeller spokesman declined comment on Mack's projects; neither article names the program or a source for the figure.[^9][^10]

### Harvard Inquiry

In the summer of 1994 Dean [[Daniel C. Tosteson]] of [[Harvard Medical School]] convened a faculty committee chaired by [[Arnold Relman]] that reviewed Mack's work with abduction subjects. In August 1995 the university counsel [[Anne Taylor]] said that the review had ended with "no adverse action taken," and Relman said that tenure was never considered. The committee's preliminary report, drafted earlier in 1995, "chastised Dr. Mack for 'affirming the delusions' of his many patients," according to the [[Baltimore Sun]] of August 4, 1995.[^11] [[Alan Dershowitz]] criticized the inquiry in the [[The Harvard Crimson|Harvard Crimson]] on June 30, 1995, and Relman replied on September 13, 1995.[^12]
