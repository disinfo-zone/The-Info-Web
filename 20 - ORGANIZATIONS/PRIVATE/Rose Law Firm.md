The Rose Law Firm of [[Little Rock, Arkansas|Little Rock]] traces its origin to a partnership formed on November 1, 1820, by [[Robert Crittenden]], secretary of [[Arkansas]] Territory, and the land speculator [[Chester Ashley]]. It took its present name in 1980 from Uriah Rose, a president of the [[American Bar Association]]. The state's reference encyclopedia lists among its alumni "one first lady of the [[United States]]," whom it does not name.[^1]

### Partners and Clients

[[C. Joseph Giroir]], a securities lawyer who had worked at the [[Securities and Exchange Commission]], practiced at the firm from 1965 to 1988 and was its managing partner. [[Hillary Rodham Clinton]], [[Vince Foster]], and [[Webster Hubbell]] were partners. In 1978, when [[Financial General Bankshares]] sued [[Jackson Stephens]], [[Stephens Inc.]], and the bank data processor [[Systematics]] over the purchase of its stock for clients of the [[Bank of Credit and Commerce International]], the firm appeared for Systematics. "Mrs. Clinton even represented one of Stephens's companies in a case related to BCCI," two reporters wrote in 1992. Foster later handled the Systematics account.[^2][^3]

Giroir sat on the board of [[Worthen Bank]] with [[Mochtar Riady]] after the Riady and Stephens families took control of it in 1983. In April 1993 he incorporated the [[Arkansas International Development Corporation]] with capital from the [[Lippo Group]], and a House committee reported that the Riadys paid him between 350,000 and 600,000 dollars a year. [[Newsweek]] reported that "through his Riady contacts" he "got involved in deals with prominent Indonesian businessmen, including Bambang Trihatmodjo, the second son of President [[Suharto]]."[^2][^4]

### 1993 and After

Hubbell became associate attorney general in January 1993, and Foster also went to Washington. Foster was found dead in [[Fort Marcy Park]] on July 20, 1993. The firm's members then "found irregularities in Hubbell's billings for 1989-92." He resigned in March 1994 and pleaded guilty that December, "admitting that he defrauded his former partners and clients out of at least $394,000."[^5]
