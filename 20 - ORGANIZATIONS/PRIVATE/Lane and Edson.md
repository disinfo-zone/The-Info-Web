Lane and Edson, P.C., was a Washington law firm first recorded in 1971 as Frosh, Lane & Edson, with a practice in federally assisted housing and real estate finance. It registered under the [[Foreign Agents Registration Act]] as agent of the [[Peru|Republic of Peru]] from October 8, 1981 to November 8, 1988. In 1988 a group of its corporate lawyers left for [[Dewey, Ballantine, Bushby, Palmer & Wood]], and in 1989 twenty-four of the remaining attorneys were asked to join [[Kelley Drye & Warren]]. Its address at 2300 M Street NW is also the address printed in 1988 for [[Counter Terrorism Consultants, Inc.]], in which [[Eugene Propper]] of the firm said he was a full partner.[^1][^2]

### Founding and housing practice, 1971 to 1980

A witness identified as a partner of Frosh, Lane & Edson testified before a Senate Banking Committee subcommittee on housing legislation in 1971.[^3] The firm's address that year was 1025 Connecticut Avenue NW.[^4]

[[Charles L. Edson]] had been chief of the public housing section in the Office of General Counsel of the [[United States Department of Housing and Urban Development]] and deputy chief counsel of the Housing Assistance Administration before he became a partner.[^5] A 1972 guide to low and moderate income housing published by the [[Bureau of National Affairs]] lists Edson and Lane among the firm's partners, and the firm's practice in 1972 was in federally assisted housing.[^6]

In 1975 [[Bruce S. Lane]] appeared before the Senate Finance Committee as legal counsel to the Council of State Housing Agencies.[^7] A 1985 contributor note for Lane of Lane and Edson, P.C., gives a [[Harvard University|Harvard Law School]] LL.B. of 1955 and a practice in tax, securities and real estate matters.[^8] A 1978 trade article described the firm as specializing in real estate matters, including financing.[^9]

The firm's address moved from 1800 M Street NW, in a 1977 directory and in the Attorney General's reports for 1981 to 1983, to 2300 M Street NW in the report for 1984.[^10]

### Republic of Peru, 1981 to 1988

The firm's initial registration statement, filed in October 1981, described services to the Peruvian embassy aimed at legislation that would implement the United Nations Convention on the Means of Prohibiting and Preventing the Illicit Import, Export and Transfer of Ownership of Stolen Property, implement an executive agreement between Peru and the United States for the recovery and return of stolen archaeological, historical and cultural properties, and otherwise limit the importation into the United States of objects illegally exported from Peru.[^11] The same objectives appear in the annual reports for 1982 through 1986.[^12][^13][^14][^15][^16] The report for 1988 to 1991 describes the registrant's work as general legal services in connection with the recovery of pre-Columbian artifacts and antique art objects illegally exported from Peru, and services to assist the government of Peru in obtaining favorable legislation or administrative regulations on the importation of items from Peru.[^17]

The Attorney General's reports record the receipts for each twelve-month period ending October 8: $19,778.00 for 1982, $15,513.00 for 1983, $37,281.71 for 1984 and $23,758.89 for 1985, a total of $96,331.60. The entries for 1981, 1986 and the 1988 to 1991 report record "None Reported" for finances.[^11][^12][^13][^14][^15][^16][^17]

Frederic J. Truslow, an attorney with the firm, is quoted in *The International Trade Law Journal* in 1981 as saying that scores of archaeological sites had been stripped to feed between 40,000 and 80,000 pieces a year into the American art market.[^18] In 1982 the *Stolen Art Alert* described him as a Washington lawyer retained as counsel to Peru and reported his comments on Peru's priorities in stopping the flow of antiquities out of the country.[^19] He published "Peru's Recovery of Cultural Patrimony" in the *New York University Journal of International Law and Politics* in 1983.[^20] In 1986 *ARTnews* quoted him as calling preposterous the notion that Peru would file blanket suits against American museums and individuals claiming title to Peruvian antiquities.[^21]

The registration of the firm was terminated on November 8, 1988, the same date on which the short-form registrations of Propper and Truslow ended.[^22]

### Partners and lawyers

Lane and Edson letterheads printed in congressional hearing records of 1983 list the lawyers Frederic J. Truslow, Kenneth G. Hance Jr., Martin Klepper, Barry R. Schenof, Eugene M. Propper, Jack M. Feder, Daniel C. Smith, Bonnie S. Temple and Frank H. Henneburg.[^23] A letter on the firm's letterhead from Edson to Donna Denman, president of the [[National Leased Housing Association]], dated February 25, 1985, and printed in the record of Senate housing authorization hearings, lists Lane, Edson, Truslow, Hance, Klepper, Robert C. Zimmer, Schenof and Propper, among others.[^24]

A 1989 *Anuario Hispano* directory lists Truslow with Lane & Edson at 2300 M Street NW.[^25] A roster of counsel in the record of 1988 hearings lists him with Dewey, Ballantine, Bushby, Palmer & Wood.[^26] The firm's listing in the 1987 Catholic University alumni directory gives 2300 M Street NW.[^27]

### Mergers and publications

In 1988 the trade press reported that Jack Feder, who headed the firm's mergers and acquisitions group and had been a lawyer for the [[Wesray Capital Corporation]], was taking sixteen lawyers from the firm's ninety to the Washington office of Dewey, Ballantine.[^26] Bruce Lane, identified as a founding partner, told a 1988 publication that the firm was discussing merger possibilities; the publication reported that six more lawyers left a week after the sixteen to join another firm.[^28] On January 4, 1989 the *Wall Street Journal* reported that twenty-four Lane and Edson attorneys had been asked to join Kelley Drye & Warren, subject to the approval of that firm's partners, according to two members of Kelley Drye's executive committee.[^29] A 1991 *Washingtonian* article states that the firm became part of Kelley Drye & Warren.[^30] The Who's Who in American Law entry of a Washington lawyer gives Lane & Edson, P.C., 1979 to 1989, followed by Kelley Drye & Warren, 1989 to 1993.[^31]

The firm's lawyers also contributed to *The Art of M&A*, a 1989 guide written with [[Stanley F. Reed]], to which Propper contributed substantially to the due diligence chapter.[^32]
