Underground Cinema 16 was a 16-millimeter film theater at 2021 [[Sansom Street]] in [[Center City, Philadelphia|Center City]], [[Philadelphia]], opened in mid-October 1969 by [[R.C. Horsch]], then known as Raymond Hoersch, a 26-year-old filmmaker. The *[[The Philadelphia Inquirer|Philadelphia Inquirer]]* called it "Philadelphia's first commercial venture into noncommercial underground theater."[^1]

### 1969

The theater occupied premises where the Pocket Playhouse had once "packed them in." Horsch tore out the stage, installed plush blue seats taken from a small preview theater in the city, remodeled the lobby, hung white-framed photographs of his own, and built a projection booth. It opened with an underground double bill, *Lenny Bruce* and *Relativity*, and Horsch said he would draw its films from the [[Film-Makers' Cooperative|Filmmakers Cooperative]] in [[New York City]] and had started the theater in part to finance an 80,000-dollar feature of his own.[^1]

On Wednesday, October 29, 1969, [[Leo Goldstein]], deputy commissioner of the [[Philadelphia Department of Licenses and Inspections]], posted the theater as closed for lack of the required licenses, and police were instructed to keep it empty. Horsch described his failure to obtain the papers as a "simple confusion" over the city's requirements and said the officials at City Hall were "bureaucratic, but helpful." A preview of the [[Vietnam War]] documentary *[[In the Year of the Pig]]* had been scheduled for that night. Horsch said the city would inspect the building the next day; Goldstein said the process could take two weeks.[^1] Horsch later said that he ran the theater on Sansom Street as the Roxy.[^2]
