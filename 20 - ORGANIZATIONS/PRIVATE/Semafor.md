Semafor is a news company founded by [[Justin Smith]], former chief executive of [[Bloomberg Media]], and [[Ben Smith]], former editor-in-chief of [[BuzzFeed News]] and columnist at the *[[New York Times]]*; the two are not related. It launched on October 18, 2022 with a staff of 60 and 25 million dollars in private capital.[^1]

### Launch investors

The first round came from the [[Atlantic Media|Atlantic]] chairman emeritus [[David Bradley]], [[The Information]] founder [[Jessica Lessin]], [[John Thornton]], founder of the [[American Journalism Project]], [[Sam Bankman-Fried]], founder of [[FTX]], and [[3G Capital]] co-founder [[Jorge Paulo Lemann]], as well as Justin Smith. [[Gina Chua]] was executive editor, [[Benjy Sarlin]] Washington bureau chief, [[Liz Hoffman]] business editor, [[Reed Albergotti]] technology editor and [[Steve Clemons]] editor at large. Justin Smith said the name meant "the bearer of a signal" and was "the same word in 35 different languages."[^1]

### Bankman-Fried stake

After the collapse of FTX, Justin Smith wrote to staff on December 2, 2022 that the company had "set up a series of firewalls to ensure independence from our investors" and that Bankman-Fried "has no influence over our editorial coverage or operations"; Semafor said it would let "government investigations determine what happens with Bankman-Fried's investment."[^2] On May 24, 2023 Semafor announced a 19-million-dollar round, bringing its total raised to 34 million dollars after "the repurchase of the approximately $10 million in funds from Sam Bankman-Fried," which Semafor said would be "returned to FTX creditors as directed by officials." The new investors were [[Gallup]]; [[Henry Kravis]], co-founder of [[KKR]]; [[Jamal Daniel]], founder of Crest Media and owner of [[Al-Monitor]]; [[Jerry Yang]] of [[Yahoo]] and AME Cloud Ventures; Lemann again; and [[Stand Together]], the philanthropic network founded by [[Charles Koch]].[^3]

### Coverage of effective altruism and technology

On December 7, 2025 Semafor's media reporter [[Max Tani]] reported that the [[Tarbell Center for AI Journalism]], mostly funded by [[Open Philanthropy]] as Coefficient Giving, had placed fellows at *[[Time]]*, [[Bloomberg]], *[[The Verge]]*, the *[[Los Angeles Times]]* and *The Information*, and that Semafor itself had briefly hosted a fellow, ending the agreement "after several weeks without publishing any of their work." Coefficient's communications director, [[Naina Bajekal]], said: "We have no involvement in coverage decisions."[^4] Semafor has also reported on [[Jaan Tallinn]]'s AI investments and donations (April 2023), a lawsuit against [[Charles C. Johnson]] (October 2024) and a private club founded by [[Peter Thiel]] and [[Auren Hoffman]] (August 2025).[^5][^6][^7] On September 3, 2026 Ben Smith published an interview with Coefficient Giving's chief executive, [[Alexander Berger]].[^8]
