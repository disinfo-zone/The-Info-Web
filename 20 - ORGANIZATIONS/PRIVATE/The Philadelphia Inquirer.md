*The Philadelphia Inquirer* is a daily newspaper in [[Philadelphia]], founded on Monday, June 1, 1829, as the *Pennsylvania Inquirer*. Since 2016 it has been owned by a tax-exempt institute created at the request of [[H.F. Lenfest|H.F. "Gerry" Lenfest]] by the [[Philadelphia Foundation]]. Since June 2026 it has reported the [[Olney House Investigation]] into women connected to the pornographer [[R.C. Horsch]], and its files from 1969 to 1975 record the closing of his [[Underground Cinema 16]] and the cancellation of his film [[Black Mass (1975 film)|Black Mass]].[^1][^2][^3][^4]

### Annenberg

[[Moses Annenberg]] announced in 1936 that he had bought the paper for four million dollars, with the assumption of 6.8 million dollars in debt. His son [[Walter Annenberg]] inherited the business, and in 1957 bought the *[[Philadelphia Daily News]]* for three million dollars from the Philadelphia builder [[Matthew McCloskey|Matt McCloskey]]. In 1969 the two papers were sold for 55 million dollars to [[John S. Knight]]'s company, Knight Newspapers, which later merged with Ridder Publications to form [[Knight Ridder]].[^1]

### Tierney, the Hedge Funds, and the 2012 Group

Investors led by the public relations executive [[Brian P. Tierney]] bought the papers in 2006 for 515 million dollars. Hedge funds led by [[Alden Global Capital]] and Angelo, Gordon and Company bought the company out of bankruptcy in 2010 for 139 million dollars. In April 2012 a local group led by [[Lewis Katz]] and [[George E. Norcross III]], with Lenfest, [[Krishna P. Singh|Krishna P. "Kris" Singh]], [[William P. Hankowsky]], and Joseph Buckelew, agreed to pay 55 million dollars for the papers and their website, the same price Walter Annenberg had received in 1969.[^5] In May 2014 Lenfest and Katz won the company at auction for 88 million dollars, outbidding their co-owner Norcross and his associates.[^2]

### The Lenfest Institute

On January 12, 2016, Lenfest announced that he had donated the *Inquirer*, the *Daily News*, and philly.com to a newly created tax-exempt organization, the [[Lenfest Institute for Journalism|Institute for Journalism in New Media]], operating under the TPF Special Assets Fund of the Philadelphia Foundation, and would contribute 20 million dollars to its endowment. The operating company, Philadelphia Media Network, was converted to a public benefit corporation and remained a self-governing, for-profit company. "Of all the things I've done, this is the most important. Because of the journalism," Lenfest said. The foundation's president, [[Pedro Ramos]], said: "We're not getting into the newspaper business," and "In a democracy, great cities need and depend on quality journalism."[^2][^6]

### Coverage of the Horsch Cases

On October 30, 1969, the paper reported that the [[Philadelphia Department of Licenses and Inspections]] had ordered Cinema 16 closed.[^3] On February 28, 1975, it reported that *Black Mass* had been cancelled at the [[Arcadia Theatre (Philadelphia)|Arcadia]] "after reported pressure by the Philadelphia Archdiocese" and would be shown at the [[Bandbox Theater]].[^4] From June 2026 its reporters, among them Ellie Rushing, Barbara Laker, Max Marin, and Ryan W. Briggs, reported the search of the house on West Chew Avenue, the prosecutions of [[Eugene Horsch]], R.C. Horsch's access to the crematorium of the [[Garzone Funeral Home]], and his 2009 conviction for luring a child. On August 28, 2026, the paper reported, citing unnamed sources, that investigators believed a video showed Horsch killing a woman.[^7][^8]
