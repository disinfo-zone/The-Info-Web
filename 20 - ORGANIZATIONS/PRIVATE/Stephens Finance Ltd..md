Stephens Finance Ltd. was a [[Hong Kong]] finance company established in 1978 by [[Mochtar Riady]] of the [[Lippo Group]] and the Arkansas financiers Jackson and [[Witt Stephens]]. According to a 1998 report of the [[United States House Committee on Government Reform and Oversight|House Government Reform and Oversight Committee]], Mochtar Riady had first met the Stephens brothers in 1976, when he was "looking into buying Bert Lance's share of the [[National Bank of Georgia]]"; that deal fell through, and the friendship produced Stephens Finance two years later and "several other joint projects before Lippo and Stephens acquired a substantial stake in Worthen Bank of Arkansas in 1983."[^1]

[[John Huang]] was executive vice president at Stephens Finance under [[James Riady]] before moving to [[Little Rock, Arkansas|Little Rock]] as vice president for the Far East at the [[Worthen Bank]], and was later appointed Principal Deputy Assistant Secretary for International Economic Policy in the International Trade Administration of the [[United States Department of Commerce|Commerce Department]].[^1] The committee's account of these ventures rested chiefly on depositions of James Riady in *Stephens Group, Inc. v. [[United States]]* (1993), of the Little Rock lawyer [[C. Joseph Giroir]] (1997), and of Arthur Vernon Weaver (1997). The Riadys later traded their interest in Worthen for the Stephens family's interest in the [[Hong Kong Chinese Bank]], and traded their interests in other joint ventures.[^1]

The National Bank of Georgia that Riady had considered buying from [[Bert Lance]] was sold instead, at a closing in January 1978, to [[Ghaith R. Pharaon]]; the [[Federal Reserve Board|Federal Reserve]] concluded in 1991, according to the [[BCCI Kerry-Brown Senate Report|Kerry-Brown Senate Report]], that Pharaon had borrowed at least part of the purchase money, "with [[Bank of Credit and Commerce International|BCCI]] as his partner in the transaction from the beginning."[^2][^3]
