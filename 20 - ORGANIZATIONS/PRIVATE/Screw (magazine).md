*Screw* was a weekly sex tabloid published in [[New York City]], founded in 1968 by [[Al Goldstein]] and [[Jim Buckley]] with 175 dollars each. "Screw grew from a combination of many factors, chief of which was my own dissatisfaction with the sex literature of 1968 and my yearning for a publication that reflected my sexual appetites," Goldstein wrote in a 1971 anthology. It sold 140,000 copies a week at its height. In its first three years Goldstein was arrested 19 times on obscenity charges; in 1974 a federal judge threw out an obscenity case against him. The magazine was later outdone by more explicit publications including *[[Hustler (magazine)|Hustler]]*, and it folded in late 2003, when Goldstein filed for bankruptcy protection.[^1] Its review of [[R.C. Horsch]]'s 1973 film *[[The Erotic Memoirs of a Male Chauvinist Pig]]* called the sex in it disgusting.[^2]
