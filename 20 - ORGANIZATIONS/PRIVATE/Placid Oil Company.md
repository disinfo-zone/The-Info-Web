Placid Oil Company was formed in 1935 as successor to H.L. Hunt, Incorporated, with its shares placed in trusts for the six children of [[H.L. Hunt]] and [[Lyda Bunker Hunt]]. Its sister company, [[Penrod Drilling Company|Penrod Drilling]], operated the family's rigs. By the 1980s Placid was owned in equal parts by the trust estates of [[Nelson Bunker Hunt]], [[William Herbert Hunt]], and [[Lamar Hunt]].[^1]

### Black Lake

Placid discovered the [[Black Lake field]] in [[Natchitoches Parish, Louisiana]], in the mid 1960s. A landman hired in 1965 "upon the discovery of the Black Lake Field," [[Billy Bledsoe]], became "the Special Representative of the legendary oilman, H.L. Hunt" in 1970 and after 1974 "the International Negotiator for Nelson Bunker Hunt," with assignments in [[Libya]], in [[Iran]], where they involved the family of the [[Mohammad Reza Pahlavi|Shah]], and in [[United Kingdom|Britain]], [[Australia]], [[New Zealand]], and [[Canada]].[^2]

### The Banks

After the silver market broke on March 27, 1980, a consortium of banks lent Placid 1.1 billion dollars against the family's oil properties, guaranteed personally by the three brothers, in an arrangement that [[Paul Volcker]] of the [[Federal Reserve]] defended to Congress. By 1986, with oil prices falling, Placid and Penrod together owed 23 banks 1.5 billion dollars. The lead defendants in the litigation that followed were [[Bankers Trust]] and [[Manufacturers Hanover]].[^3][^4]

In the summer of 1986 the two companies and the three trust estates sued the banks in the federal court in [[Dallas, Texas|Dallas]], alleging fraud and a conspiracy to take over the offshore drilling business, and demanding damages reported at 15 billion dollars. The banks counterclaimed for the loans. Judge [[Barefoot Sanders]] declined to stop foreclosure, finding that the Hunts had "an adequate damage remedy." Interest was accruing at 230,461 dollars a day. The properties posted for foreclosure included 286 acres in [[Richardson, Texas]], the family's interest in [[Thanksgiving Tower]] in downtown Dallas, and a gas plant in [[Chambers County, Texas|Chambers County]]. A spokesman said: "We do not intend to let the banks dismantle Penrod and Placid. The companies are responsible for 20,000 workers."[^3][^5]

An agreed order of August 15 required the Hunt parties to obtain the Dallas court's approval before seeking bankruptcy protection. On August 29 Placid, a subsidiary, and the William Herbert Hunt Trust Estate filed Chapter 11 petitions in the bankruptcy court in [[New Orleans]] without it, arguing that their principal assets were in [[Louisiana]]. The Dallas court ordered the petitions transferred to [[Texas]] on September 4, and the [[United States Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit|Fifth Circuit]] affirmed on September 15. Placid's five largest trade creditors were [[Brown and Root]], [[American International Underwriters]], [[Marathon LeTourneau]], McDermott, and [[Hamanaka Chain Trading]] of [[Japan]]. A plan of reorganization was confirmed on September 30, 1988.[^3][^5]

### Sale

[[Occidental Petroleum]] acquired Placid on December 29, 1994, for about 250 million dollars in preferred and common stock. The assets were reserves of 12 million barrels of oil and 242 billion cubic feet of gas on the Gulf Coast and in the [[Netherlands]], and a 39 percent interest in a 170-mile gas pipeline system in the Dutch sector of the [[North Sea]] with thirteen licenses there.[^6]
