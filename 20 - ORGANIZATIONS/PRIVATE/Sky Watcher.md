Skywatcher (also written Sky Watcher) is a private group that described itself as an "independent initiative focused on aerial intelligence, advanced sensing, and unconventional aerospace research." It was announced in January 2025 in connection with the [[NewsNation]] special "[[Hunting UFOs: The Crash Retrieval Whistleblower]]," and NewsNation described it on April 7, 2025 as "a private group known as Skywatcher," "founded by UFO whistleblower [[Jake Barber]]."[^1][^2] Its website names [[Jacob Barber]], [[James Fowler]], [[Alex Klokus]], [[Dan Flechsenhaar]], [[Garry Nolan]], [[Joseph DiNoto]], [[Fred Baker]] and [[Don Paul Bales]] on its About page.[^3]

### Formation and founders

NewsNation described Barber as the founder of Skywatcher and reported on January 16, 2025 that his "Skywatcher Team" had deployed "on behalf of the [[U.S. government]]" to investigate the [[2024 New Jersey drone sightings|2024 East Coast drone sightings]], and that in August 2024 the team had conducted "their own operation, with a psionic asset, to see if they could summon a UAP themselves."[^1] A later NewsNation column by [[Ross Coulthart|Coulthart]] gave the founders as "Jake Barber, James Fowler and financier Alex Klokus."[^4] The writer Jimmy of the [[UAP Files]] newsletter stated in August 2026 that the Skywatcher [[YouTube]] account was created on January 18, 2025, six days before Barber's January 24, 2025 appearance on Coulthart's "[[Reality Check (NewsNation)|Reality Check]]," and that Klokus "personally registered the skywatcher.ai domain in his name in December 2024."[^5] [[Jason Colavito]]'s blog wrote on January 19, 2025 that [[Steven Greenstreet]] had reported that Barber "launched his own UFO organization, Skywatcher, in November, and its website is owned by Alex Klokus, the co-founder of the [[SALT (conference)|SALT]] conference, where UFO figures frequently speak to tech and financial elites."[^6]

The website's FAQ states that Skywatcher "is funded through private partnerships and professional consulting engagements," and, in answer to the question whether it is "a direct extension, surrogate for, or acting as a public messaging conduit for the US Government or any element of the US Intelligence Community," answers "No. Skywatcher is an independent and private entity." It also states that it does not "currently claim possession" of recovered materials, although it is "actively engaged in the public recovery and analysis of exotic aerospace materials."[^7] It lists 20 or more experts and "400+ years of combined" experience, and describes its approach as bridging "Special Operations, Advanced Sensing, & Non-Traditional Air Defense Techniques."[^7] Its research page lists a "Skywatcher Discovery Framework," described as "our proposed model for responsible UAP engagement, civilian access to data, and long-term transparency."[^8]

The same owner holds applications for ENNEA (serial 98873547, filed November 26, 2024) and PSIONIC (serial 99091501, filed March 19, 2025). Every filing in the public [[United States Patent and Trademark Office]] file for SKYWATCHER (the application of November 20, 2024, an extension request of February 11, 2026 and a petition to revive of September 14, 2026) is signed "/[[Robert Drolet]]/," attorney of record, of [[Fort Lauderdale]]; no owner-signed declaration, specimen or owner email appears in the public copy. The owner telephone on the filings is the number printed on the 2016 Form D of Futurism, LLC (signed by Alex Klokus as chief executive officer) and on the March 30, 2026 Form ADV of [[SALT Fund Management LLC|SALT Fund Management, LLC]].[^16]

ENNEA LLC is registered in Florida (document M25000008651, filed June 16, 2025) with "ALEX KLOKUS" as sole listed person, titled Member on the application, "CEO" on the annual report of April 17, 2026 and "Authorized Signer" on the amended report of July 15, 2026. The domain skywatcher.ai was registered December 6, 2024; its public registration record lists as registrant "Skywatcher Technologies," 12555 Biscayne Boulevard #1083, North Miami, Florida, and does not show Klokus's name. The amended Florida report of July 15, 2026 lists the same mailbox as ENNEA LLC's mailing address.[^17][^18]

### Field operations

The Skywatcher media page lists two videos. "Skywatcher Part I: The Journey Begins" is described as including interviews with Klokus, Barber, Nolan, [[David Grusch]], Col. [[Karl Nell]] and Baker, followed by an attempt "to replicate the process firsthand." "Skywatcher Part II: Mapping the Unknown" is described as introducing Fowler, "who leads the technology team," a taxonomy of nine UAP classes, and field work with "Dr. Garry Nolan, Alex Klokus, Matt Pines, and others," using "electromechanical signaling ('machine calling') and neuromeditative interaction ('psionic calling')."[^21] The nine classes on its research page include Tetra, [[Tic Tac]], jellyfish and manta ray; its Tic Tac class is described as seen "with the Manta Ray in proximity."[^8]

On April 7, 2025 NewsNation reported Coulthart's statement that Skywatcher used a "secret location known as 'The Range'" and an "electro-mechanical dog whistle," that "every time they use their dog whistle, it's worked 100% of the time in summoning the phenomenon," and that the group said it had hosted representatives of the [[All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office]] "at its private base."[^2] Coulthart wrote in August 2026 that the base was at [[Sierra Blanca, Texas]], southeast of [[El Paso, Texas]], and that Skywatcher operated there "until July" 2025.[^4]

### Cessation and later reporting

Coulthart wrote that Skywatcher's last post on [[X (social network)|X]] was on December 25, 2025, that nothing had been posted on the website since Fowler left "in mid-August last year to follow what he described as a new opportunity in aerial intelligence," and that "multiple insiders" had told NewsNation that by its last field operation in July 2025 Skywatcher "was working with and reporting to a number of government agencies, including [[NASA]], the [[Department of Defense]] and several intelligence agencies, including the [[Central Intelligence Agency]]."[^4] He stated that a [[Federal Aviation Administration]] notice to airmen posted on November 8, 2025 closed airspace southeast of El Paso through November 7, 2026 as "national defense airspace," that it was canceled on November 14, 2025, and that he had "learned the TFR was initiated by the Defense Department's [[Joint Special Operations Command]] liaison to the FAA and imposed 'to shoot down drones.'"[^4] He added that "Sources in the Trump administration" told him the government was using "the same so-called dog-whistle" and "psionic summoners" in operations at [[White Sands Missile Range]], and that "[[White House]] sources" said [[Donald Trump]] had personally authorized it; these statements are Coulthart's, attributed to unnamed sources.[^4] Coulthart's column also describes a February 10, 2026 closure of airspace over El Paso by the Federal Aviation Administration, rescinded about seven hours later; he wrote that [[The New York Times]] reported the closure followed use by immigration officials of an antidrone laser shared by the [[Pentagon]] against what turned out to be a party balloon, and that the administration had first cited cartel drones; he called the cartel-drone explanation one that "made no sense."[^4] On August 7, 2026 NewsNation published a Coulthart question-and-answer segment titled "Skywatcher didn't disappear, it went government."[^22]

The Defense Department's response to Barber's January 2025 claims, given by spokesperson [[Sue Gough]], was that it "has not discovered any verifiable information to substantiate claims that any programs regarding the possession or reverse-engineering of extraterrestrial materials have existed in the past or exist currently."[^23] On February 24, 2025 NewsNation reported that the department confirmed it was investigating Barber's claims, and Coulthart said that AARO director [[Jon Kosloski]] had indicated his office was talking with whistleblowers such as Barber.[^24]
