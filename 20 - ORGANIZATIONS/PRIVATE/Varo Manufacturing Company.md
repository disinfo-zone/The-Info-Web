Varo Manufacturing Company, later Varo, Inc., was an electronics maker in [[Garland, Texas]], that supplied equipment to the U.S. military.[^1][^2] The introduction to the Annotated Edition of [[Morris K. Jessup]]'s [[The Case for the UFO]] names it as the firm that offered to republish the annotated paperback "in a very limited edition as a prelude to consideration of further pursuit of this unconventional material."[^1]

### The Annotated Edition

The unsigned introduction to the Annotated Edition states that "Varo Mfg. Co., Garland, Texas, offered to re-publish the book together with all notations," after the annotated copy had reached the [[Office of Naval Research]] (ONR) and had drawn "direct interest" from Captain [[Sidney Sherby]] and Commander [[George W. Hoover]], Special Projects Officer at ONR. It names Miss [[Michael Ann Dunn]] as the person who "has undertaken the task of rewriting this book including all notes, interjections, underscoring, and etc.," retaining by form, position, color and footnotes as much of the relationships of the annotated copy as possible, and states that no attempt was made to read crossed-out material with ultraviolet light.[^1]

[[Gray Barker]] wrote in 1973 that Varo was at that time "deeply involved in aero-space design and manufacturing for the military," that one division was called "Military Assistance," which "may have coordinated the firm's activities with the government," that the division "agreed to run off a limited number of copies," and that Dunn was personal secretary to the company's president, "a Mr. Stanton." He wrote that Varo said its personnel records failed to find a record of her employment. Barker stated the book was "evidently taken to the Varo firm by Sherby, possibly in conjunction with Hoover," and he named two theories: that senior officers passed the matter down through lower officers so that Varo would print it without publicity, or that lower-ranking officers such as Sherby asked for the reprint as a personal favor. He gave the second as "the writer's best guess," and wrote that Jessup was called in by Varo, shown the book, and given several copies. He described a spiral bound volume of more than 200 pages, each page passed twice through an office duplicator, black ink for the original text and red for the annotations. The number of copies is not stated in his preface.[^2]

The two accounts differ on the date. The introduction places the envelope's postmark in 1955; Barker places the arrival of the book at ONR in April 1956, after [[Frederick R. Furth]] had, according to ONR's house publication, been relieved as Chief of Naval Research on December 31, 1955.[^1][^2][^4]

### Defense work

A trade-press item reproduced on the site of the Salisbury archive reports that Varo, Inc., Garland, Texas, developed the "Varotron," a device generating infrared, visible and ultraviolet radiation by passing an electron beam over a grating, described by its inventor [[Winfield W. Salisbury]] in a report at the "recent American Optical Society meeting in [[Rochester, New York|Rochester, N. Y.]]," and covered by U.S. Patent No. 2,634,372, issued to Salisbury in 1953. The item says the device "appears to have application to communications and possibly exotic weapons, similar to optical masers (lasers)." The same archive lists Salisbury's position at Varo from 1958 to 1965 as "Research and Development in Radiation Weapons for [[U.S. Air Force]]; Chief Scientist."[^3] Later Varo night vision products included the Noctron series, for which Varo, Inc. filed trademark applications on March 5, 1973 (Noctron IV, a night vision passive viewer using a multiple stage image intensifier) and May 5, 1980 (Noctron V, a second generation viewer using microchannel plate image tube technology), serial numbers 72450566 and 73260816. The full list of Varo Inc. marks also includes AMP-CHAMP (filed March 6, 1963, portable engine generators), SAR and IBR (March 17, 1964, silicon avalanche rectifiers and integrated bridge rectifiers), "V" (June 11, 1969, silicon diodes, semiconductors, integrated circuits, electric motors, power supplies and transformers), "V VARO" (March 5, 1973, research services in electricity, electronics, optics, electrooptics, electromechanics and microwave), "V VARO" and "VARO" (May 24, 1982, image converters, image intensifiers, ultraviolet detectors, reticles and electro-optical test apparatus), NITE-EYE (May 31, 1985, a passive low level light viewer and magnifier) and NIGHTHAWK (April 3, 1989, a battery-operated rifle sight system).[^5]
