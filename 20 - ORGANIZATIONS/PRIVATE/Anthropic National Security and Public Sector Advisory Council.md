[[Anthropic]] announced the council on August 27, 2025, as "a group of leading bipartisan national security and public policy practitioners who will help Anthropic support the [[U.S. government]] and closely allied democracies in building and maintaining enduring technological advantages in an era of strategic competition," with the task of identifying "high-impact applications" in areas "ranging from cybersecurity to intelligence analysis to scientific research." Its inaugural members were:[^1]

- [[Roy Blunt]], former senator from Missouri and member of the [[Senate Select Committee on Intelligence]], director of Tenet Healthcare and of the cyber company Nightwing, and former chairman of the advisory board of the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency.
- [[David S. Cohen]], former deputy director of the [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]] and under secretary of the Treasury for terrorism and financial intelligence.
- [[Richard Fontaine]], chief executive of the [[Center for a New American Security]], executive director of the [[Trilateral Commission]], former foreign-policy adviser to Senator John McCain, and a member of Anthropic's own [[Long-Term Benefit Trust]].
- [[Christopher Fonzone]], former head of the [[Department of Justice|Justice Department]]'s Office of Legal Counsel and general counsel of the [[Office of the Director of National Intelligence]] under Biden.
- [[Lisa E. Gordon-Hagerty]], administrator of the [[National Nuclear Security Administration]] in Trump's first term.
- [[Josh Hodges]], former national security adviser to Speaker [[Mike Johnson]] and senior director for Western Hemisphere affairs at the [[National Security Council]], then an executive at VEON Ltd.
- [[Jill M. Hruby]], administrator of the National Nuclear Security Administration under Biden and former director of [[Sandia National Laboratories]].
- [[Mike Kuiken]], former national security adviser to Senate Majority Leader [[Chuck Schumer]] and an adviser to the [[Special Competitive Studies Project]].
- [[Dave Luber]], former director of cybersecurity at the [[National Security Agency]] and executive director of [[United States Cyber Command|U.S. Cyber Command]].
- [[Patrick Shanahan]], former acting Secretary of Defense, chief executive of Spirit AeroSystems and director of [[Leidos]].
- [[Jon Tester]], former senator from Montana and chairman of the Appropriations Subcommittee on Defense, "where he oversaw $800 billion in defense and intelligence spending."

Six months later Anthropic refused the [[Pentagon]]'s demand to drop its exclusions for mass domestic surveillance and fully autonomous weapons, and was designated a supply-chain risk.[^2]
