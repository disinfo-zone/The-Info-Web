Spartacus International was the Amsterdam-based publisher of the *Spartacus International Gay Guide* (in its thirteenth edition in 1983, edited by [[John Stamford]]) and of the Spartacus Club, a membership scheme that supplied guide copies, discounts and "Holiday Help Portfolios." In 1986 the *[[The Sunday Times|Sunday Times]]* reported that the Club's Manila portfolio led its reporters to boys for hire, and the organisation had been named in testimony to the U.S. Senate in 1984; the British writer S. Dovey has since assigned it a larger role in a network of brothels, concierges and film production, an account found in Dovey's own book.[^1][^2][^3]

### Publisher and club

A Spartacus brochure for the thirteenth edition of the guide (1983) names the editor as "John D Stamford" and gives the publisher's address as P.O. Box 3496, 1001 AG Amsterdam, with text in English, German, French and Spanish. It offers an annual membership of the Spartacus Club that includes the guide free; a membership card to introduce members to private gay clubs; discounts; and "Spartacus Club Services": "Holiday Help Portfolios," described as "very comprehensive, frequently up-dated studies of specific popular gay locations, mostly outside of Europe" and "available only to club members," and "Holiday Introductions," by which "a local Spartacus friend ('Holiday Introduction') meet you and show you the local scene." The same brochure sells stationery made from photographs of "Alex, a Spartacus friend in The Philippines," designed by Stamford.[^4]

Spartacus letterhead of 1981 carries the motto "Towards a better world for gays" and the same Amsterdam box number and telephone (020) 950 950.[^5]

### Elm Guest House

On August 25, 1981 Laurie Page, Data Processing Manager, wrote on Stamford's behalf, as "Director," to the manager of the Elm Guest House, 27 Rocks Lane, Barnes, London SW13, that Spartacus had noticed the house in *Gay News*, was entering it in the 1982 guide with a free standard listing, and invited it to advertise through "our Business Manager, Mr Peter Glencross," and to join "our highly successful new venture, THE SPARTACUS CLUB," at no charge. On October 23, 1981 Glencross sent Harry Kasir a "formal invoice" for what the letter assumed would be camera-ready artwork. An advertisement for the house in the gay press read "10% Discount to Spartacus Club Members."[^5]

The Metropolitan Police raided the house on the night of June 19, 1982 after a surveillance operation that began when an individual told Richmond police station in late 1981 of concern about a 10-year-old boy; twenty people were arrested, and Harry and [[Carole Kasir]] were later convicted of running a disorderly house. The Independent Inquiry into Child Sexual Abuse recorded that the investigation identified evidence of abuse of only the one child and that no prominent individuals were observed or found there.[^6] Dovey wrote that a sign reading "Spartacus, Club - Welcome" hung at the house during the raid, that Terry Dwyer and John Rowe persuaded Carole Kasir in 1979 to convert the townhouse into a spa for Club members, and that Peter Glencross was instrumental in establishing it as a Spartacus venue.[^3]

### Scotland Yard, 1984

*Capital Gay* reported on February 17, 1984 that Scotland Yard had that week "refused to comment on reports that they are seeking the extradition from Amsterdam of Spartacus publisher John Stamford," and quoted an unofficial remark to the paper: "You'll never get a comment on extradition enquiries. If word gets back there's always a chance that the person will go missing."[^7]

### Senate testimony, 1984

At hearings of the Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations of the Senate Committee on Governmental Affairs on November 29 and 30, 1984, Defense for Children International (DCI) told the subcommittee that it had found in a 1982 study that child sex package tours were being offered from the Netherlands, West Germany, Japan and the United States to Sri Lanka, Thailand and the Philippines, with guides to negotiate prices, hotels, and ways to circumvent local laws and officials. "The leading magazine in this field," the statement said, "is called Spartacus," with a West German firm, ERO Tours, as "merely one example" of the organizations involved.[^2]

### The Sunday Times inquiry, 1986

The *Sunday Times* of August 3, 1986 ran Mazher Mahmood's "Scandal of Britons who buy young boys for £3 a night" in its Insight section. It reported that the Philippine government would ask [[Margaret Thatcher]] for help against British paedophiles visiting the islands, and that an Insight investigation "infiltrated the organisation, called the Spartacus Club, which is run by John Stamford, a 47-year-old homosexual paedophile and former Sunday school teacher from Lancashire," who operated from a "luxurious home-cum-office in the Baarn suburb of Amsterdam aided by seven colleagues and a computer." The paper said the Club claimed more than 25,000 British subscribers, that members could buy "Holiday Help Portfolios" with "detailed information about where to find young boys, how much to pay them, the risks involved, and how to avoid being arrested," and that the Manila portfolio listed resident couriers. A Filipino called Robert, one of six men acting as Spartacus guides in Manila, introduced the reporter to boys, among them Edwin Artogi, 8, and Rani Dinyege, 14. Stamford was quoted: "If you are discreet I can guarantee you will get as many boys as you want in the Philippines. Our chaps there will fix it up and all it'll cost you is a meal for the guides, and just the equivalent of a pound or so for the kid per night."[^1]

The same article reported that Stamford said he had been "forced to flee Britain" because the British "are too pig-headed and can't understand the quality of sexual relationships between men and boys," that he left for Holland "shortly after being convicted for sending obscene literature through the post in 1972," that Spartacus International was a British-registered company described as "general publishers of trade and business directories, periodicals, newspapers and journals" whose publishing was in fact "confined to a host of homosexual literature including books for pederasts, and Paedo Alert News, described as 'a magazine about boy love,'" and that Club membership cost £28. Superintendent Iain Donaldson of Scotland Yard's obscene publications squad told the paper: "We are aware of the Spartacus gay guide, but have not heard of Stamford's club catering for paedophiles. Since the gay guide itself does not contravene the law we have not been able to take any action." The Philippine social services minister, Mita Pardo de Tavera, asked Britain to furnish the names of suspected exploiters.[^1]

### Dovey's account of the network

S. Dovey wrote that Stamford was a defrocked Anglican priest who fled to Amsterdam in 1972 after being charged with running a child pornography service through the mail, and that from Amsterdam through the late 1970s, 1980s and early 1990s he operated a child sex tourism business "under the guise of an international homosexual travel guide," which also sponsored boy brothels under the franchise Spartacus Club; that Stamford inverted the usual trafficking route by delivering paedophiles to children in the Philippines and Thailand; and that the Club had 25,000 British members and relied on "host pedophiles" around the world who acted as concierges, such as [[Clarence Henry Osborne]] in Australia, where Brisbane boys appeared in Spartacus publications. Dovey quoted Marie-France Botte as saying the "Spartacus" guide and "Paedo Alert News" were visible in most Thai hotels that made children available.[^3]

Dovey wrote that between 1979 and 1985 Stamford published *PAN* ([[PAN Magazine]]), "a magazine about boy-love," through a publishing house he owned, that [[Francis Shelden]] (who fled Michigan in 1976) started the newsletter under the pen name [[Frank Torey]] in Amsterdam, and that Peter Glencross, a South African based in Holland, served as commercial manager and built a network of venues across Europe before disappearing without trace after 1989. Dovey named [[Russell Tricker]], operator of the coach service "[[Toff's Travel]]" across the Channel, as an associate of Glencross who used it to smuggle boys into Amsterdam, and [[Warwick Spinks]] as a friend of Tricker who ran a similar concierge service.[^3]

Dovey wrote that when authorities cracked down in Amsterdam Stamford fled to Germany and transferred control of Spartacus International to [[Bruno Gmunder Verlag]], fell under German investigation, fled to Belgium, and was charged there in 1993 with "incitement to the sexual exploitation of children"; and that he died of a heart attack in prison in Belgium in December 1995, aged 56, just before trial, facing a maximum sentence of one year. Dovey reproduced a 1992 German tabloid report that Scotland Yard suspected Stamford of trafficking in snuff films ("Stamford filmed 20 boys dying after sex orgies in England"; the videos sold "for around 1,500 marks per strip"), and wrote that a former associate testified at his trial about a video in which "a Filipino child is tortured and put to death." Dovey tied the network to [[Operation Framework]] (1992-93), a joint Dutch and Scotland Yard investigation of snuff pornography, and to the American organization [[NAMBLA]].[^3]

### Ownership after 1986

Bruno Gmünder Verlag of Berlin published the guide from 1987 and credited it to "Bruno Gmünder & John D. Stamford" through the 1993/94 edition, according to library catalogue records of those editions; the vault page [[Bruno Gmunder Verlag]] records the later history of the publishing house.[^8]
