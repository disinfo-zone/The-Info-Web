Worthen Banking Corporation was a bank holding company in [[Little Rock, Arkansas|Little Rock]]. The family of [[Jackson Stephens]] owned 38 percent of it in January 1993. It was sold to [[Boatmen's Bancshares]] in 1994.[^1]

### The Riadys

[[Mochtar Riady]], founder of the [[Lippo Group]] of [[Indonesia]], met Jackson and [[Witt Stephens]] in 1976, and the families formed [[Stephens Finance Ltd.]] in [[Hong Kong]] in 1978. Both invested in Worthen in 1983. Riady's son [[James Riady]], who had been an intern at [[Stephens Inc.]], moved to Little Rock to run the bank's international division. [[John Huang]], born in Fujian and raised in [[Taiwan]], joined in February 1985 as vice president for the [[Far East]], a title he held together with a vice presidency of the [[Hong Kong Chinese Bank]], reporting to Riady in both. A former partner of the [[Rose Law Firm]], [[C. Joseph Giroir]], organized the [[Arkansas International Development Corporation]] on Lippo money. A Riady memorandum obtained by House investigators dated the family's relationship with [[Bill Clinton]] to a five-minute car ride with the governor.[^2][^3]

In 1985 the bank lost 52 million dollars of state funds that it had placed in repurchase agreements with the [[New Jersey]] securities dealer [[Bevill, Bresler and Schulman]], which failed amid fraud charges. Stephens covered the loss, by one account with a check written "in the middle of the night," and led a rights offering to restore the bank's capital. About 1987 the Riadys exchanged their Worthen shares for the Stephens interest in the Hong Kong Chinese Bank. James Riady and Huang went to [[California]] to run [[LippoBank]]. At the end of 1991 Lippo was the expected purchaser of the Hong Kong subsidiary of the [[Bank of Credit and Commerce International]], until investigators there found 145 million dollars in unrecorded claims. The Saudi investor [[Abdullah Taha Bakhsh]], the third largest stockholder of [[Harken Energy]], was reported in 1991 to own 10 percent of Worthen.[^2][^4][^5]

### The 1992 Campaign

Under its chairman, [[Curt Bradbury]], who as a Stephens officer had attended the 1977 meeting in Little Rock between Stephens, [[Bert Lance]], and a representative of the Bank of Credit and Commerce, Worthen gave the Clinton presidential campaign a line of credit of 3.5 million dollars, secured on federal matching funds. About three million was drawn. By another account the line was opened in early January 1992, before the crises of the primary season, and the first million was drawn on March 4. The campaign's finance officer, [[David Watkins]], negotiated it. The campaign kept as much as 55 million dollars in federal funds on deposit at the bank. Bradbury and [[Mack McLarty]], chairman of [[Arkla]], the gas company the Stephens family had controlled, organized Little Rock dinners that raised two million. James Riady and his wife gave 450,000 dollars to the 1992 effort, and on August 12, 1992, the Riady company [[Hip Hing Holdings]] gave 50,000 dollars to the [[DNC Victory Fund|Democratic Victory Fund]] and was reimbursed from [[Jakarta]]. Stephens Inc. had given 100,000 dollars to a dinner for President [[George H.W. Bush]] in May 1991.[^1][^2][^4][^6]
