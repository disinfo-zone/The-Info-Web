ZR/RIFLE was a covert program established within the [[Central Intelligence Agency|CIA]]'s clandestine service in early 1961 to develop what agency documents called an "executive action" capability - the ability to arrange the assassination of foreign leaders. The program was headed by [[William Harvey]] and was applied primarily to operations targeting [[Fidel Castro]] of Cuba. It was exposed by the [[Church Committee]] in 1975, which found that its existence had been deliberately concealed from the Warren Commission investigating President Kennedy's assassination.[^1]

### Origins and Authorization

The concept of an executive action capability had circulated within CIA leadership since at least 1959. [[Richard Bissell]], the CIA's Deputy Director for Plans, directed Harvey to develop such a capability. The program was given the cryptonym ZR/RIFLE (with the cover name EXEC ACTION used in some communications). The Senate interim report records that in early 1961 Bissell assigned Harvey to establish the capability, and Harvey testified that he was "almost certain" of meetings on January 25 and 26, 1961.[^2][^3]

Harvey's handwritten notes of the January 25 and 26, 1961 meetings, preserved at the CIA, contain the phrases "last resort beyond last resort and a confession of weakness," "the magic button," and "never mention word assassination." Harvey confirmed that the notes suggested a discussion of assassination.[^3] The Inspector General's 1967 report, as quoted by the Senate committee, found "no indication in the file that the Executive Action capability of ZR/RIFLE-QJ/WIN was ever used," and said that "after Harvey took over the Castro operation, he ran it as one aspect of ZR/RIFLE."[^3] Harvey testified that during the whole project "no agent was recruited for the purpose of assassination." Bissell described the program as "internal and purely preparatory."[^3]

The single agent placed under Harvey's supervision, cryptonym QJ/WIN, was described by [[Richard Helms]]: "If you needed somebody to carry out murder, I guess you had a man who might be prepared to carry it out."[^3]

Harvey testified that Bissell had told him the White House had urged the capability more than once. Bissell's account changed across his appearances. In July 1975 he said there had been no White House urging for the project, though tacit approval by [[McGeorge Bundy]] was probably given after it was established.[^3] The Inspector General's 1967 report describes Harvey as "Chief of FI/D" when he was briefed in February 1961, by authority of Bissell, on the gambling-syndicate operation, and quotes Harvey's notes as recording Bissell saying, "The White House has twice urged me to create such a capability."[^4] A dispatch of June 8, 1961 from "Chief, KUTUBE/D" to the Chief of Station, Rome, captioned ZRRIFLE, concerns the tracing of potential agents for "this KUTUBE/D purpose."[^5] A memorandum for the record of May 23, 1967 by the Inspector General records that the Director's instructions of May 22, 1967 were to retain the report in eyes-only safekeeping and to destroy all notes and source materials, which were destroyed on May 23, 1967.[^4]

Harvey drafted a planning memorandum defining the requirements: the program would develop foreign national assets capable of performing assassinations, maintain plausible deniability through layers of cutout relationships, and be available for use against specific targets as authorized by higher authority. Harvey's memos were deliberately oblique, using phrases like "termination with extreme prejudice" rather than explicit language.[^1]

### CIA-Mafia Connection

The primary operational implementation of ZR/RIFLE targeted Castro. Harvey, building on relationships initiated by CIA officer [[Robert Maheu]] in 1960, worked with [[Johnny Roselli]] of the Chicago Outfit to develop assassination plots using organized crime assets. The theory was that organized crime figures with pre-revolutionary Cuban casino interests had both motivation and access - through exile Cuban contacts and Cuban underworld connections - that the CIA lacked.

[[Sam Giancana]] (Chicago Outfit boss) and [[Santos Trafficante, Jr.|Santo Trafficante Jr.]] (Tampa boss) were brought in alongside Roselli. The CIA's Technical Services Division developed poison pills intended for Castro's food. Multiple attempts were made through 1960-1963; none succeeded. Castro's G2 security service was highly effective at detecting infiltration, and the exile Cubans who might have been used as operational assets were under intense surveillance.[^1]

Early in 1962 Harvey was appointed chief of [[Task Force W]], the CIA component of [[Operation Mongoose]], and [[Evan Thomas]] wrote that his ZR/RIFLE assets were incorporated into Mongoose planning.[^6][^7] Harvey continued meeting with Roselli, providing operational guidance and CIA resources while maintaining the cutout structure intended to preserve deniability.[^1]

### Church Committee Disclosure

The Church Committee - the Senate Select Committee investigating intelligence activities - disclosed ZR/RIFLE in its 1975 report *Alleged Assassination Plots Involving Foreign Leaders*. The committee found that:

- The CIA had maintained assassination plots against Castro from 1960 through at least 1963
- ZR/RIFLE had also contemplated or discussed targets including [[Patrice Lumumba]] of the [[Congo]], [[Rafael Trujillo]] of the [[Dominican Republic]], and the South Vietnamese [[Ngo Dinh Diem|Diem brothers]]
- CIA Director Helms had not informed the [[Warren Commission]] of ZR/RIFLE's existence or its operational relationship with the Cuban exile and organized crime networks
- This concealment was intentional; Helms and other CIA officials believed the Warren Commission investigation should not know about the assassination programs

The committee found that while it could not definitively prove the CIA-organized crime anti-Castro network had been involved in Kennedy's assassination, the concealment of ZR/RIFLE from the Warren Commission had fundamentally compromised that investigation's ability to examine the possibility.[^1]

### Subsequent Operations

After FitzGerald replaced Harvey as chief of Task Force W in January 1963, ZR/RIFLE operations continued under [[Desmond FitzGerald]], the CIA's Chief of the Western Hemisphere Division. FitzGerald met personally with [[Rolando Cubela]] (AM/LASH), a senior Cuban official who claimed willingness to assassinate Castro, in September 1963. This meeting took place on the same day as the [[JFK Assassination|Kennedy assassination]]; Cubela was being offered a poison pen when Kennedy was shot in Dallas. The AM/LASH operation ultimately produced no result against Castro and was penetrated by Cuban intelligence.[^8]
