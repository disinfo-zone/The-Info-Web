Operation Framework was a [[Scotland Yard]] investigation, set up after negotiations with Dutch counterparts, into suspected snuff pornography produced by British nationals operating in Amsterdam. The investigation targeted a group of foreign British pedophiles who were trafficking boys into Amsterdam boy brothels that masqueraded as gay nightclubs in the city's Spuistraat district.[^1]

### Background and Initiation

[[Nick Davies]] wrote that by October 1990 detectives on the [[Obscene Publications Squad]] at Scotland Yard were receiving informant reports that [[Alan Williams]] was trafficking boys into Amsterdam and that [[Warwick Spinks]] was selling a video of a boy being abused and tortured. He wrote that in 1993 an informant called Frank, who had gone to Amsterdam in July 1990, told the same officers that Spinks had shown him in the Canary Islands a video of a boy of about twelve being attacked with needles, castrated and cut open, and that after long negotiations within Scotland Yard and with their counterparts in Holland the detectives set up Operation Framework.[^2] The writer S. Dovey wrote that Scotland Yard investigated the possibility that [[Sidney Cooke]] and his gang had filmed the deaths of boys, an inquiry Dovey linked to the murder of Jason Swift.[^3]

The focus on Amsterdam was deliberate, as the city had become a hub for international child trafficking due to its relatively permissive attitude toward the adult sex industry and its strategic location within Europe. British traffickers like Warwick Spinks had established operations there, exploiting the legal environment and infrastructure of the red light district to facilitate their criminal activities.[^1]

### Key Targets and Suspects

The primary targets of Operation Framework were Warwick Spinks and his close associates. Nick Davies wrote that Spinks managed the [[Gay Palace]] on Spuistraat in the late 1980s, where he sold alcohol and young men to tourists.[^4] Its lawful business was a bar with adult male prostitutes, and an informant told Scotland Yard he had watched videos of boys aged eleven to fourteen being abused by masked men there.[^2] Another key target was Alan Williams, a Welsh pedophile known as "The Welsh Witch" who managed Boys Club 21, another venue in the same district investigated for its role in child trafficking.[^2]

Williams, along with two other Welsh pedophiles named [[John Gay]] and [[Lee Tucker]], operated a film production company called TAG Films. Davies wrote that TAG Films sold its films through distributors in the United States and Germany, and that the Bristol detectives, following Tucker and Gay, led German police to raid a video distributor in Dusseldorf.[^2]

### Snuff Film Allegations and Evidence

Multiple informants provided detailed accounts of films showing boys being tortured and murdered. One informant named "Frank" described a film Spinks had shown him, shot inside a barn, featuring a boy being tortured with needles, then castrated and cut open with a knife.[^2][^4] A Bristol informant, not named, told the [[Avon and Somerset Police]] detectives that in 1989 he had found in a flat belonging to one of the key figures on the Amsterdam scene a video showing a murder. Davies noted that Frank described a video shot in a barn and the Bristol informant one shot in a flat.[^2] A third informant named "Edward" claimed to have seen at least five such snuff films, alleging they were produced by Spinks and his associates at various locations including a house in Hoofddorp, a second house where he said Spinks lived, a lake where he said bodies were dumped, and one video shot on a boat.[^4]

### International Cooperation and Raids

The investigation involved unprecedented cooperation between British and Dutch authorities. Davies wrote that Dutch police confirmed Spinks had been living at one of the houses Edward identified,[^4] and that Scotland Yard's detectives could not justify sending officers to Amsterdam, where they lacked police powers.[^2] [[Der Spiegel]] reported in August 1998 that investigators had found nothing punishable at [[Gero-Video]] Produktions GmbH, a Düsseldorf distributor, that several proceedings against it had been discontinued, and that [[Robbie Van Der Plancken]] appears in at least five Gero-Video films, which the firm's owner said it bought in 1993 from a Dutch production company.[^5]

### Undercover Operations and Informant Testimony

Undercover officers played a crucial role in Operation Framework. Davies wrote in 1997 that the tapes of Spinks had been secretly recorded by an undercover police officer in Holland,[^4] and in 2000 that the officer had been recruited "to pose as a child abuser and to befriend Warwick Spinks in England."[^2] Spinks said: "They're not six grand," and continued: "I know, well I knew some people who were involved in making snuff movies and how they did it was, they only sold them in limited editions, made ten copies or something, ten very rich customers in America, who paid $5,000 each or something like that - which is a lot of money to watch some kids being snuffed."[^4]

Spinks went on to describe a specific case: "I know the person who made the film. I felt sorry for this boy, it was a German boy." Asked "How old?", Spinks replied: "About 13, 15. He thought he was going to make 200 guilders and ended up being dead."[^4]

### Connection to Other Investigations

Davies wrote that in September 1994 Rotterdam police set up a team to investigate [[Lothar Glandorf]] and to find [[Manuel Schadwald]], and that the team produced a report entitled De Handel In Kinderen, known as the [[HIK Investigation|HIK report]].[^6]

### Investigation Outcomes and Limitations

Davies wrote that the Dutch police investigated Edward's claims and said they could find no evidence to support them. He wrote that when detectives from Avon and Somerset Police took a second informant's account to Amsterdam in the autumn of 1998, the Dutch said they would not investigate, and that the Bristol force had neither the funds nor the legal power to run an inquiry in Holland.[^2]

Davies reported that Operation Framework "ended in frustration": Spinks "divulged no more about the video and failed to produce a copy of it," and without more evidence the detectives could not justify the expense of keeping the undercover officer or of sending officers to Amsterdam, where they lacked police powers. The detectives arrested Spinks in [[Hastings]] and charged him with abducting two homeless boys from the streets of [[London]] and selling one of them into a Spuistraat brothel. In February 1995 he was jailed at [[Lewes Crown Court]] for seven years, reduced on appeal to five.[^2]
