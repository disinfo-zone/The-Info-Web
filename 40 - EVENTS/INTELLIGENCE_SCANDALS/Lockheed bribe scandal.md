The Lockheed bribe scandal refers to a series of bribery incidents involving the [[Lockheed Aircraft Company]]. In his 1992 book, [[Ari Ben-Menashe]] described a specific instance in [[Israel]] that he said came to light in 1976 through an intercepted telegram.[^1]

In his 1992 book, Ben-Menashe wrote that, while serving as a duty officer in [[Unit 8200]], he intercepted a telegram from the [[Iran|Iranian]] Embassy in [[Ramat Gan]] to [[Tehran]]. He wrote that the telegram detailed a meeting between a Lockheed representative in Israel and [[Shimon Peres]], then Defense Minister, revealing a bribe of $3.5 million deposited into one of Peres's brother's business accounts in [[Europe]].[^1]

He wrote that the information was covered up by Israeli military intelligence superiors, including [[Yoel Ben-Porat|Col. Yoel Ben-Porat]] and [[Sasson Yishaek|Lt. Col. Sasson Yishaek]], that the original telegram, its translation, and related logs were ordered to be erased, and that the young woman working with him was transferred to another unit. He wrote that no public disclosure or inquiry was ever made regarding Israel's involvement in the scandal.[^1]
