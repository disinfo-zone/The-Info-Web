On November 25, 2022, [[Gabriel Rodrigues Castiglioni]], 16, attacked the [[Escola Estadual Primo Bitti]] and then the [[Centro Educacional Praia de Coqueiral]] in the Coqueiral district of [[Aracruz, Espírito Santo]], [[Brazil]], with a .40 pistol issued to his father by the [[Polícia Militar do Espírito Santo]] and a .38 revolver his father had bought privately. Three teachers and a 12-year-old student were killed and twelve people were wounded.[^1][^2] He wore camouflage clothing, a hat, a vest and a mask, with a swastika armband that Governor [[Renato Casagrande]] confirmed as a Nazi symbol; [[Agência Brasil]] described the mask as a skeleton face similar to the one worn in photographs by one of the perpetrators of the 2019 [[Suzano school shooting]].[^3] The government's sentencing memorandum against [[Terrorgram Collective]] leader [[Dallas Humber]] lists "G.C." among seven people who plotted or committed attacks "inspired and guided by her leadership."[^4]

### The Attacks

According to the reconstruction given by police delegate [[André Jaretta]] on November 28, 2022, the teenager took the family car with its license plate covered while his parents were shopping, entered Primo Bitti, a school he had attended until June 2022, by a rear gate whose padlock he cut, and fired into the teachers' room, reloading once. He then drove to the Centro Educacional Praia de Coqueiral, a private school, and fired a revolver at students in the entrance hall and through a window into an upper-floor classroom, before returning home and putting away the clothing and weapons.[^5] The attack began at about 9:30 in the morning.[^3] He was apprehended at about 2:10 in the afternoon at a family beach house, the [[Polícia Civil do Espírito Santo]] said.[^6]

The dead were the teachers [[Maria da Penha Pereira de Melo Banhos]] (48) and [[Cybelle Passos Bezerra Lara]] (45), killed at Primo Bitti; the teacher [[Flávia Amboss Merçon Leonardo]], who died in hospital on November 26 (outlets give her age as 36 and 38); and [[Selena Sagrillo Zuccolotto]], 12, a sixth-grader at the private school.[^1][^7] Police charged him with acts analogous to qualified homicide and attempted homicide; he confessed after being shown surveillance footage.[^5][^8]

### Terrorgram and the United States Record

The government's sentencing memorandum in [[United States v. Humber]] (November 26, 2025) lists "G.C. shot eleven people, killing four, at two schools in Aracruz, Brazil" among attacks "inspired and guided by her leadership," and says it would file under seal the "Saint cards" Humber created to commemorate the shootings by J.K. ([[Juraj Krajčík]], Bratislava) and G.C., "whom she considers 'symbolically [her] kids.'"[^4] [[Raw Story]] reported in March 2025 that a government filing opposing bail for [[Matthew Robert Allison]] described a secret chat of October 23, 2022 in which Humber told Allison she "had direct messages with a Terrorgram user who was planning to commit a racially motivated school shooting," with details matching the Aracruz attack a month later.[^9]

[[ProPublica]] and [[FRONTLINE]] reported that after the attack Humber posted to a Terrorgram channel a ZIP file of information on it, including 17 photographs and four videos, said the massacre was motivated by "Hatred of non-Whites," and wrote that the assailant would get a "SLAP ON THE WRIST" sentence because of his age; they called it the most deadly known crime stemming from Terrorgram.[^10] At Humber's sentencing on December 17, 2025, prosecutor [[Christopher J. Perras]] told the court she "was responsible for inciting" the Bratislava shooting, a stabbing outside a mosque in Turkey "and a school shooting in Aracruz, Brazil that left teachers and children dead." She was sentenced to 360 months.[^11][^12]

The researcher [[Michele Prado]] told Raw Story that a Terrorgram pseudonym, "Wolf Boy Winter," contributed pages to the Terrorgram zine [[The Hard Reset]] and interacted with Brazilian adolescents.[^9]

### Brazilian Proceedings

On December 7, 2022, Judge [[Felipe Leitão]] of the Vara da Infância e Juventude de Aracruz imposed the socio-educational measure of internment for up to three years, the maximum under the [[Estatuto da Criança e do Adolescente]], with psychiatric follow-up.[^13] He left internment in late November 2025; the [[Tribunal de Justiça do Espírito Santo]] told [[A Gazeta]] on December 2, 2025 that he continues under assisted liberty, and the [[Ministério Público do Espírito Santo]] said Brazilian law does not allow him to be held responsible again as an adult.[^1]

The Polícia Militar opened a disciplinary procedure against his father, a lieutenant, on November 28, 2022, over how the son obtained the two weapons.[^5] In June 2025 victims and relatives circulated a petition stating that the father had "until today not been punished and has not even been located to be officially cited in the process."[^14] The state government compensated only those hurt at the state school Primo Bitti, under a 2023 plan modeled on São Paulo's after Suzano.[^15]

### Brazilian Investigative Response

The [[Polícia Federal]] found two antisemitic [[Telegram]] groups, "Movimento Anti-Semita Brasileiro" and "Frente Anti-Semita," on the phone seized from the teenager; PF delegate [[Leopoldo Soares Lacerda]] wrote that the minor "was inspired by the material shared on the application to commit the act of violent extremism."[^16][^17] Justice Minister [[Flávio Dino]] had ordered a PF inquiry into neo-Nazi organizations on April 5, 2023. The PF asked the federal court in Linhares on April 14, 2023 to break the groups' data; Telegram supplied administrator data for one channel and said the other had been deleted. On April 26, 2023, Federal Judge [[Wellington Lopes da Silva]] found the compliance partial, raised the daily fine to R$1 million and ordered Telegram suspended nationwide, directing [[Google]] and [[Apple]] to remove it from their app stores.[^17][^18] The suspension was lifted on April 28, 2023.[^19]

On June 2, 2023 the PF executed search warrants in São Paulo and Petrolina, Pernambuco, in Operação Bedel, against two members of the group, investigated for corrupting a minor by inducing him to commit qualified homicide and an offense under the Anti-Terrorism Law; the PF wrote that "the use of the swastika on the minor's clothing at the time of the attack demonstrates the influence of Nazi ideology received from the application group."[^20]

### Michele Prado and ABIN Reporting

In 2023, Prado, founder of the Brazilian NGO [[Stop Hate Brasil]], submitted a report to the [[Agência Brasileira de Inteligência]] (ABIN) and the [[Ministry of Justice and Public Security]] identifying Telegram channels linked to Terrorgram members operating in Brazil, including a channel associated with the "Wolf Boy Winter" pseudonym. Prado had been monitoring Terrorgram's Brazilian operations since at least 2022 and by 2023 documented more than 40 school-related violent incidents with some connection to online extremist networks over a three-year span. At least two of the Terrorgram-affiliated groups she identified had been created by adolescents.[^21]

### Federal Police Statistics

The Polícia Federal recorded a 279% increase in investigations of Nazi propaganda on social media platforms from 2022 to 2023, making 2023 the highest year on record for such inquiries. A broader government accounting reported 2,830 ongoing investigations into school violence by June 2023, with over 380 arrests by that date. A [[Global Network on Extremism and Technology]] analysis published in November 2024 documented 177 Federal Police investigations into Nazi propaganda on social media platforms between January 2021 and February 2024, with 82 still ongoing at that date.[^21][^22]

### Ciro Daniel Amorim Ferreira

On January 13, 2025, the [[United States Department of State]] designated [[Ciro Daniel Amorim Ferreira]] of Belo Horizonte, [[Minas Gerais]], as a [[Specially Designated Global Terrorist]] in connection with his role as a Terrorgram channel administrator.[^23]
